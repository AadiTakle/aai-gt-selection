# Program-Mechanism and Effect-Size Benchmarks

## Bottom Line

The evidence makes GT’s program effect plausible but not established.

Prudent planning benchmarks:

- **0.05–0.10 SD:** modest but potentially meaningful on an independent broad outcome
- **0.10–0.20 SD:** realistic/strong scaled field effect
- **0.20–0.30 SD:** ambitious
- **Above 0.30 SD:** exceptional for a full-year independent evaluation

Do not use Alpha’s 2.6× growth claim as an assumed effect size.

## Human Tutoring

Nickow, Oreopoulos, and Quan’s final peer-reviewed meta-analysis:

- Overall: 0.288 SD, SE 0.029
- Literacy: 0.290 SD
- Math: 0.268 SD

Effects were larger in earlier grades, in-school delivery, frequent sessions, and professional tutors, but moderator comparisons are largely across studies rather than randomized mechanism tests.

At scale on independent standardized outcomes, later synthesis suggests approximately 0.16–0.22 SD is a more defensible expectation.

GT transfer limits:

- literature primarily serves mainstream/struggling students;
- human tutors differ from adaptive software/guides;
- tutoring is often supplemental, while GT replaces ordinary instruction;
- upper-tail outcomes are underrepresented.

## Intelligent Tutoring and Adaptive Software

Headline meta-analytic effects depend strongly on comparison and measurement:

- Steenbergen-Hu & Cooper K–12 math: adjusted 0.01; unadjusted 0.09
- Ma et al. broad synthesis: approximately 0.41 overall
- Kulik & Fletcher:
  - local tests around 0.62–0.73
  - standardized tests around 0.09–0.13

Independent field evidence:

- ASSISTments Maine cluster RCT: approximately 0.18 SD at the end of grade 7
- Separate North Carolina replication: approximately 0.10 SD on the grade-8 state test one year after implementation; COVID canceled the intended grade-7 state outcome
- DreamBox: approximately 0.11 SD on MAP after four months
- Cognitive Tutor Algebra I: null year one; approximately 0.20 year-two high-school effect
- Reasoning Mind randomized trial: approximately −0.06, nonsignificant

Interpretation:

- tutoring, feedback, practice, curriculum alignment, and implementation may drive much of the effect;
- adaptivity alone is not established as the active ingredient;
- immediate/aligned outcomes are often larger than delayed/standardized outcomes;
- null results are normal.

## Evidence for High-Ability Students

Direct evidence is sparse.

A 2026 working paper randomized 620 Olympiad-nominated students to a structured advanced combinatorics course versus independent study:

- ITT: +0.165 SD
- Full engagement: only 15%
- IV estimate for full engagement: +0.66 SD, dependent on stronger exclusion,
  monotonicity, engagement-definition, and functional-form assumptions

The defensible causal result is the +0.165-SD offer/package ITT. The IV estimate
does not isolate instruction, feedback, or engagement as a mechanism. The study
supports the complete offered course package over the independent-study
alternative in that setting; it does not validate GT or unattended self-paced
software.

Bui–Craig–Imberman found an approximately +0.28 SD science attendance-IV/LATE
estimate but no statistically detectable math/reading/language/social-studies
effects in the gifted magnet lottery; the intervals do not establish
equivalence, and attrition bounds weakened the science result.

A conclusion-changing 2026 revision by Card, Chyn, and Giuliano reports:

- approximately 3,500 disadvantaged students near a gifted-eligibility threshold;
- null standardized-test effects;
- +28 percentage points in on-time college entry for marginally eligible boys;
- 95% CI approximately [7, 48] percentage points.

This remains local to boys/compliers near one IQ threshold, uses a donut RD because of score bunching, loses observations through district/data requirements, and is not direct evidence for the Snapshot. It shows that long-run attainment can move even when near-term test scores do not.

## Mastery and Compacting

Mastery meta-analyses report around 0.5 SD on average, but:

- standardized-test effects are much smaller;
- studies are old and heterogeneous;
- completion can worsen;
- high-ability-specific evidence is thin.

Curriculum compacting randomized district training:

- removed roughly 40%–50% of already-mastered content;
- produced no broad achievement loss on above-level tests;
- some math/science gains appeared;
- replacement quality varied and often did not provide real acceleration.

The strongest supported claim:

> Demonstrated repetition can be removed without obvious harm when advanced replacement work is substantive.

## Recent Generative-AI and Scaled Tutoring Evidence

- Bastani et al. (2025): unrestricted GPT-4 improved assisted practice by 48% but reduced subsequent unassisted performance by 17%; hint-based teacher-informed tutoring avoided the harm.
- Nigeria teacher-guided Copilot package: roughly +0.23 SD English and +0.31 SD composite after six weeks, but only 759 of 1,328 assigned students entered final analysis and outcomes were locally aligned.
- Hybrid human tutoring/computer-assisted learning: +0.19 SD ITT and +0.23 SD TOT in a first cohort, with cost reductions; working-paper evidence.
- District-operated tutoring at scale: approximately 0.04–0.09 SD reading and null average math/course-grade effects.

These findings strengthen two boundaries:

1. guardrails and pedagogy are treatment components; and
2. assisted performance must be separated from independent learning.

## Acceleration and Grouping

Acceleration evidence is favorable but predominantly matched/observational.

- Same-age comparison effects can be large.
- Comparison with older classmates is near zero.
- Causal gifted-program studies remain mixed.

Randomized grouping evidence outside gifted education suggests grouping works when it allows instruction to target readiness, not because homogeneous peers alone cause growth.

Design implication:

- compact mastery;
- advance immediately;
- group dynamically by domain/readiness;
- retain structured instruction, feedback, milestones, and community;
- monitor the fastest student and wellbeing.

## Benefit Moderators

### Confirmatory candidate

Domain-specific pretreatment achievement/knowledge relative to the instruction offered.

Direction must follow the treatment mechanism:

- high assistance may help lower-knowledge learners more;
- low-assistance or advanced instruction may benefit higher-knowledge learners more.

### Optional second candidate

A prespecified curriculum-mismatch/assistance-fit measure distinct from the baseline outcome.

### Equity reporting, not directional benefit claims

- SES/opportunity
- ELL
- disability/2e

### Exploratory only

- broad ability/CogAT profiles
- spatial ability outside spatial instruction
- conscientiousness
- motivation/interest
- age/grade
- high-order interactions

Use direct interaction tests. “Significant in one subgroup but not another” is not interaction evidence.

## Fastest-Student and Wellbeing Guardrails

- Time waiting after mastery
- Proportion receiving substantive advanced replacement work
- Delayed retention after advancement
- Advanced-content completion
- Feedback latency
- Disengagement despite high mastery
- Academic self-concept
- Belonging
- Anxiety, workload, sleep, and school interest
- Assessment/curriculum ceiling among highest-baseline students

No universal thresholds exist; set them from GT’s actual service.

## Provisional MME Framework

Proposed for later evaluator ratification:

- 12-month primary MME: +0.10 SD
- 24-month persistence threshold: +0.05 SD
- High-performer noninferiority margin: −0.05 SD
- Sensitivity values: 0.05, 0.15, 0.20 SD

Interpret:

- **Effect-existence test:** two-sided 95% CI against zero on the overall ITT.
- **Meaningful-superiority test:** one-sided lower 95% bound above +0.10 SD.
- **Equivalence test:** separate TOST procedure with 90% CI inside [−0.10,+0.10].
- **Highest-baseline noninferiority:** define the absolute high-baseline offer
  effect \(\tau_{high}\) and require its one-sided lower 95% bound above −0.05
  SD. A treatment-by-baseline interaction alone tests relative heterogeneity
  and is not this safety estimand.
- **Persistence:** separate 24-month estimand and +0.05-SD threshold.

Interpretation:

- Positive effect but lower bound below +0.10: benefit may exist, but meaningful superiority is not established.
- Upper bound below +0.10: the preregistered meaningful-superiority claim is ruled out.
- Upper 95% bound below zero: evidence of harm.
- Equivalence, superiority, persistence, and noninferiority are different hypotheses and cannot share one ambiguous CI rule.

Preregister a hierarchy/multiplicity plan. A plausible order is overall primary ITT, meaningful-superiority test, then key-secondary progression/persistence; the high-performer guardrail remains a separately protected safety family.

These are proposals, not GT facts.

## Power Reality

For equal individual randomization and no covariate gain, detecting 0.10 SD with 80% power requires roughly 3,140 applicants. If baseline covariates explain 50% of outcome variance, roughly 1,570.

Clustering, attrition, noncompliance, multiplicity, and minimum-effect testing increase required information.

Do not lower the meaningful-effect threshold to match available sample size.
