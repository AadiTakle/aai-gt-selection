### D-010 — Ratify future two-stage evaluation design, MAP outcome, and MVP remedy/consent boundaries

- **Date:** 2026-07-18
- **Status:** Approved
- **Decision:** Ratify the following governance-sync clarifications, restoring the BrainLift's intended two-stage evaluation interpretation and aligning MVP scope with the RES-008/RES-009 findings: (1) any future evaluation of Track B is a two-stage design, not a single Track-A-vs-Track-B comparison — stage one randomizes offer-versus-not-offered among equally eligible Track B candidates to estimate the Track B initial-offer/package effect, and stage two compares treated Track B against treated Track A as a service-fit noninferiority comparison; neither stage, nor their combination, supports a claim that Track A and Track B have equal route-specific causal effects without a Track A counterfactual. (2) MAP is ratified as the selected common future outcome measure for this design, verified only with lightweight upper-tail and administration-consistency checks, never scored or used for eligibility. (3) The proposed non-offered-candidate follow-up is a fall/winter/spring MAP testing schedule, a proposed next-cycle application-fee waiver of about $100 for submitting all three MAP results, and a 2–3 minute structured questionnaire per MAP submission covering schooling, tutoring, adaptive software, enrichment, and MAP prep since the prior test. (4) The questionnaire is hidden from admissions and cannot affect eligibility or re-entry; the questionnaire and other post-assignment resource data are descriptive mechanism/substitution information for a future evaluator, not primary intent-to-treat controls. (5) Sufficient Track B applicant volume/oversubscription (needed for randomized offer-versus-not-offered) is treated as an external go-to-market assumption, not a product blocker. (6) Pooling cohorts across cycles and handling rule/rubric/rater version drift are future analyst responsibilities using existing operational metadata; the MVP adds no new applicant-facing fields to support this. (7) MVP decision remedies are limited to explanation and factual/procedural correction; a substantive rubric-application appeal and automated re-entry are deferred beyond MVP scope, though manual staff-initiated re-entry may remain. (8) All prototype Track A/Track B rules remain fictional, versioned, and tagged `synthetic_only=true` and `validated=false`. (9) Any future seat allocation and the future evaluation design described above remain strictly downstream of this MVP and outside its scope.
- **Requirements served:** R2, R3, R4, R6, R7, R8, R9, R10, H3, H5, H6, H8, H9
- **Evidence:** RES-008 (two-stage estimand correction), RES-009 (cohort pooling and version-drift analysis), RES-004 (explanation/correction/appeal boundary), RES-005 (privacy/consent separation for research fields)
- **Alternatives considered:** Ratifying a single Track-A-vs-Track-B comparison as an equal-effect causal claim; collecting the resource questionnaire as a decision-used or admissions-visible field; adding new MVP persistence fields for cohort pooling or version tracking now; implementing substantive rubric appeal and automated re-entry within the four-week MVP; leaving prototype rules unmarked or implicitly authoritative.
- **Rationale:** RES-008 confirms the BrainLift intended two distinct comparisons — a randomized intent-to-treat estimate and a non-randomized service-fit comparison — while the prior PRD wording collapsed them into one equal-effect causal claim; separating them preserves a valid future identification strategy without overstating what either stage can show. Treating MAP, the fee waiver, and the questionnaire as proposed and hidden from admissions keeps the eligibility product from being contaminated by future-outcome incentives while still recording enough design intent that a future evaluator is not foreclosed. Deferring substantive appeal, automated re-entry, and cohort-pooling fields keeps the four-week MVP scoped to explainable eligibility and correction rather than adjudication or longitudinal-analysis capacity the team cannot build or validate in the available time.

Applicant-volume risk is explicitly not a reason to block MVP delivery. All prototype rules remain fictional/versioned/`synthetic_only=true`/`validated=false` until superseded by a live-validation decision.
- **Owner:** Team lead
- **Relationship to prior decisions:** Builds on D-008 (Track A/Track B prototype ratification) and D-009 (Supabase/PostgreSQL backend) by fixing the future evaluation and remedy boundaries those decisions left open; does not supersede either.

### D-012 — Move the prototype backend platform from Supabase to AWS (retain PostgreSQL)

- **Date:** 2026-07-19
- **Status:** Approved
- **Decision:** Replace the Supabase development platform with AWS managed services for the synthetic prototype, **keeping PostgreSQL as the database engine**. The row-level-security firewall, `SECURITY DEFINER` RPC write surface, immutable successor versioning, hash-chained audit, reason-code explanations, and deterministic canonical replay are retained unchanged; only the platform bindings move. The canonical Supabase→AWS mapping is:

  | Concern | Supabase (D-009) | AWS (D-012) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Database engine | Supabase-hosted PostgreSQL | **Amazon Aurora Serverless v2 (PostgreSQL-compatible)** — same SQL, RLS, RPCs, replay |
  | Auth / identity | Supabase Auth (GoTrue) JWT | **Amazon Cognito** user pool; JWT carries a `custom:user_role` claim; identity is `sub` |
  | RLS principal binding | `auth.uid()`, `auth.jwt()->>'user_role'` | App verifies the Cognito JWT and sets request-scoped session GUCs (`SET LOCAL app.user_id`, `app.user_role`); RLS predicates read `current_setting(...)` |
  | DB client | `@supabase/ssr` request-scoped client | **`pg` (node-postgres)** via **Amazon RDS Proxy**, one transaction per request, connecting as a non-`BYPASSRLS` role |
  | Write/read API surface | PostgREST-exposed `api` RPCs | Same `api` schema `SECURITY DEFINER` RPCs, invoked over `pg` from Next.js Server Actions/route handlers |
  | Object storage | Supabase Storage | **Amazon S3** (private buckets, pre-signed URLs; no public objects) |
  | Compute / hosting | Supabase-adjacent / local | **Next.js container on Amazon ECS Fargate**, fronted by **CloudFront + ALB** |
  | Secrets / elevated access | service-role key | **AWS Secrets Manager** + IAM database authentication; no RLS-bypassing credential in the app runtime (the "by construction" control is retained) |
  | Infrastructure as code | Supabase CLI/config | **Terraform** (cloud-portable) |
  | Synthetic/dev safety | local-only, loopback fail-closed | **dedicated dev AWS account, synthetic-only, resource-tag/account-id fail-closed guard** (replaces the loopback guard) |
  | Website integration | own origin | **own CloudFront origin** (subdomain, or a path behavior on the host distribution) |

- **Requirements served:** R7 (auditability/replay preserved), R8 (feasibility/portability), R9 (student protection — synthetic-only, least-privilege, no RLS-bypass credential), R10 (claim boundaries unchanged)
- **Evidence:** D-009 (prior platform), D-011 (architecture it revises); `docs/architecture/ARCHITECTURE_PLAN.md`; `RLS_AND_AUTH_BLUEPRINT.md`; `PROVISIONAL_IMPLEMENTATION_CONTRACT.md`; `CANONICALIZATION_AND_REPLAY_BLUEPRINT.md`
- **Alternatives considered:** (a) DynamoDB + Lambda + Cognito (fully serverless/NoSQL) — rejected because it discards PostgreSQL RLS, forcing a full rewrite of the data model, auth firewall, and canonical replay, and reduces portability. (b) RDS Postgres + API Gateway/Lambda for the RPC layer — deferred; retained as a possible future refactor, but keeping the definer-RPC surface in-database minimizes churn now. (c) Staying on Supabase — overridden by the team's platform decision.
- **Rationale:** Keeping PostgreSQL preserves the security and replay invariants that most of the corpus depends on, so the migration is a binding swap rather than a redesign. Standard PostgreSQL + a container + Terraform keeps the system portable across clouds or self-hosting and cleanly attachable to a pre-existing website via a CloudFront origin, satisfying the "easy to migrate / move across platforms / hook up to an existing site" objective.
- **Consequences:** Documentation and stack descriptions across the canonical, active-contract, and code layers are updated to the AWS mapping. The **functional code migration** (replacing `@supabase/ssr`/`@supabase/supabase-js` with `pg` + Cognito verification and session GUCs, replacing the Supabase CLI dev loop, and adding Terraform) is a **tracked follow-up**, not completed by this decision; the existing Supabase prototype code remains runnable until that migration lands (see `ASSUMPTIONS_AND_EVIDENCE.md` A-AWS-1..3 and the backend backlog). Development uses a dev AWS account with synthetic data only; no live child data, production account, or public endpoint is authorized.
- **Owner:** Team lead
- **Supersedes:** D-009

### D-015 — Reframe the selection target to Timeback-fit (giftedness core) and adopt a scalable, tunable, GT-validated screener

- **Date:** 2026-07-24
- **Status:** Approved
- **Decision:** Reframe the selection target from "identify giftedness" to "identify applicants who are gifted *and* able to thrive and accelerate on the Timeback platform," with giftedness a necessary but not sufficient component. Adopt a scalable, tunable, GT-validated screener as a first-class deliverable (new R11) and reframe R5 to a capability-and-fit standard. Record GT's current operational anchors — the multi-path admission rubric (high CogAT; or two MAP screeners above the 95th percentile; or a blended CogAT+MAP aggregate above the 90th percentile; or a 99th-percentile composite waiver) and the 85th-percentile fall MAP reading gate with an ESL/exceptional-cognitive exception — as documented validation references, not a locked cut. The screener must run algorithmically at applicant volumes in the thousands (GT Anywhere at scale), with a human shadow-day retained for behavioral fit. North-star context: the "MIT-ready by 8th grade, ~100,000 students" leadership goal (expansion-dependent; current cohorts ~40-46 physical + ~300 virtual).

### D-016 — Build the R11 screener as a born-synthetic adaptive test sub-application

- **Date:** 2026-07-24
- **Status:** Approved
- **Decision:** Build the R11 screening instrument as a bounded, born-synthetic adaptive test sub-application inside the existing pnpm monorepo (Mode A per D-011), on the runnable local Supabase/PostgreSQL stack (D-012 keeps this code runnable until the AWS binding migration). Scope: (1) an item bank of interactive question types drawn from the `research/exam-question-types` catalog, each item carrying versioned IRT parameters; (2) an in-stack TypeScript IRT/CAT engine (`packages/cat-engine`) that estimates per-domain ability (theta), selects each next item by maximizing item information, applies an engagement gate and consistency weighting, and stops on a target standard error or item cap with exposure control; (3) private `app.*` tables + `api.*` `SECURITY DEFINER` RPCs + forced RLS for participants, sessions, item responses, raw telemetry events, and derived per-domain metrics, exposed only through RPCs and generated `api` types; (4) a Next.js test-taking surface that embeds the existing self-contained demos through a `postMessage` telemetry contract and drives the adaptive loop. ### D-029 — One source of truth for screener selection and scoring: the database stores, the TypeScript packages decide

> **Renumbered 2026-07-28 (was D-019).** This decision originated on the exam
> backend branch as "D-019"; that number was independently used on `dev` for the
> AWS-Lambda scoring decision (kept above as D-019). On merging the unified
> platform into `dev`, this entry was renumbered D-029 to preserve both. Doc and
> application-code references were updated; some **already-applied exam migration
> comments** (`20260725050000`, `20260725160000`, `20260725170000`) and the

> left untouched to avoid file-vs-live-DB drift on migrations that are already
> applied. Likewise E-072/E-073 on this branch were renumbered to E-093/E-094.

- **Date:** 2026-07-25
- **Status:** Proposed (awaiting team-lead ratification)
- **Decision:** Split ownership of the adaptive screener along the line `docs/architecture/EXAM_ADAPTIVE_BUILD_PLAN.md` already draws. The **database keeps** answer keys, per-item answer verification against the server-only key (`app.exam_score_response`), the full trace (responses, metrics, telemetry), and outcome **storage**. The database **stops being an authority** on item selection, on the battery stop rule, and on the final score. `api.exam_submit_response` no longer auto-computes an outcome; outcomes now arrive through a new `api.exam_record_outcome`, which stores the `packages/exam-scoring` output **verbatim** together with the audit inputs needed to recompute it (scoring policy id, scorer version, and a database-derived sha256 of the canonical scorer input drawn from the stored trace). `app.exam_compute_outcome` and the difficulty-stepping logic are **demoted, not deleted**: both remain callable, only the automatic call site and the convergence branch are removed. The database retains a **hard safety cap** on item count (`hardItemCap`, seeded at 120) as a runaway guard that sits above the engine's own cap of 60, so it can never truncate a legitimate battery; it is explicitly not the stop rule.
- **Requirements served:** R7 (auditability and reproducible replay), R8 (feasibility — one tested implementation rather than two), R10 (claim boundaries), R11 (scalable, tunable in-house screener)
- **Why the contract already allocates it this way:** BUILD_PLAN **§3** assigns `startState/nextType/nextItem/update/isDone` to `packages/exam-engine` and specifies a stop rule keyed on core-metric coverage, even area spread, and estimate stability. BUILD_PLAN **§5** assigns the score to `packages/exam-scoring` and specifies *accuracy sets the bracket, metrics position within the bracket*. BUILD_PLAN **§6** scopes `supabase/` to storage tables plus RPCs and never grants the database scoring authority. This decision does not create a new allocation; it removes database behaviour that contradicted the existing one.
- **Defects this resolves (both independently verified against the running local stack):** (1) *Duplicate, disagreeing scoring* — `app.exam_compute_outcome` reported per-area proficiency as simply the final adaptive difficulty, while `packages/exam-scoring/src/scorer.ts` implements the §5 bracket-then-position contract, so one trace could yield two competing scores and reproducibility was not well defined. (2) *A broken stop rule in the wrong place* — the rule `attempts >= minItemsPerArea and (attempts >= maxItemsPerArea or abs(delta) <= stableDelta)` with seeded `stepSize = 0.8` and `stableDelta = 0.5` could effectively never take its convergence branch (`abs(delta)` is 0.8 on a correct answer and 0.8 on a wrong one, 0.4 only on a near miss), so every area ran to `maxItemsPerArea`, silently converting the REQUIRED variable-length battery of BUILD_PLAN §0 into a fixed-length one.
- **Alternatives considered:** (a) *Make the database the scoring authority* — port the §5 bracket-then-position pipeline into plpgsql, retire the TypeScript scorer's authority, and fix `stableDelta` so it exceeds `stepSize`. Rejected: it rewrites roughly 3,000 tested TypeScript lines into a language with weaker test ergonomics to obtain a number already computed correctly, it puts the admin-tunable policy behind migrations, and it still leaves selection split. It is a coherent choice and remains available (see reversal). (b) *Retune `stableDelta` and leave both scorers live* — rejected: it fixes the battery length but leaves the two-competing-scores defect untouched. (c) *Move answer verification into the application tier too* — rejected: the answer-key firewall is only meaningful because key comparison happens inside a schema no client role can reach.
- **Evidence/assumptions:** `supabase/EXAM_BACKEND_STATUS.md` §2/§4/§6 and unreconciled item 4 (the prior agent's executed verification: 274/274 pgTAP, the three-way answer-key firewall proof, and the recommendation this decision acts on); BUILD_PLAN §0/§3/§5/§6; `packages/exam-engine/src/done.ts` and `src/config.ts` (the §3 stop rule as implemented); `packages/exam-scoring/src/scorer.ts` (the §5 score as implemented). Fresh evidence from this change: `supabase/tests/122_exam_outcome_ownership.test.sql` (30 assertions) plus the amended `120` (46), 306/306 pgTAP across 15 files, `db lint` clean. **Open assumption:** no engine-versus-database convergence simulation has been run against the real banks, so the claim that an engine-driven battery finishes well under the 120-item guard is a design expectation, not a measured result. Everything remains born-synthetic (`synthetic_only = true`, `validated = false`); nothing here establishes predictive validity or authorizes an admissions decision.
- **Rationale:** Reproducibility does not require in-database computation — it requires a frozen trace plus a frozen policy, both of which this schema stores. `scoreExam` is pure in `(items, policy)`, so it reproduces wherever it runs. Key custody, by contrast, genuinely requires the database. Splitting on that line keeps the security property that must be in Postgres in Postgres, and puts the psychometric logic where it is specified, tested, and cheap to tune.
- **Consequences:** The database now stores a score it did not compute and cannot independently re-derive. That risk is mitigated in the same change rather than deferred: `app.exam_scorer_input_json` exposes the canonical scorer input from the stored trace, `app.exam_scorer_input_hash` hashes it server-side at record time, and both are stored on the outcome, so any recorded score can be recomputed from the trace and checked. `api.exam_submit_response` keeps its signature but its payload changes (`done` and `outcome` are replaced by `itemsAdministered`, `hardItemCap`, `hardCapReached`, and `stopRuleOwner`), and it no longer closes a session except at the safety cap — the application must now drive the stop through the engine and end the session by recording an outcome. `api.exam_get_next_item` becomes a fallback selection path that must not be mixed with engine-driven selection in one session. The dead policy knobs `minItemsPerArea`, `maxItemsPerArea`, and `stableDelta` are removed from the shipped policy; `stepSize` stays because it still drives the fallback difficulty bookkeeping. Two RPCs are added to the `api` surface, so `packages/db-types` regenerates additively. `apps/web` must be wired to the new flow before an end-to-end battery can complete.
- **Reversal:** Nothing was dropped, so reverting is additive. To restore database scoring authority: (1) re-add `if v_hard_cap_reached then v_outcome := app.exam_compute_outcome(p_session_id); end if;` to `api.exam_submit_response` (or restore the original `v_session_done` variable and its stop rule); (2) restore `minItemsPerArea`, `maxItemsPerArea`, and `stableDelta` to `app.exam_policy.config`, setting `stableDelta` **above** `stepSize` so the convergence branch is reachable at all; (3) optionally revoke `api.exam_record_outcome`. Reverting migration `supabase/migrations/20260725050000_exam_outcome_ownership.sql` wholesale also works, at the cost of the outcome audit columns. `app.exam_compute_outcome` was left intact and carries a `DEMOTED` comment naming the exact call site to restore.
- **Owner:** Proposed by the backend workstream (session 2026-07-25); requires team-lead ratification.
- **Relationship to prior decisions:** Implements the ownership already stated in BUILD_PLAN §3/§5/§6 and acts on the recommendation recorded in `supabase/EXAM_BACKEND_STATUS.md` §6. Does not supersede D-017 or any prior decision, and does not change the platform bindings ratified in D-012.

### D-022 — Split the screener's core-metric registry into per-item observed and trace-derived metrics

- **Date:** 2026-07-25
- **Status:** Proposed (awaiting team-lead ratification)
- **Decision:** Treat the adaptive screener's core-metric set as two kinds of metric rather than one. `observed` metrics are genuinely emitted per item by a renderer or by server verification, and their `minSamples` counts emissions as before. `derived` metrics are session-level aggregates the engine and scorer FIT from the accumulated trace, and their `minSamples` counts the inputs to that fit. `M-RTVAR`, `M-CONSIST`, `M-LEARNRATE`, `M-DIFFREACH` and `M-ROTSLOPE` are reclassified as derived. Additionally, the per-child response-time family (`M-RT`, `M-RTFIRST`, `M-RTVAR`) is given `session` rather than `per_area` adequacy, matching how `MEASUREMENTS.md` states their "how much to collect". `M-ROTSLOPE` remains derived but is demoted to NOT enforced, with the reason recorded in code.
- **Problem it fixes:** The stop rule in `packages/exam-engine/src/done.ts` required every *enforced* core metric to reach `minSamples` in every area, and `update.ts` counted a sample only when a metric id appeared on a per-item result. Four enforced metrics could never accumulate that way: `M-ROTSLOPE` is declared by 0 of the 66 catalog types; `M-LEARNRATE` by 0 verbal and 0 spatial types; `M-RTVAR` by 3 types with none in fluid or quantitative; `M-CONSIST` by 6 types spread 1–2 per area. A fifth, `M-DIFFREACH`, is only attached by `/api/exam-submit` on a correct answer. `isDone` could therefore only ever return true via `state.itemsServed >= hardItemCap`, making the battery silently FIXED-LENGTH at 60 items. The root cause is a category error: a response-time variance, a consistency rate, a growth slope and a rotation slope are statistics over a series of items, so no correct renderer can ever emit one per item.
- **Requirements served:** R11 (scalable/tunable in-house screener) via `EXAM_ADAPTIVE_BUILD_PLAN.md` §0 ("Battery length is variable — keep asking until there is adequate data in all core metrics to conclude a score. No fixed item count"), §3 (engine stop rule), §4 (basic-core metric set) and §5 (deterministic, trace-reproducible scoring).
- **Alternatives considered:** (a) Delete or lower the unreachable metrics until the tests pass — rejected: the metric taxonomy is a deliberate product artifact traced to `MEASUREMENTS.md`, and deleting it would hide the coverage gap rather than close it. (b) Require renderers to emit the aggregates — rejected as a category error no correct renderer can satisfy. (c) Make enforcement dynamically conditional on live bank supply — rejected: it would require passing the banks into `isDone`, breaking the callers that already code against the §3 signature, and it would silently relax the rule whenever a bank was missing. (d) Enforce `M-ROTSLOPE` as derived — rejected on supply grounds (below).
- **Evidence:** Cross-tabulation of `research/exam-question-types/catalog/master_types.jsonl` (66 type specs) against the 49 wired bank files, verified in this session; `MEASUREMENTS.md` entries for M-RT (">=20 RT samples per child"), M-RTFIRST (">=15 samples"), M-RTVAR (">=20-30 RT samples"), M-CONSIST (">=3 matched pairs"), M-LEARNRATE (">=8-12 escalating trials") and M-ROTSLOPE (">=12 trials spanning >=3 disparities"). Born-synthetic throughout (`syntheticOnly=true`, `validated=false`); no metric here is validated against any outcome.
- **Rationale:** Deriving aggregates from the trace is the only formulation under which "adequate data to conclude a score" is both meaningful and reachable. It also makes coverage *stronger*, not weaker: `M-CONSIST` and `M-LEARNRATE` become reachable in every area instead of depending on which one or two types happened to be selected. Because every derived value is a pure function of the stored per-item trace, the §5 requirement that a score be reproducible from a stored trace is preserved.
- **M-ROTSLOPE demotion:** The metric IS fittable — `SPA-VIEW-01` (140 items, disparities 0/60/120/180) and `SPA-XSCAN-01` (38 of 140 items, disparities 0–90) record `answer.angularDisparityDeg`, and a forced 40-trial block recovers a slope. It is not adequately SUPPLIABLE: no catalog type declares it, `SPA-XSCAN-01`'s disparity items all sit at difficulty >=14.6, and a >=12-trial fit cannot be obtained inside a proportionate spatial block without crowding out spatial construct breadth. It is therefore tracked and scored where fittable, but does not gate completion, and should be re-enforced when a dedicated mental-rotation type is wired with disparity across the full difficulty ramp.
- **Consequences:** `packages/exam-engine` gains `derived.ts` (adequacy conditions), `replay.ts` (state rebuilt from a stored trace), a per-area `ItemObservation` trace on `AreaState`, an optional `ScoredItem.stimulus` for server-only stimulus parameters, and `auditMetricSupply`. `packages/exam-scoring` gains `derived-metrics.ts`, which fits the aggregate VALUES; without it the exam profile's `learningRate` and `consistency` signals would have stayed permanently `unknown` on any real trace. A new real-bank test drives the stop rule against the actual registry and banks, closing the blind spot that let this ship: the previous synthetic harness fabricated every metric, so 25 passing tests could not detect an unsatisfiable rule. Three sole-source fragilities are now pinned by test (quantitative `M-PAE`, `M-REV`, `M-RT`, each supplied by exactly one wired type). **Open follow-up, not fixed here:** core-metric coverage now completes at 33 items for every ability profile, but total battery length is set by the estimate-stability arm and ranges 33–83 items, because item selection targets the current estimate and so pins the difficulty update to its 0.4/item floor. A child seeded far from their true ability — including a gifted child, the screener's target population — still reaches the 60-item cap. That is a distinct defect in the difficulty-update/selection interaction; changing it would move every score, so it is escalated rather than retuned here.
- **Owner:** Proposed by the exam-engine workstream (session 2026-07-25); requires team-lead ratification.
- **Relationship to prior decisions:** Implements the variable-length battery already locked in `EXAM_ADAPTIVE_BUILD_PLAN.md` §0; does not change the metric taxonomy's membership, the scoring policy defaults, the claim boundaries, or the born-synthetic status.

### D-023 — Replace the screener's fixed difficulty step with a reversal-indexed decaying step schedule

- **Date:** 2026-07-25
- **Status:** Proposed (awaiting team-lead ratification)
- **Decision:** The per-area difficulty update in `packages/exam-engine/src/update.ts` no longer moves the estimate by a fixed magnitude spanned by "surprise". It moves it by a **scheduled step that starts large and decays as evidence accumulates**: `step(m) = minUpdate + (initialStep − minUpdate) / m^stepDecayExponent`, where `m` counts the **direction reversals** already accumulated in that area (a reversal being a correct answer following a wrong one or vice versa), including the reversal the current response produces. Surprise is retained but demoted to a multiplier on the scheduled step (`step × (1 + surpriseGain × surprise)`), and the `M-ERRTYPE` near-miss softening is unchanged. The whole schedule is exposed as tunable engine policy: `initialStep` (2.0), `minUpdate` (0.25, was 0.4), `maxUpdate` (3.0, was 1.0), `stepDecayExponent` (1.0), `stepBurnInReversals` (0), `surpriseGain` (1.0). Nothing else changes: the stop rule, the core-metric registry, the selection rule, the scoring policy, and the public signatures of `startState/nextType/nextItem/update/isDone` are all untouched.
- **Problem it fixes:** The open follow-up recorded in D-022's consequences. Core-metric coverage completed at 33 items for every ability profile, but *total* battery length was set by the estimate-stability arm and ran 33–83 items. Item selection targets the **current** estimate, so a child is always served items matched to where the engine already believes they are; the surprise term (`|difficulty − estimate| / 19`) therefore stays near zero by construction and the estimate could only ever move at its 0.4-per-item floor. Climbing the 7 points from a '4-5' seed of 11 to a true ability of 18 took ~18 items of pure climbing **per area** before stability could even begin to be judged. A gifted child — the screener's entire target population — ran 83 items and passed the 60-item safety cap without the estimate settling; an above-level-seeded child with low ability ran 128. Measured against the real registry and banks, the same profiles now finish at 36 and 45 items, and no profile in the matrix reaches the cap.
- **Requirements served:** R11 (scalable, tunable in-house screener) via `EXAM_ADAPTIVE_BUILD_PLAN.md` §0 (variable-length battery that concludes when there is adequate data, realistic target ≤45 minutes of child time) and §3 (engine stop rule and estimate); R7 (auditability and reproducible replay), since the schedule is indexed off the stored trace and adds no hidden state.
- **Alternatives considered:** (a) *Widen the item-selection window so the engine deliberately serves off-target items and re-animates the surprise term* — rejected: it buys estimate movement by degrading measurement, serving items the child has no chance on (or no challenge from) purely to generate a signal, and it changes what the child experiences rather than how the engine reasons. (b) *Decay the step by item count instead of reversals (plain Robbins-Monro `a_n = c/n^α`)* — rejected: the step would shrink while the estimate is still travelling, so it penalises exactly the child the fix is for. Indexing on reversals keeps the full stride until there is evidence the ability region has been bracketed (Kesten's accelerated variant of Robbins-Monro; the same rule as a Levitt/transformed up–down staircase, which shortens its step at each reversal). (c) *Raise the fixed floor from 0.4 to ~1.0* — rejected: it fixes the climb and breaks the settle, since the estimate can then never resolve finer than a full scale point and the tail of the scale loses precision. (d) *Loosen `stabilityDrift`/`stabilitySd` so the unsettled estimate counts as settled* — rejected outright: that lowers the bar for "adequate data" instead of meeting it, which is the failure mode D-020 was written to close. (e) *Escalate again without fixing* — rejected: the escalation ground was that changing the update "would move every score", which is true but not blocking, because every bank is born-synthetic and uncalibrated (`syntheticOnly=true`, `validated=false`), no score is validated against any outcome, and no admissions decision depends on any current number.
- **Evidence:** Fresh, from this session, against the **real** registry and banks (`research/exam-question-types/`), via the extended `packages/exam-engine/src/real-bank.test.ts`. The band-matched 33-item case did not regress in length and its estimate did not become jittery. Everything remains born-synthetic; the simulated child is a deterministic threshold responder against uncalibrated banks, so these are **convergence** results, not psychometric validity.
- **Rationale:** A single fixed step cannot serve the two jobs an adaptive estimate has — travel far enough to find a child who is nowhere near their grade-band seed, then move finely enough to settle on them. A decaying gain is the standard resolution in stochastic approximation, and indexing the decay on reversals rather than on item count is what makes it correct **here**: because selection targets the current estimate, one-sided responses are the engine's only signal that it has not arrived yet, and reversals are its only signal that it has. Keeping the asymptote strictly positive (`minUpdate` 0.25) preserves the ability to travel again if a child's performance shifts, and makes the tail of the scale **finer** than the 0.4 fixed floor it replaces, so precision improves rather than being traded away. Reversal counts are read off `AreaState.trace`, which `update` already builds purely from stored `ScoredItem`s, so the schedule introduces no state that is not persisted and a stored trace still reproduces the estimate exactly (asserted for every profile).
- **Consequences:** **Every score moves, as expected and as sanctioned above** — but the movement is much larger than a step-size retune would suggest, and it exposes a pre-existing miscalibration rather than creating one. `packages/exam-scoring` brackets an area by its difficulty-weighted accuracy over the items served. Under the old slow climb, a gifted child accumulated a long tail of far-below-ability items answered correctly, so their measured accuracy was 0.88 and they landed in the top bracket (composite 15.81 against a true mean ability of 16.5). Under a battery that actually converges, every child — gifted or struggling — ends near 50% accuracy, because that is what a matched item is; the same gifted child now measures 0.49–0.62 accuracy and scores a composite of **8.72**, while the band-matched child at true ability 11 scores 6.33. The engine's own per-area estimates separate those two children cleanly (16.05 vs 11.19 in verbal); the scorer no longer does. **This is a defect in the §5 accuracy-bracket contract, not in this change, and it is deliberately NOT fixed here** — see the open follow-up below. `difficultyDelta` changes signature (first argument is now the prior area state rather than a bare estimate) and `directionReversals`/`stepSize` are added to the public surface; `difficultyDelta` has no caller outside the engine and its tests, and the five contract functions are unchanged, so `apps/web` needs no edit. `EngineConfig` gains four knobs and changes two defaults; `apps/web`'s `EXAM_ENGINE_OVERRIDES` sets none of them and inherits the new schedule. The database's demoted `stepSize` (D-029) is a separate fallback knob and is untouched.
- **Open follow-up, not fixed here (escalated):** The scorer positions a child by accuracy first and difficulty second. That inverts the information content of a converged adaptive battery, in which accuracy is approximately constant by construction and **difficulty reached** carries the ability signal. As delivered, a gifted child and a band-matched child now receive composites of 8.72 and 6.33 on a 1–20 scale while their true abilities are 16.5 and 11. Fixing it means changing what §5 brackets on — most likely scoring from the converged per-area estimate and `M-DIFFREACH` rather than from raw accuracy — which is a change to a ratified scoring contract and to every reported number, so it needs its own decision rather than being folded into a convergence fix. Nothing depends on the current numbers (born-synthetic, `validated=false`, no admissions decision), so no applicant is affected while it is open.
- **Reversal:** No code needs to be reverted. The schedule strictly generalises the rule it replaces: setting `initialStep` equal to `minUpdate` removes the decay entirely, and `{ minUpdate: 0.4, maxUpdate: 1.0, initialStep: 0.4, surpriseGain: 1.5 }` reproduces the pre-D-023 `magnitude = 0.4 + 0.6 × surprise` **exactly** — pinned by a test in `engine.test.ts` that checks equality to 10 decimal places across the estimate/difficulty/near-miss grid. Restoring the old behaviour is therefore a policy edit, not a revert, which also means the admissions team can A/B the schedule from config once the admin portal lands.
- **Owner:** Proposed by the exam-engine workstream (session 2026-07-25); requires team-lead ratification.
- **Relationship to prior decisions:** Closes the open follow-up recorded in D-022's consequences. Does not change the metric taxonomy, the stop rule, the selection rule, the scoring policy defaults, the claim boundaries, or the born-synthetic status. Does not supersede any prior decision.

### D-024 — Offer a difficulty-adjusted ability statistic as an alternative bracket driver; the accuracy default is unchanged pending the owner's decision

- **Date:** 2026-07-25
- **Status:** Proposed (awaiting team-lead ratification). **Nothing is switched on by this entry.**
- **Decision:** Add `bracketing.mode` to `ExamPolicy` in `packages/exam-scoring`, with two values. `accuracy` is the existing, unchanged contract and remains the default: (difficulty-weighted) accuracy over the items served picks the bracket by each bracket's `accuracyMin`. `ability` picks the bracket by each bracket's existing θ span, using a **difficulty-adjusted ability fitted from the trace** — the location parameter of a one-parameter (Rasch-style) logistic response model over the (difficulty, score) pairs, i.e. the difficulty at which the child's fitted success probability is 50%. The fit lives in a new `packages/exam-scoring/src/ability.ts`, is exposed as `ABILITY_BRACKET_POLICY`, and is tunable through `bracketing.ability` (`slope` 1.0, `priorSd` 6.0). **The two-stage §5 structure the project owner specified is preserved in both modes:** the bracketing statistic sets the bracket and never positions; the within-bracket metric average (M-DIFFREACH, inverse M-RTVAR, M-LEARNRATE, M-ERRTYPE, process/domain terms) positions the child inside it and never brackets. `scoreExam` keeps its `(items, policy)` signature, its purity, and its single input: the fit reads only per-item `difficulty` and `score` from the stored trace and consults no engine state.
- **Problem it fixes:** The open follow-up escalated in D-023's consequences. Since the engine began converging (D-023), item selection targets the current estimate, so a well-targeted item is by definition one the child has about an even chance on, and every child — gifted or struggling — finishes near the same accuracy. Accuracy therefore no longer carries the ability signal; **difficulty reached** does. Measured on this branch against the real registry and banks, a child with equal true ability in all four areas seeded at band '4-5' produces a composite that spans only **2.26–11.04** across true abilities 3–19 on a 1–20 scale, never approaches the top of the scale for a maximally able child, and barely separates true ability 11 from 15 (6.33 vs 6.59). The engine's own per-area estimates track the same children to within about 1.0 point, so the information is present in the trace and the scorer discards it (E-078).
- **Requirements served:** R11 (scalable, tunable in-house screener) via `EXAM_ADAPTIVE_BUILD_PLAN.md` §5 (deterministic bracket-then-position scoring on the [1, 20] scale); R7 (auditability and reproducible replay), since the new statistic is fitted from the stored trace and adds no hidden state; R10 (claim boundaries), since the alternative is offered as evidenced and unratified rather than adopted.
- **Alternatives considered:** (a) *Bracket on `M-DIFFREACH` (the hardest difficulty solved)* — rejected: it is an extreme order statistic, so one lucky correct answer high on the ramp defines the whole bracket and no amount of subsequent failure can move it down; it is also already the dominant within-bracket positioning term, so promoting it to bracket driver would double-count it and leave stage 2 mostly refining on the same number it just bracketed on. (b) *Bracket on the mean difficulty of correct items* — rejected: it ignores failures entirely, so it is bounded above by whatever the child happened to solve and is dragged down by however many easy items the adaptive path served early; two children with identical ability but different seeds would bracket differently. (c) *Bracket on the engine's converged per-area estimate* — rejected on contract grounds even though it is the most accurate number available: it would make the scorer depend on engine internal state rather than on the trace, breaking the §5 property that a stored trace plus a frozen policy reproduces the score, and would couple two packages that BUILD_PLAN §7 keeps independent. (d) *Bracket on a "highest difficulty with k consecutive correct" reliability threshold* — rejected: `k` is arbitrary, it discards partial credit, and an adaptive path frequently never serves `k` items at one difficulty. (e) *Fit a two-parameter model and estimate discrimination as well* — rejected: with ~8–11 items per area the slope is not identifiable, and against a deterministic responder it runs to infinity; discrimination belongs in policy, where an admin can see and change it. (f) *Change the default outright* — rejected: the two-stage bracket-then-position contract and its accuracy driver are the owner's stated product requirement, so replacing the driver is the owner's decision, not the implementer's. (g) *Fix it by widening the brackets or reweighting the within-bracket metrics instead* — rejected: it treats a symptom, since no reweighting of stage 2 can recover a signal stage 1 has already thrown away.

### D-025 — Price the screener's age-band item preference in scale points instead of letting it override difficulty targeting

- **Date:** 2026-07-25
- **Status:** Proposed (awaiting team-lead ratification)
- **Decision:** `nextItem` in `packages/exam-engine/src/selection.ts` no longer ranks an age-band match strictly ahead of closeness to the area estimate. It ranks candidates by a single **targeting cost**, `|item.difficulty − estimate| + (item is tagged for the child's grade band ? 0 : ageBandBias)`, with the seeded jitter and item-id tie-breaks unchanged behind it. `ageBandBias` is a new `EngineConfig` knob, default **0.5** scale points, so the age band still decides between comparably targeted items but can no longer buy a 3-point targeting error. Nothing else changes: the `±difficultyWindow` pre-filter, the type-level `AGE_BAND_BONUS`, the difficulty update (D-023), the stop rule, the core-metric registry, the scoring packages, the banks, and the public signatures of `startState/nextType/nextItem/update/isDone` are all untouched.
- **Problem it fixes:** A systematic estimate bias that the existing accuracy tests could not see. `real-bank.test.ts` asserted a 1.5-point tolerance over seven hand-picked `CONVERGENCE_PROFILES`; sweeping every integer ability from 2 to 19 (equal ability in all four areas, band '4-5', real registry and banks) breached that tolerance at **five** of the eighteen levels — 12 by 2.58, 13 by 2.67, 14 by 1.87 upward, and 5 by 1.53, 7 by 1.91 downward — while all seven named profiles passed. The mechanism is an interaction between selection and the D-023 update, not a bank shortage. Almost every wired type's `'4-5'` items stop between difficulty 11.8 and 13.4 (`VER-*` at 11.8, `FLU-MATRIXBUILD-01` at 12.0, `FLU-ANALOGY-01` at 13.4) and start around 7.6–8.1, so once an area's estimate left that range every band-matched candidate sat on one side of it. At true ability 13 the engine served items **1.19 points below the estimate on average**, and 22 of 43 items were not the closest available: with the estimate at 15.00 it served a difficulty-12.00 item while a difficulty-15.00 item of the same type was unseen. `difficultyDelta` takes its direction from correctness alone and its `surprise` term is clamped at zero below, so it amplifies a response that contradicts the estimate but never attenuates one that merely confirms it — a correct answer on an item 3 points *below* the estimate still raised the estimate by a full scheduled step. That closes a positive feedback loop: the estimate rises, the band-matched items become relatively easier, the child keeps getting them right, and the estimate parks around 15.0–15.7 for a child at 13. Below the band the same loop runs in reverse (at true 7 the engine served a difficulty-8.00 item to an estimate of 5.15, because no band-matched fluid item exists below 7.6), which is why 5 and 7 undershoot rather than overshoot. The region hit hardest, 12–14, is exactly where a screener decides whether a child looks gifted.
- **Requirements served:** R11 (scalable, tunable in-house screener) via `EXAM_ADAPTIVE_BUILD_PLAN.md` §0 (variable-length battery that concludes when there is adequate data) and §3 (engine estimate and selection rule); R7 (auditability and reproducible replay), since selection stays a pure function of stored state and config and every swept trace still rebuilds its estimate exactly; R10 (claim boundaries), since the entry reports a recovery result against born-synthetic banks and claims nothing about validity.
- **Alternatives considered:** (a) *Attenuate the difficulty update when a response merely confirms what the estimate already says* — the mirror of D-023's `surprise` term, and the more general fix, since it holds whatever selection does. Rejected as the primary fix after tracing it: with the confirming step damped, the only items that can still raise a '4-5' estimate above ~13 are the off-band ones selection was already declining to serve, so the gifted climb stalls near 15 and either the accuracy or the 48-item budget breaks. It treats the symptom (mis-weighted evidence) while leaving the cause (the engine declining on-target items that exist and are unseen). Worth revisiting **after** selection is targeted, where it would be a second-order refinement rather than a compensator. (b) *Drop the age-band preference from item selection entirely* — rejected: age-band tagging is a content-appropriateness signal, not noise, and `ageBandBias: 0` is a strictly worse default than a small positive value because it discards a free tie-break between items the engine considers equally informative. ### D-026 — Served bank items are registered into the database on demand, and the two per-item verdicts are both recorded

- **Date:** 2026-07-25
- **Status:** Proposed (awaiting team-lead ratification)
- **Decision:** Wire `apps/web` to persist the full adaptive exam trace through the existing `api.exam_*` RPCs, adding exactly one RPC — `api.exam_register_item` — which idempotently admits a generated bank item (with its SERVER-ONLY answer key) into `app.exam_item` before the first response against it is recorded. Registration is INSERT-only, is reachable only by the `admissions_operator` proctor persona, and returns no part of the key. Two supporting changes ride with it: `app.exam_question_type.type_code`'s check is widened to the mixed-case vocabulary D-020(a) ratifies, and `app.exam_question_type` / `app.exam_item` gain INSERT (not UPDATE or DELETE) policies for `api_executor` so a `SECURITY DEFINER` function can write under FORCED RLS. Separately, `/api/exam-submit` now appends an `app_verdict` telemetry event per item recording the verdict and metric map that `packages/exam-engine` and `packages/exam-scoring` actually consumed.
- **Requirements served:** R7 (auditability and reproducible replay), R11 (the in-house screener stores a full trace, `EXAM_ADAPTIVE_BUILD_PLAN.md` §0/§6)
- **Why registration was necessary:** `api.exam_submit_response` resolves `p_item_id` against `app.exam_item` and raises `PT400` when it is unknown. The only rows in that table are the 80 placeholders `20260724130200_exam_adaptive_seed.sql` generates at migration time; the runner serves from the 63 generated banks (7,900 items) which carry their own ids. The two sets are disjoint, so before this change not one real served item could be recorded and no trace existed at all.
- **Alternatives considered:** (a) *Bulk-load all 7,900 bank items in a migration or an ops script*, so the database owns the whole bank as BUILD_PLAN §6 describes — rejected for this change: ~22 MB of generated content in version control, a load step that must be re-run whenever a bank is regenerated, and bank syncing is a separate workstream. It remains available and does not conflict, since registration is a no-op for an item already present. (b) *Rename the 11 mixed-case type codes to satisfy the existing check* — rejected for the same reason D-020 rejected it (churn against the established catalog vocabulary). (c) *Grant UPDATE alongside INSERT on the bank tables* — rejected: an immutable bank means a registered item's answer key cannot be swapped after a response was scored against it.
- **Evidence:** A round trip against the running local stack, 2026-07-25: a 22-item engine-driven battery recorded as 1 session row, 22 `exam_item_response` rows in administration order with raw answers and metric maps intact, 88 `exam_telemetry_event` rows, and one `exam_session_outcome` row with `scorer_source = 'packages/exam-scoring'` and `scorer_input_hash` re-derivable from the stored trace. Reproduced by `apps/web/src/lib/exam/persistence.integration.test.ts`. Everything is born-synthetic (`synthetic_only = true`, `validated = false`).
- **Rationale:** The trace is worthless if it cannot record the items actually served, and lazy registration records exactly those and nothing else. Putting the real key in `app.exam_item.answer_key` — a column revoked from every client role and excluded from every served projection — strengthens rather than weakens the firewall D-029 protects, because it is what lets the database verify at all.
- **Consequences:** D-029's split is preserved: `packages/exam-engine` still selects (`api.exam_get_next_item` is never called on a persisted session), `packages/exam-scoring` still scores and is stored verbatim through `api.exam_record_outcome`, and the demoted `app.exam_compute_outcome` is never called. Persistence is opt-in per environment and every write is best-effort, so an unreachable database degrades to the previous in-memory behaviour rather than failing a child's exam. The proctor persona is authenticated with the synthetic `admissions_operator` fixture seeded by `pnpm db:users` and the app's existing publishable-key convention; no service-role or secret key enters the app runtime. `packages/db-types` regenerates additively.
- **Open defect this exposes, escalated not fixed:** `app.exam_score_response` and the app's per-type verifiers grade the same raw response by different rules and disagree on **15 of 22 items** in the recorded round trip. The database's vocabulary is a comparison against `answer_key.correctKey`/`solution`; 30 of the 63 wired types are graded by a constructed-response verifier that re-derives the expected answer from `content` or from a non-key field of `answer`, 18 declare `computed_solver` but store `correctKey` rather than the `solution` the database reads, and 7 key by numeric option index the database does not read. Because `api.exam_submit_response` writes its own verdict into `exam_item_response.correct/score/metrics['M-ACC']`, and `app.exam_scorer_input_json` projects those columns, re-running `scoreExam` over the database's canonical scorer input yields **3.775** against a recorded composite of **7.434** — the D-029 audit path does not currently reconcile. Reconciling it means either porting 30 typed verifiers into plpgsql or moving verification authority to the application tier, which D-029 considered and rejected as its alternative (c); both are governance decisions, not persistence work. Note D-029(c) was already contradicted in practice before this change, since `/api/exam-submit` has verified against on-disk keys in the Next.js server tier since the runner was wired. The mitigation shipped here is that nothing is silent: the `app_verdict` telemetry rows carry the verdict the score was actually computed from, and re-running `scoreExam` over them reproduces the recorded composite to the last digit (7.43376016008243, difference 0.000000).
- **Reversal:** Additive throughout. Reverting `supabase/migrations/20260725160000_exam_item_registration.sql` drops the RPC, the two INSERT policies, and the widened check (the narrower check must be restored by hand). Setting `GT_EXAM_PERSISTENCE_ENABLED` to anything but `true` disables every write with no code change.
- **Owner:** Proposed by the exam-persistence workstream (session 2026-07-25); requires team-lead ratification.
- **Relationship to prior decisions:** Implements the storage contract in BUILD_PLAN §6 under the ownership D-029 sets; adopts D-020(a)'s type-code vocabulary in the database. Supersedes nothing.

### D-027 — The database is the single authority on per-item correctness: the per-type verifiers are ported to plpgsql

- **Date:** 2026-07-25
- **Status:** Proposed (awaiting team-lead ratification)
- **Decision:** Port the per-type answer verifiers from `apps/web/src/lib/exam/verifiers/` into plpgsql, behind a dispatcher `app.exam_verify_response(p_item_id uuid, p_raw_answer jsonb)` that resolves a verifier in the same precedence order as `resolveVerifier()` — registered per-type verifier, then the generic named by the item's server-only `scoring.rule`, then the option-key default — and make `api.exam_submit_response` verify through it. `app.exam_score_response` is **demoted, not deleted**, following the convention D-029 established for `app.exam_compute_outcome`. This migration lands the foundation and a proof of the pattern: the dispatcher, all three generic verifiers, and four per-type verifiers (one per bank domain, each deliberately the awkward shape in its domain). The remaining 27 land in later additive migrations, each adding one function and one row to `app.exam_verifier_registry`.
- **Requirements served:** R7 (auditability and reproducible replay), R11 (in-house screener, `EXAM_ADAPTIVE_BUILD_PLAN.md` §0/§2/§5/§6)
- **Owner's rationale (anti-cheat):** Verification that happens inside a schema no client role can reach cannot leak a key, a solution, or a derived expected answer, and cannot be bypassed by a client that reaches the RPC surface directly. Verification that happens in the Next.js server tier depends on that tier being the only caller, which is an operational property rather than a schema-enforced one. This is a security posture, not a performance or convenience choice.
- **Relationship to D-029:** This **implements D-029's stated intent rather than reversing it**. D-029 rejected moving verification into the app tier as its alternative (c) precisely because "the answer-key firewall is only meaningful because key comparison happens inside a schema no client role can reach". Practice had drifted from that: `/api/exam-submit` and `bank-loader.ts` have graded against on-disk keys in the Next.js server tier since the runner was wired, and the database's own comparison had become a second, disagreeing verdict rather than the authority. D-027 restores the database as the authority D-029 named.
- **The defect this closes:** D-026 escalated it and `docs/architecture/EXAM_PERSISTENCE_NOTES.md` §4 documents it. On a real 22-item battery the database's verdict and the app tier's disagreed on **15 of 22 items**, so recomputing the composite from `app.exam_scorer_input_json` — the canonical scorer input the D-029 audit path hashes — gave **3.775** against a recorded composite of **7.434**. The hash was fine; what it hashed was not the input the recorded score came from.
- **Alternatives considered:** (a) *Move verification authority to the app tier and stop verifying in the database* — rejected: this is D-029(c), and it is what the owner's anti-cheat rationale rules out. (b) *Keep both verdicts and reconcile at read time* — rejected: it is what D-026 already shipped as a mitigation, and it leaves `exam_item_response.correct/score/metrics['M-ACC']` — the columns `app.exam_scorer_input_json` projects — holding the verdict nobody scored from. (c) *Widen `app.exam_score_response`'s single comparison to cover more shapes* — rejected: 31 of the served types re-derive the expected answer from `content` or a non-key field of `answer`, so there is no single comparison that covers them; attempting one would produce a third set of rules agreeing with neither side. (d) *Port all 31 in one migration* — rejected as unreviewable and un-parallelisable; the registry table exists so the remaining 27 can land in independent migrations that cannot conflict.
- **Evidence:** E-081. Measured by `pnpm exam:verify:diff` over 63 served types × 12 real bank items × 3 responses (correct / wrong / skipped) = 2,268 cases: the demoted `app.exam_score_response` agrees with the app tier on **1,659/2,268**; `app.exam_verify_response` agrees on **1,989/2,268**. Every one of the 33 generic-verifier types and all four ported types agree on every case, metric maps included, and 444 of those agreements are cases both sides scored **correct**, so the agreement is not the vacuous kind. Every remaining disagreement is an unported per-type verifier, reported as PENDING rather than passing quietly. `pnpm db:test` 335/335 (was 306/306; +29 new), `pnpm db:lint` clean, `pnpm -r test` 718/718 unchanged.
- **What becomes of the app-tier verifiers:** they stay, and they become a **cross-check, not the authority**. `/api/exam-submit` still grades the response it returns to the runner, because the adaptive engine runs client-side and must update on a verdict before the persistence write completes; and it still appends the `app_verdict` telemetry event D-026 added. What changes is that `exam_item_response.correct/score/metrics` — the columns the audit path reads — are now written from the database's own verdict, and the differential harness makes any divergence between the two an observable, failing condition rather than a silent one. Deleting the app-tier verifiers is explicitly **not** part of this decision: two independent implementations that must agree is the property the harness tests, and collapsing to one would remove it.
- **Consequences:** Per-item verdicts change for every type whose database verdict was previously wrong, which is most of them — this is the point, and it means **no stored trace recorded before this migration is comparable to one recorded after it**. `api.exam_submit_response` now merges the verifier's key-dependent metrics (`M-POLY`, `M-PAE`, `M-HYP`, …) into `exam_item_response.metrics` between the client-tracked metrics and the server-authoritative `M-ACC`, so the database can no longer be given a metric map the client invented for a metric only the server can compute. A skipped or timed-out submission is now scored incorrect **without grading**, mirroring `/api/exam-submit`, which is the only way a partially-built artefact submitted at timeout cannot be credited in the database while the app tier calls it wrong. Two pgTAP fixtures in `120_exam_adaptive_backend.test.sql` moved from `{"key":"B"}` to `{"selectedKey":"B"}`: the ported keyed verifier reads the two fields the runner actually sends, and the demoted implementation's acceptance of `key` was never exercised by a client. The answer-key firewall is unchanged in posture and newly asserted in `123_exam_verify_plpgsql.test.sql`: every new function and the registry are revoked from PUBLIC, `anon`, `authenticated` and `service_role`, granted only to `api_executor`, and one assertion fails if any future port forgets its revoke. **Reported and not fixed:** five app-tier verifier behaviours that look wrong (including one metric id, `M-POLY`, carrying opposite directions on different types) are listed in `docs/architecture/EXAM_VERIFIER_PORT_INVENTORY.md` §8 — changing any of them is a scoring-semantics decision this one does not make. **One type cannot be ported without a further decision:** GB-WORDLADDER-01 judges a rung against a 4,000-word child lexicon read off disk, which is not a bank field and so is not in `app.exam_item`; §6 of the inventory sets out the three options and recommends none of them on the owner's behalf.
- **Reversal:** Additive and forward-only. Nothing is dropped: `app.exam_score_response` is unchanged and still granted to `api_executor`, and pgTAP assertion 8 in `123_exam_verify_plpgsql.test.sql` fails if that stops being true. To restore it, change the one call site in `api.exam_submit_response` back to `v_scored := app.exam_score_response(p_item_id, p_raw_answer);` and drop the metric merge. To retire a single ported verifier without touching code, delete its row from `app.exam_verifier_registry` and the dispatcher falls back to the generic chain for that type.
- **Owner:** Decided by the project owner (session 2026-07-25) on the anti-cheat rationale above; implementation proposed by the exam-verification workstream and requires team-lead ratification.
- **Relationship to prior decisions:** Implements D-029's alternative-(c) reasoning rather than reversing it, and closes the defect D-026 escalated. Does not change selection ownership (D-029: `packages/exam-engine` still selects), the final score (D-029/D-024: `packages/exam-scoring` output is still stored verbatim), the stop rule, the metric taxonomy (D-022), the age-band pricing (D-025), the claim boundaries, or the born-synthetic status. Supersedes nothing.

### D-028 — The child-facing screener hides the per-child telemetry panel; `?telemetry=1` retains it for demonstration

- **Date:** 2026-07-25
- **Status:** Proposed (awaiting team-lead ratification)
- **Decision:** Every renderer demo hides its `Live telemetry · per-child` sidebar when it is embedded in a host (the exam runner), unless its own URL carries `?telemetry=1`. The gate is presentational only: sampling, metric computation, the `metrics` map on the `ItemResult`, and the `{type:'telemetry', event}` postMessage stream are untouched, so the recorded trace is byte-for-byte what it was.

- **Why this is a validity decision, not a cosmetic one:** The panel does not merely look internal — it *names the behavioural construct as it is being measured*, beside its live value. `M-PLANFUL` is labelled "Planful: pause before first move"; `M-ENGAGE` is "Focus losses (tracked, not enforced)"; `M-REV` is "Revisited cells". The same reactivity applies to every process metric on the panel, and it is worst for exactly the metrics that are meant to distinguish deliberate problem-solving from guessing — the ones the profile leans on hardest. The appearance problem (a scored child assessment that looks like a debugging view) is real but secondary; it would not by itself justify a change to all 63 served demos.
- **Alternatives considered:** (a) *Delete the panel from the demos* — rejected: it is the researcher's only live read of the renderer, and standalone use is an explicitly protected behaviour. (b) *Strip the panel in `scripts/sync-exam-demos.mjs` at copy time* — rejected: the script's own `COMPAT_PATCHES` docblock records that a copy-time patch is a latent divergence between what ships and what the demo author sees, and it would also make the panel unreachable for a live demonstration of the real runner. (c) *Host-driven toggle over postMessage* — rejected as heavier than the problem: it needs a protocol addition, and the panel would still be visible for the frames before the host's message lands. (d) *A per-demo bespoke edit* — rejected; see the mechanism note below.

### D-030 — Learning rate is measured once per session over a novel block in one area, and is reported as an ordinal band or not at all

- **Date:** 2026-07-28
- **Status:** Proposed (awaiting team-lead ratification)
- **Decision:** Three parts, which stand or fall together.
  1. **A learning rate is a session-level figure over ONE area, not four per-area figures.** A dedicated novel block of 30 unseen items runs in a single area, fixed by policy for every child, and only after that area's standing estimate has settled (`areaEstimateStable`). `M-LEARNRATE` moves from `enforced: true, minSamples: 8` per area to `enforced: false, minSamples: 30`, so the battery stop rule no longer demands a growth fit in all four areas.
  2. **Every item records which regime served it.** `stage: 'standing' | 'learning'` is added to `ItemObservation`, `ScoredItem` and the contract, defaulting to `standing`. The learning-rate fit reads `learning` items only. `stage` is server-added, not part of `itemResultSchema`, so a client cannot relabel its own trace into or out of the fit.
  3. **The reportable output is an ordinal band, and `indeterminate` is a first-class answer.** The rate is fitted by a MAP logistic learning curve (`theta(t) = theta0 + lambda * t`, 1PL matching `deriveAbilityEstimate`) and reported through `learningRateReadout`, which returns `below | typical | above | indeterminate` with a reason. It returns `indeterminate` when the block is short, when the fit does not converge, or when the posterior SE is at or wider than the band half-width. The `[0,1]` block-half value from `deriveLearningRate` is retained as a labelled diagnostic and is not a reported score. Neither figure enters `scoreExam`'s output.
- **Requirements served:** R7 (auditability — the stage marker is what makes a rate recomputable from the stored trace), R9/R10 (claim boundaries — an ordinal band with an explicit refusal case, `validated=false`), R8 (feasibility — 30 items in one area rather than 32+ across four)
- **Alternatives considered:** (a) *Keep the per-area growth index and lengthen each area* — rejected on measurement: four 15-trial blocks recover at r ≈ 0.18 each and cost more items than one 30-trial block at r ≈ 0.45, so splitting the budget buys strictly less for strictly more (E-095). (b) *Keep it enforced per area at 8 samples and just report it more cautiously* — rejected: at 8 trials recovery is r ≈ 0.07 and the estimate is attenuated to a fifth of its true size, so no amount of caution in the wording makes the number informative, and enforcing it held the stop rule hostage to producing it. (c) *Pick each child's strongest area for the block* — rejected: a rate measured in different areas for different children is not comparable, and comparability is the only thing the figure is currently good for. (d) *Report a numeric rate with a confidence interval instead of a band* — rejected: at 30 trials the interval is wider than the spread children are thought to occupy, so the interval would be the entire scale and the point estimate would be read anyway. (e) *Report a band using a synthetic reference distribution* — rejected as the most dangerous option: it would put a made-up mean and SD behind a sentence about a child, which is why `LearningRateReference` has no defaults and must be supplied by the caller. (f) *Drop the learning-rate construct entirely* — not taken, because the block is cheap to administer and ordering a measured cohort is supportable now; but this remains the correct outcome if real data shows the between-child spread is narrower than any feasible block can resolve.
- **Evidence:** E-095 (recovery, attenuation and SE by block length; the confound demonstrated in this engine and shown not to affect the difficulty-conditioned fit). Builds on E-073, which reached the same conclusion in logit units on the retired engine.
- **Rationale:** A growth statistic computed over a bracketing trace measures the algorithm, not the child. While an area is still homing in, the hardest difficulty solved rises as the search converges on a child seeded away from their level — so a child who learned nothing reports growth, and reports MORE growth the further their seed started from them. The block removes that confound structurally rather than by correction: it begins after the search has finished, so the only thing left that can move is the child. Everything else follows from measuring the result honestly. At the block length the evidence supports, one child's estimate is less precise than the differences between children are believed to be, which is exactly the condition under which a band may be named for a cohort and must not be named for an individual.
- **Consequences:** Expect `indeterminate` on real sessions and treat it as correct output, not a defect — no reference distribution for lambda exists, and E-095 stays open until there is real per-child data at two or more occasions. Two named limits carry forward. The fit assumes a **linear** climb while empirical learning curves are commonly exponential or power-shaped, so the recovered slope summarises a possibly-wrong curve. And the response model uses **guessing = 0** to stay consistent with the standing fit, which is a known misspecification on four-option multiple choice; it is exposed as an option with that noted. Adding 30 items in one area lengthens the battery, and whether that is affordable against a child's attention is a product decision this does not settle. 45–60 trials is where the statistic becomes genuinely sharp (r ≈ 0.75–0.86), so if a rate is ever needed for an individual rather than a ranking, the block has to grow rather than the estimator improve.
- **Reversal:** Additive and cleanly separable. Setting `M-LEARNRATE` back to `enforced: true, minSamples: 8` in `packages/exam-engine/src/config.ts` restores the previous stop-rule behaviour exactly; `deriveLearningRate` was never changed, only re-labelled, so the old number is still computed by the same code. `stage` defaults to `standing`, so every trace, fixture and stored session predating this remains valid and re-scores identically — nothing needs backfilling. Deleting `learning-block.ts`, `learning-curve.ts` and `learning-rate-readout.ts` with their tests and the two barrel exports removes the new surface without touching selection, the standing fit, or `scoreExam`.
- **Owner:** Proposed by the exam structure workstream (session 2026-07-28); requires team-lead ratification.
- **Relationship to prior decisions:** Narrows the metric registry set by D-022 (one metric's enforcement and threshold) without changing the taxonomy. Respects D-029's ownership split: the fit and readout live in `packages/exam-scoring`, block administration in `packages/exam-engine`, and the database gains no new authority — the two packages remain mutually independent per BUILD_PLAN §7, so the target difficulty crosses between them as a plain number. Does not change selection (D-025), the standing bracketing (D-023), persistence (D-026), or verification (D-027). Supersedes nothing.

### D-200 — The learning-curve fit assumes the item format's chance floor, and no band may be named without a measured contamination floor

- **Date:** 2026-07-30
- **Status:** **Approved** (owner sign-off 2026-07-30). This changes statistical scoring logic, which `AGENTS.md` puts behind the full-mode bar; the owner reviewed and signed it off explicitly before merge.
- **Decision:** Three parts, of which the first is a correction and the other two are judgments the owner should confirm.
  1. **`estimateLearningCurve` defaults its lower asymptote to the five-option chance rate (`DEFAULT_GUESSING = 0.2`) instead of 0.** Because `nextTargetTheta` forwards its options into the same estimator, one default corrects both the reported fit and the difficulty targeting, and there is no configuration in which only one of them is corrected. Callers administering a different item format pass the reciprocal of their option count.
  2. **`LearningRateReference.contaminationFloor` is a REQUIRED field, and separability is tested against posterior SE PLUS that floor.** A band is no longer named on the strength of random error alone. The floor is the largest climb the pipeline is measured to fit for a child who learned nothing, for the caller's own bank, length and item format; `pnpm exam:block-harness -- --fix-probe` measures it. This follows the existing rule on this type that `mean` and `sd` have no defaults, for the same reason: a number nobody chose must not reach a results screen.
  3. **The comparability of `theta0` with the standing estimate is given up.** `ability.ts` has no guessing parameter and remains a plain 1PL, so a block `theta0` and a `deriveAbilityEstimate` no longer make identical response-model assumptions. That comparability was the stated reason the floor was 0.
- **Requirements served:** R9/R10 (claim boundaries — the readout is shown about a child, and as it stood a child who learned nothing could be told their learning pace was above average), R7 (auditability — the defect and every remedy cost are reproducible as commands, not paragraphs).
- **Alternatives considered:** (a) *Correct the floor in the readout fit only* — measured, and it is close to useless: λ̄ falls only 0.0398 → 0.0338 while 48.5% of blocks become `indeterminate`, because the difficulty walk is still driven by a misspecified fit. (b) *A 3PL fit that estimates the floor* — rejected without implementing it, and stated as a judgment rather than a measurement: the floor is not identifiable from 30 dichotomous responses when `theta0` and `lambda` are already being estimated from them, and the fit is documented as running to the boundary on this block length even with two free parameters. If the owner wants this costed, the harness is where to do it. (c) *Lengthen the block* — measured; it works on an idealised grid (λ̄ = 0.0041 and r = 0.680 at 60 trials) and does not rescue the wired bank (0.0150, r = 0.398), and it spends 30 more items of a child's time. (d) *Freeze the served difficulty* — measured, and it is the only arm that returns a true zero (−0.0034 ± 0.0029), but it abandons the adaptive targeting that §4.5 relies on to keep the child near p = 0.5 where the linear climb is least wrong, and it costs 29% `indeterminate`. Worth reconsidering only as a deliberate redesign, not as a patch. (e) *Report the fitted λ with no band at all* — already the live behaviour, and it is why nothing user-facing was contaminated; the decision above is about what happens when a reference is eventually wired.
- **Evidence:** E-200 (the defect, the loop attribution, and every remedy cost). E-095 as amended 2026-07-30 (its published ladder assumed a floorless responder and halves under a real one; its reproduction path and the parameters needed to regenerate it are now recorded).
- **Rationale:** The estimator's output is a readout shown about a child. A misspecified floor inside a closed adaptive loop does not add noise that averages away — it produces a signed bias that the loop amplifies, and at 33% false `above` the readout was not weakly informative but actively wrong in a way that flatters. Part 1 removes three-quarters of it. Part 2 exists because part 1 does not remove all of it, and the honest way to carry a residual you have measured is to require it to be declared and to count it against the precision claim, not to hope it is small.
- **Consequences:** **The headline consequence is a negative result and should not be read as a fix that restores the measurement.** With the floor handled correctly and its residual declared, a 30-item block does not support a reportable learning rate on the bank the block would actually run on (94.5% `indeterminate` against the widest reference anyone has proposed). Against the SD-0.03 reference the project's own synthetic work used, nothing is reportable at any length from 30 to 60 — which was already true before this change and is now visible rather than implied. `learningRateCohortRank` remains the supported question. STAGE2_QUESTION_DESIGN §4.6's prediction that lucky successes "flatten `lambda`" is correct for the fit in isolation and wrong for the block, and §8.4's "decision on the guessing floor" is what this entry answers. Follow-up the owner may want: a per-item floor from the served option count (the option array does reach the client, so it is feasible) for mixed-format banks, and a decision on whether Gate A's A1 should be run against a five-option responder rather than the floorless default.
- **Owner:** Proposed by the exam scoring workstream (session 2026-07-30); **approved by the team lead 2026-07-30 and merged.**
- **Relationship to prior decisions:** Corrects an implementation assumption inside D-030 without changing what D-030 decided — the block is still one area, 30 items, reported as an ordinal band or not at all. It sharpens D-030's own expectation that `indeterminate` is the normal answer, and it strengthens rather than weakens D-030's refusal to put the rate in the scored decision.

### D-201 — Burst eligibility is a claim about item shape; tracked-inert metric coverage may tilt selection but not own it

- **Date:** 2026-07-30
- **Status:** **Approved** (owner sign-off 2026-07-30). It changes what content a child is served and how long a battery runs, which is session design rather than a mechanical fix, so it was held for explicit sign-off and reviewed alongside the combined measurement in D-203.
- **Decision:** Four parts, all measured in E-201 and reproducible with `pnpm exam:instruction-cost`.
  1. **The declared-process-metric disqualifier is retired from burst classification, not widened.** A type no longer becomes ineligible for bursting by declaring `M-PATH`, `M-EFF`, `M-PLANFUL` or `M-IDEAFLU`. Eligibility now rests on two per-item facts read off the bank: every item offers a bounded choice of at most `burst.maxOptions`, and no item carries a field that paces or time-bounds the response. Both are statements about the SHAPE of a response, so a burst is asserted to be a coherent experience rather than asserted to be fast.
  2. **Tracked-inert metric shortfalls are capped at `trackedCoverageCap` (one metric's worth) in a type's selection score; enforced shortfalls still sum without limit.** An enforced shortfall is a hard requirement the session cannot end without, so summing is correct. A tracked-inert metric cannot block a score, and four of them summed to a bigger margin than the age-band content preference — enough that one type per area won every selection until its counts filled.
  3. **The stop rule requires each area's evidence to span at least `minTypesPerArea` (2) question types** (`areaBreadthCovered`). Bursting can otherwise hand a whole area to one type, and it did so in the app's own battery test.
  4. **Burst length is bounded by the remaining item budget** (`roundHeadroom`), because a burst of `n` commits the session to about `4n` items under `coverageIsEven`. Without it a bursting battery could only exit on whole rounds and ran to the safety cap.
- **Requirements served:** R11 (a screener that runs at applicant scale — a child's attention is the scarce input, and a battery where every item opens a fresh instruction spends it on overhead); R9/R10 (claim boundaries — the retired rule asserted a response-time property from a telemetry declaration, and the replacement asserts only what the bank can be checked for); R7 (auditability — the whole before/after is one command).
- **Alternatives considered:** (a) *Widen the fast list and leave selection alone* — measured, and it is nearly the whole of what the task looked like it needed, and nearly worthless: 6% fewer instruction screens for an 8% longer session (arm B), then 19% (arm C). Nineteen types were already eligible and simply never selected, so the eligibility rule was not the binding constraint. (b) *Keep the process-metric signal but use it to SHORTEN a burst rather than forbid one* — rejected: the only observation bearing on that signal is the owner's session, in which all six types served declared a process measurement and were reported as answered too fast, so grading a burst down on it would dress a contradicted assumption as a measurement. (c) *Widen `evenSpreadTolerance` to the burst length* — rejected on precision grounds (four reported per-area levels would rest on evidence differing by up to six items) and then found unnecessary: once burst length is bounded by the remaining budget, arm E measures identically to arm D. (d) *Refuse to burst into an area whose estimate has already settled* — implemented, measured, and reverted: the items that land in a settled area are balance items `coverageIsEven` will insist on regardless, so delivering six as a burst costs one instruction and one at a time costs six; the bound cost one extra screen per session to save one item. (e) *`trackedCoverageCap` of 2 or 3 instead of 1* — measured: 28% and 5% fewer screens against 42%, with every session running to the 40-item cap in both, which is the documented signature of a battery that has silently become fixed-length. (f) *Raise the burst ceiling above six* — not done, and the measurement says it would backfire: longer bursts lengthen the session faster than they save instructions.
- **Evidence:** E-201 (the three independent causes, the arm-by-arm attribution, the costs, and the confirmation that no response-time data exists). `docs/product/EXAM_BURST_INSTRUCTION_COST.md` records the method, the claim boundaries, and the smallest collection that would let a speed term return. D-025 for the `ageBandBias` plateau this narrows.
- **Rationale:** The engine was being asked which types are answered quickly, and it has never had the data to answer that. The retired rule substituted a proxy — "declares a process measurement" — that is a fact about a renderer's telemetry rather than about a child, and the first live session contradicted it. Removing it rather than replacing it is the honest move while `M-RT` / `M-RTFIRST` remain uncollected, because the *benefit* of a burst does not depend on the type being fast: reusing an instruction saves the instruction either way. What a burst genuinely requires is that consecutive items be a coherent experience, and that is checkable. The coverage cap is the substance of the fix because the defect was never really about bursting: allowing a metric that cannot block a score to outrank content targeting meant the test's content was chosen by which renderer declared the most telemetry.
- **Consequences:** `packages/exam-engine/src` (`burst.ts`, `coverage.ts`, `selection.ts`, `done.ts`, `config.ts`, `types.ts`, `index.ts`) and `apps/web/src/lib/exam` (`engine-config.ts` — new, `adaptive.ts`, `bank-loader.ts`) change. Two new `EngineConfig` fields, `trackedCoverageCap` and `minTypesPerArea`, both defaulted in the engine so a caller inheriting defaults gets the fixed behaviour. **Measured effect: 26.9 → 15.6 instruction screens per session, 42% fewer, with the session no longer than before (24.0 items against 26.9) and no child reaching the safety cap.** **The costs are real and are the reverse side of the same coin:** instruction screens ARE the count of type-runs, so distinct types met necessarily falls with them — 4.3 → 3.2 types per area, 24 → 15 of 48 wired types across the cohort. Tracked process metrics accumulate more slowly; a research question needing them at volume now needs its own quota rather than a selection bias. The `ageBandBias` plateau from D-025 narrows at its upper edge (accuracy at the operating value improves, 1.20 → 0.77) and `real-bank.test.ts` now asserts the edge is soft instead of asserting it is absent. **A process consequence worth more than the fix:** the browser's engine configuration was only reachable from a module carrying browser globals, so every offline verification of routing ran against the engine's defaults and the research catalog instead — which is why bursting was demonstrated in simulation while never once firing in a browser. It now lives in `engine-config.ts`, and `apps/web/src/lib/exam/adaptive.test.ts` drives `planNextSelection` over the served index so the app's own battery test covers the loop the runner actually runs.
- **Owner:** Proposed by the exam workstream (session 2026-07-30); **approved by the team lead 2026-07-30 and merged.**
- **Relationship to prior decisions:** Narrows the plateau established by D-025 without changing what D-025 decided (the age band is priced in difficulty points, not ranked ahead of targeting); the operating value of 0.5 is unchanged and measures better than before. Independent of D-200 and D-030, which concern Phase 2 scoring rather than Phase 1 routing. Supersedes nothing.

### D-202 — The question order is drawn per session, not replayed from one constant seed

- **Date:** 2026-07-30
- **Status:** **Approved** (owner sign-off 2026-07-30). It changes which questions a child is served, which is session design rather than a mechanical fix, so it was held for explicit sign-off and reviewed alongside the combined measurement in D-203.
- **Decision:** Three parts, all measured in E-202.
  1. **The engine seed is drawn per session.** `DEFAULT_CONFIG.seed` was the constant `0xc0ffee` and nothing overrode it, so every selection draw in every sitting was identical and every child replayed one sequence. `apps/web/src/lib/exam/adaptive.ts` now draws a 32-bit seed per sitting from the platform CSPRNG. A session still replays exactly from the seed recorded in its own state, so variety is ACROSS children and determinism is WITHIN one.
  2. **Randomesque exposure control (Kingsbury & Zara) replaces the strict argmax**, for the type choice (`typeSelectionTolerance`, in selection-score points) and the item choice (`itemSelectionTolerance`, in difficulty scale points). The previous rule added a ±0.1 jitter to the score, which could never outweigh a metric weight (1 or 2) or the age bonus (1.5), so it varied nothing.
  3. **A recency discount** (`typeRecencyPenalty`, `typeRecencyWindow`) discourages returning to a type the child has just been given, fading linearly across the window.
- **Requirements served:** R11 (a screener that runs at applicant scale — a child meeting the same four games on rotation is a fixed test wearing an adaptive label); R4 (construct coverage — an estimate drawn from six of 49 wired types is an estimate of those six); R7 (auditability — selection stays fully seeded and replayable).
- **Alternatives considered:** (a) *Fix the seed alone* — measured, and it varies the order BETWEEN children while leaving within-session repetition completely unchanged (5.0 → 5.0), because the strict argmax still picks the same coverage leader every rotation. (b) *Widen the existing ±0.1 jitter* — rejected: a jitter that could change a winner would have to exceed a metric weight, at which point it is a tolerance and should be named as one and priced in the same units as the scores. (c) *An unseeded random draw* — rejected outright: a session must replay from its own recorded state or the audit trail is gone.
- **Evidence:** E-202. Two constraints found by measurement rather than reasoning, both recorded there and both pinned by tests: `itemSelectionTolerance` must stay strictly below `ageBandBias` (at 0.5 it exactly cancels D-025's age-band content preference), and the recency discount must not apply while an area is still homing (applied throughout it cost a distant-seeded child 4 items and broke D-023's convergence budget).
- **Rationale:** Adaptive selection that is deterministic in the seed is not adaptive between children; it is one fixed form administered repeatedly. The exposure-control literature's answer to exactly this — always serving the single most informative item — is to draw from the near-optimal set rather than its argmax, which is what the tolerances do, in the scores' own units so the trade is legible.
- **Consequences:** `packages/exam-engine/src` (`selection.ts`, `config.ts`, `types.ts`) and `apps/web/src/lib/exam/adaptive.ts` change. Four new `EngineConfig` fields, every one policy-configurable, and `0` in each restores the previous behaviour exactly. Variety costs targeting precision by construction: an item within `itemSelectionTolerance` of the best-targeted one is served instead of it. See D-203 for what changed when this landed beside D-201.
- **Owner:** Proposed by the exam workstream (session 2026-07-30); **approved by the team lead 2026-07-30 and merged.**
- **Relationship to prior decisions:** Bounded by D-025 (the age band is priced in difficulty points) and D-023 (the convergence budget), both of which constrain a knob here rather than being changed by it. Retuned by D-203.

### D-203 — With tracked coverage capped, the type tolerance comes down: exposure control is priced against the score spread it draws from

- **Date:** 2026-07-30
- **Status:** **Approved** (owner sign-off 2026-07-30), together with D-201 and D-202, which it prices the interaction between.
- **Decision:** `typeSelectionTolerance` moves from 1.0 to **0.5**, and the constraint is stated as a relationship rather than a number: it must stay strictly below `trackedCoverageCap`. `typeRecencyPenalty` (1.0), `typeRecencyWindow` (3) and `itemSelectionTolerance` (0.25) are unchanged, having been re-derived against the combined behaviour and found to be at their measured optimum. The homing guard from D-202 is re-pinned directly on selection rather than through D-023's battery-length budget, which stopped being a live test for it.
- **Requirements served:** R11, R4, R7 — the same requirements D-201 and D-202 each serve, since this decision only prices the interaction between them.
- **Alternatives considered:** (a) *Merge and keep 1.0* — rejected on measurement: 1.9 more items per session over 80 sittings for 0.3 of a distinct type, and it is the value at which the capped tracked signal stops deciding anything. (b) *Zero the type tolerance* — measures marginally better still in the app configuration, and is rejected because it deletes one of D-202's three parts on evidence from one configuration; under the engine defaults the tolerance buys 2.6 distinct types per session that nothing else recovers. (c) *Hold 1.0 to keep D-202's first-item-variety test passing* — rejected, and the reason is the finding: that test passed only because a tolerance of exactly 1.0 admits a rival exactly one selection point behind, which is precisely the margin between closing an enforced shortfall and closing none, i.e. the margin D-201's cap exists to keep decisive. Buying first-item variety with it was buying it out of the coverage signal. (d) *Raise `trackedCoverageCap` instead, so 1.0 stays admissible* — rejected: D-201 measured caps of 2 and 3 and both ran every session to the safety cap.
- **Evidence:** E-203 (the four-arm comparison and the knob sweep). E-201 and E-202 for the two arms being combined.
- **Rationale:** A tolerance in score points is only meaningful relative to the spread of the scores it filters. D-202 derived 1.0 against the UNCAPPED coverage sum, where a type could earn four tracked-metric points; D-201 then capped the tracked total at one point and compressed that spread, so the same number became proportionally a much wider net. At 1.0 exactly, a type carrying a tracked shortfall and a type carrying none fall within tolerance of each other — cancelling the tilt the cap was deliberately left in place to preserve — and a type closing an ENFORCED shortfall (weight 2) comes within tolerance of a rival closing none but holding the full capped tracked gain (2 − 1 = 1.0). Enforced coverage is what gates the stop rule, so a tolerance that reaches it lengthens the battery, which is what the measurement shows. Below about 1/3 the knob stops doing anything at all, because no score gap is narrower than the smallest recency step.
- **Consequences:** One value changes in `packages/exam-engine/src/config.ts`. **Measured effect on the combination, 80 sittings on the wired bank under the browser's configuration: 32.3 → 30.4 items per session, one fewer session on the 40-item safety cap, at a cost of 0.3 distinct types per session.** The combination is complementary rather than over-correcting: against `dev` it takes instruction screens 28.6 → 19.7 while recovering to 17.1 distinct types per session, where D-201 alone gives 18.5 screens but only 14.9 types; enforced metric coverage completes in 80 of 80 sittings in every arm. **The residual cost is real and belongs to the combination rather than to either part:** 15 of 80 sittings reach the safety cap against 10 for D-201 alone, so the combination gives back half of D-201's improvement in stop-rule reliability, and it is worse than D-201 alone by 1.2 instruction screens. **A pinned constraint went slack and had to be re-pinned:** D-202's homing guard was held by D-023's convergence budget, and with the coverage cap in place the worst battery runs 47 items against a budget of 48 whether the guard is present or not — so the budget test passes either way and would no longer catch its removal. Whether the discount applies while an area is homing is a property of selection, so it is now asserted on selection, and the assertion was verified to fail when the guard is removed.
- **Owner:** Proposed by the exam workstream (session 2026-07-30); **approved by the team lead 2026-07-30 and merged.**
- **Relationship to prior decisions:** Retunes D-202 against D-201. Changes neither decision's substance: the coverage cap, the burst policy, the per-session seed and the recency discount all stand as decided. D-025 and D-023 continue to bound the knobs and are unchanged.

### D-204 — Stage 1 selects on a live belief: the chance-success floor is corrected, and the item served is the one that shrinks the range fastest

- **Date:** 2026-07-31
- **Status:** **Approved** (owner sign-off 2026-07-31). It changes which questions a child is served AND the standing level every child is reported at, so it was held for explicit sign-off rather than review as a mechanical fix.
- **Decision:** Four parts, all measured in E-204.
  1. **The Phase 1 standing fit gains a chance-success floor.** `AbilityFitOptions.guessing` and `AbilityBracketing.guessing`, defaulting to 0 for a caller that omits it and to **0.2** in `DEFAULT_ABILITY_BRACKETING`, so the shipped policy path is corrected while no existing caller's numbers move without opting in. The fit assumed nobody guesses while every wired item is multiple choice.
  2. **The engine maintains a live per-area belief** (`posterior.ts`): a floor-aware one-parameter logistic on a quarter-point grid over the 1..20 scale, rebuilt from the stored trace on demand and never held in memory. This is the estimate-plus-uncertainty that selection previously did not have — the shipped policy did not compute a standard error during a session at all.
  3. **Item selection aims at the most informative item rather than the nearest one.** `selectionRule` defaults to `mepv` — minimum expected posterior variance, "whichever way they answer the range shrinks as much as possible". `mfi` (maximum Fisher information at the belief mean), `cut` (maximum information at a decision point) and `staircase` (the previous Levitt/Kesten rule, exactly) are the other supported values.
  4. **Area choice ranks on uncertainty, and type choice pays a small bonus for a task format the area has not met.** Among the areas tied for fewest items seen, the next item goes to the one whose belief is widest; `typeNoveltyBonus` is 0.5. `areaSpreadSlack` exists but ships at 0, so even spread is unchanged by construction.
- **Requirements served:** R4 (construct coverage — an area's estimate should rest on more than a few task formats), R7 (auditability — the belief is rebuilt from the persisted trace, so a session still replays exactly), R11 (a screener that runs at applicant scale), H3 (applicant experience — a child should not spend a fifth of the battery on items they cannot reach).
- **Alternatives considered:** (a) *Aim at the ends of the uncertainty interval* — rejected before implementation on the measurement already in `ITEM_SELECTION_BRACKETING_ANALYSIS.md`, and re-derived here as arithmetic in `posterior.test.ts`: a wide belief aimed at its own edges cannot shrink, because an item that far out has a nearly predetermined answer. (b) *`mfi` instead of `mepv`* — the cheap rule, and it wins the obvious metric: 6.4 items to an SE of 1.25 against 7.0, 195 of 240 areas ever reaching it against 147, mean final SE 1.14 against 1.29. Rejected because its estimates are further from the planted ability (refitted RMSE 1.74 against 1.30), so it reports ±1.14 while erring ±1.74 where `mepv` reports 1.29 and errs 1.30. An interval shown to a family has to mean what it says. The comparison is also partly circular in `mfi`'s favour and is reported as such: `mfi` maximises information at the estimate and the SE is one over the root of exactly that information, so judging the two by SE scores `mfi` on its own objective. **Retained as a policy value; the knob is the whole change if a later bank moves this.** (c) *`cut` — aim every item at a decision point* — measured and NOT adopted, because it answers a different question. Against an illustrative cut of 15 it decides more areas faster (2.3 items, 223 of 240 areas, against 3.0 and 183), which reproduces Spray and Reckase. It pays with an ability estimate worthless away from the cut (refitted RMSE 3.40 against 1.30, mean SE 3.20 against 1.29, area SE spread 4.04 against 0.53), with the child's experience (47.8% of items at least 3 points above them against 7.6%; worst run of consecutive misses 5.4 against 2.2), and with exposure control: 265 distinct bank items against 1,329. **Which of these is right depends on an unresolved product question — is Stage 1 a gate or a locator — which this decision deliberately does not settle.** (d) *Cap the enforced coverage sum so diversity can outrank it* — implemented, measured, reverted: identical results at caps of 2, 4, 6 and none, because no wired type ever earns more than one enforced gap's weight. The enforced sum was never the constraint on diversity and the knob would have been dead weight. (e) *`typeNoveltyBonus` of 1.0* — buys about 1.6 more distinct types per session and costs 0.26 of refitted RMSE, taking the interval back out of calibration. Variety is worth paying for out of slack, not out of the estimate. (f) *`areaSpreadSlack` of 1* — reverted: the portal's `evenSpreadTolerance` of 1 already clamps it to 0, so it was inert in production while changing behaviour under the engine's own defaults, where it showed up as a within-session type-repeat regression.
- **Evidence:** E-204 (`pnpm exam:selection-diversity`, 60 seeded probabilistic sittings on the real wired bank under the browser's engine configuration, four arms). `ITEM_SELECTION_BRACKETING_ANALYSIS.md` for the prior measurement of the staircase and the +1.44 bias this corrects. Birnbaum (1968) / Lord (1980) / Magis (2013) for the information optimum under a lower asymptote, asserted against the closed form in `posterior.test.ts`. Chen/Ankenmann/Chang, Choi/Swartz and van der Linden for the weak empirical case for elaborate selection criteria, which is why `mfi` was measured rather than assumed away. Spray & Reckase (1994) for cut-targeted selection, measured as arm four. **New assumptions:** the 0.2 floor, the slope of 1.0 and the prior SD of 6.0 are design choices on an uncalibrated, born-synthetic bank, not calibrated parameters.
- **Rationale:** The staircase carried one number per area and no notion of how much it knew, so it could not ask which question would tell it the most, and the guessing assumption meant it was aiming at the wrong point regardless. Correcting the floor and giving selection a belief are the same change: the belief is what makes "most informative" computable, and the floor is what makes it correct — with a floor, information peaks where the child passes about two times in three, not half the time. The two had to land together, because targeting a biased estimate more precisely converges faster on the wrong number.
- **Consequences:** `packages/exam-engine/src` (`posterior.ts` — new, `selection.ts`, `update.ts`, `config.ts`, `types.ts`, `index.ts`) and `packages/exam-scoring/src` (`ability.ts`, `policy.ts`, `scorer.ts`) change. Seven new `EngineConfig` fields and one new fit option, every one policy-configurable, with `selectionRule: 'staircase'`, `typeNoveltyBonus: 0` and `areaSpreadSlack: 0` restoring the previous behaviour exactly — verified by running the full engine suite green at those values before the defaults were changed. **Measured effect, 60 sittings: reported standing bias +1.58 → +0.02 and RMSE 2.35 → 1.25; items at least 3 points above the child 21.4% → 7.6%; worst run of consecutive misses 4.1 → 2.2; sessions concluding on evidence 52/60 → 58/60, with safety-cap hits 12 → 2; distinct types per session 17.6 → 18.0.** **The costs are real.** Precision per item is slightly worse, not better: mean final SE 1.235 → 1.286 and items to an SE of 1.25 rise 6.6 → 7.0, because the corrected aim deliberately serves items easier than the child, where a floor-aware model says they are most informative. The gain is in accuracy, not in speed to a stated precision. **Every standing level previously produced or shown to anyone moves DOWN, by roughly 1.5 scale points for a typical child** and further for a low-ability child seeded high, because the Phase 1 fit had assumed nobody guesses while every wired item is five-option. Any standing level demonstrated, exported or communicated before 2026-07-31 is inflated by about that much. **This is a correction and not a regression** — the old numbers were wrong in a flattering direction and the new ones are not — but **standing levels produced before this date and after it are NOT COMPARABLE.** They must not be pooled, trended against each other, or read as a child's level having fallen; a drop of about 1.5 points across this date is the correction landing, not a change in the child. **The floor is an assumption that is harmful when wrong in either direction**, which is why it is pinned in both: applied to a child who does not guess it attenuates them (`ability.test.ts`), and for a fixed trace it can only move an estimate down, since a miss carries identical information under any floor while a pass carries less. **Two tests changed meaning and neither was deleted:** the degenerate-responder assertion no longer requires the estimate to land exactly on a scale bound, because a belief with a proper prior cannot honestly claim a bound the bank contains no evidence for; and `learning-block.test.ts`'s standing-phase confound guard is pinned to `staircase`, because under `mepv` the search reaches the child inside the first third of the trace and the ceiling no longer climbs measurably across thirds — the confound is reduced, not gone, and re-measuring it is Stage 2's.
- **Owner:** Proposed by the exam workstream (session 2026-07-31); **approved by the team lead 2026-07-31 and merged.**
- **Relationship to prior decisions:** Bounded by D-025, whose bargain is carried onto a variance objective rather than changed — the age band is applied as a preference inside `mepvTolerance`, so it decides between items that teach the same amount and cannot buy a badly-aimed one; `ageBandBias` and `itemSelectionTolerance` continue to govern the `staircase` path unchanged. D-023's homing guard on the recency discount is untouched and still asserted. D-201's coverage cap and D-203's type tolerance are unchanged; the enforced sum D-201 deliberately left free was measured here and found not to be the binding constraint on diversity. Corrects in Phase 1 the same misspecification D-200 corrected in the Phase 2 learning-curve fit, which had been anchored on an uncorrected Phase 1 handover. Supersedes nothing. The gate-or-locator question alternative (c) deliberately left open is settled by D-205.

### D-205 — Stage 1 is a locator, not a gate: its objective is an ability estimate per reasoning area, because Stage 2 is scaled from it

- **Date:** 2026-07-31
- **Status:** **Approved** (owner decision 2026-07-31). It settles the product question D-204 deliberately left open, and what Stage 1 is FOR is a product direction rather than an implementation choice, so it is the owner's to make and not an implementer's.
- **Decision:** **Stage 1's objective is to estimate ability per reasoning area — a locator, whose per-area standing estimate scales Stage 2 — and not to classify each area against a decision threshold.** Selection therefore optimises the precision of that estimate across the whole scale rather than the speed of an above/below call at a point: `selectionRule` stays `mepv` and `decisionCut` stays `null` in `DEFAULT_CONFIG`. **`cut` selection and `decisionCut` are retained as policy values rather than deleted**, so running Stage 1 as a gate later is a configuration change against a measured price, not a rebuild. Nothing about the screener's own admit / defer / retry cut is decided here: a cut applied to a finished per-area profile is a different thing from aiming each item at a cut while the estimate is still forming, and R11 keeps that cut GT's to set and tune.
- **Requirements served:** R11 (a scalable, tunable, GT-owned instrument — R11's acceptance evidence requires that the product "does not lock a final cut on GT's behalf", and no real GT threshold exists in this repository, so a selection rule aimed at a cut would aim every item a child sees at a number this project has no authority to set). R5 (a defensible capability-and-fit standard that "accounts for measurement error and boundary cases", and whose GT-data validation under RES-012 is a tunable-cut analysis over a continuous per-area estimate — a binary call taken at one illustrative threshold cannot be re-cut afterwards). R10 (claim boundaries — "whether selection is reliable" is a milestone of its own, and an interval that is only honest near a cut cannot support a reliability claim anywhere else on the scale). R7 (auditability — the objective is recorded rather than left implied by a default, and the rejected alternative stays inspectable and priced rather than deleted). H6 (design for enough statistical information — this decision is about WHERE the instrument spends the information it collects). R8's avoidable-harm clause (a battery aimed at a threshold rather than at the child serves 47.8% of its items at least 3 scale points above them).
- **Alternatives considered:** (a) *Stage 1 as a gate — `cut` selection against a decision threshold.* Implemented and measured as arm four of E-204, and it genuinely wins the thing it optimises: against an illustrative cut of 15 it reaches a 95% above/below call in **2.3 items across 223 of 240 areas**, where the adopted `mepv` needs **3.0 items and reaches it in 183** — a reproduction of Spray and Reckase (1994) rather than a surprise. Rejected on three measured costs, of which the first is decisive: the ability estimate is precise only near the cut (**refitted RMSE 3.40 against `mepv`'s 1.30**, mean SE 3.20 against 1.29, per-area SE spread 4.04 against 0.53); exposure control collapses from **1,329 distinct bank items to 265**, with the most-served item reaching **28% of sittings** against 13%, because every child starts at the same threshold and therefore meets the same items; and the child meets a battery pitched at the threshold instead of at them (47.8% of items at least 3 points above them against 7.6%, worst run of consecutive misses 5.4 against 2.2). (b) *Both — a cut-targeted screen first, then a locating pass.* Rejected by reasoning from E-204 rather than by a new measurement, and labelled as such: Stage 2 is scaled from the standing estimate for **every** area, not only for the areas near a threshold, so a gate pass would have to be followed by a locating pass everywhere and would cost strictly more of the child's time than locating once. The gate's 2.3 items are a saving only if the classification is the output. (c) *Adopt a cut now and choose the threshold here.* Refused as outside this project's authority, which `AGENTS.md` makes a stop condition: a selection threshold is a district fact with a named owner and consequences for real children. The 15 used in E-204 is illustrative, and arm four's figures must never be read as how a real gate would perform. (d) *Delete `cut` and `decisionCut`, since they are not the chosen objective.* Rejected: retention costs one enum value and one `null` field, both already measured and pinned by tests, and it is exactly what keeps this decision cheap to revisit. This log has twice refused to ship a knob that does nothing (D-204's alternatives (d) and (f)); a knob that names a deliberately rejected objective and carries its measured price is a different thing, and is the record of the trade.
- **Evidence:** E-204, arm four (the gate) against the shipped arm (the locator). **No new measurement was taken for this decision and none was needed:** PR #39 implemented both objectives as policy values and measured them side by side under matched settings, which is what makes this a choice between priced options rather than an assertion. `docs/architecture/ITEM_SELECTION_BRACKETING_ANALYSIS.md` §3.2 for the three jobs the Stage 1 number does at once (selection target, stop-rule input, reported standing level) and §3.3 for the handover — `nextBlockTarget` aims the block at `standing + LEARNING_BLOCK_TARGET_OFFSET` and the whole Phase 2 handoff is rebuilt from `standing`. Spray and Reckase (1994) for the cut-targeted result arm four reproduces. **Assumptions:** none new. E-204's own stand — the 0.2 floor, the slope of 1.0 and the prior SD of 6.0 are design choices on an uncalibrated, born-synthetic bank — and every figure quoted here is recovery under a simulated responder, not evidence about score validity, reliability or fairness.
- **Rationale:** **Stage 2 is scaled from the Stage 1 standing estimate, so an estimate that is accurate only near a cut cannot do Stage 1's job.** That is the decisive argument and the rest is support. `nextBlockTarget` pitches the learning block at `standing + 1` and the block's whole handoff is rebuilt from `standing` alone, for every area and every child, whether or not that child is anywhere near a threshold. Under `cut` selection the standing estimate carries a refitted RMSE of 3.40, so a gate-targeted Stage 1 would hand Phase 2 a starting point wrong by about three scale points for a child away from the cut — and would hand it into the one place in this system least able to absorb it, because Phase 2's fit → aim → fit loop is the closed loop E-200 caught manufacturing a learning rate for children who learned nothing, and a closed loop amplifies a biased handover rather than averaging it away. The gate's advantage is real and is denominated in a currency this product is not buying: it decides faster, which is worth having when the decision at a point IS the output. Stage 1's output is a per-area profile that something downstream is scaled from, and a number trustworthy only at 15 cannot be scaled from. The other two costs point the same way and neither is load-bearing alone: serving 265 of the bank's items instead of 1,329 because every child begins in the same place is an item-security problem at applicant volume rather than a psychometric one, and it is the reverse of what R11's scale clause needs.
- **Consequences:** **No code changes.** The shipped defaults already implement the locator; what changes is that `selectionRule: 'mepv'` and `decisionCut: null` are now a recorded product objective rather than a default nobody had chosen, and D-204's open question is closed. **What this forecloses, stated plainly: the shorter test.** Cut-targeted selection reaches a defensible above/below call in about 2.3 items per area against 3.0, across 223 of 240 areas against 183, and this decision gives that up deliberately — Stage 1 will ask more questions per area than a gate would need, and its stop rule will keep going after a gate would have been satisfied. **If the programme later needs a fast pass/fail screen rather than a per-area profile — an applicant volume it cannot afford to locate one child at a time, or a downstream design that no longer scales Stage 2 from the standing estimate — this decision has to be revisited rather than worked around.** The revision is `selectionRule: 'cut'` plus a `decisionCut` set by someone with the authority to set a threshold, and it arrives with arm four's costs attached: the estimate stops being usable away from the cut, and exposure control drops to about a fifth of the bank. **What this does NOT decide:** which statistic the scorer brackets on is still D-024's open question, unchanged and still awaiting the owner; the screener-level admit / defer / retry cut (AX-06) stays tunable and GT's to own; and nothing here makes a born-synthetic estimate valid, so R10's ladder from "can be operated" to "caused the change" is untouched.
- **Owner:** **Team lead**, deciding 2026-07-31 on the measurement the exam workstream produced in E-204 the same day. This entry records a decision already taken; it is not a proposal awaiting sign-off.
- **Relationship to prior decisions:** Settles the product question D-204 explicitly declined to settle — its alternative (c) says "which of these is right depends on an unresolved product question — is Stage 1 a gate or a locator — which this decision deliberately does not settle" — and changes nothing D-204 decided: the chance-success floor, the live per-area belief, `mepv`, the uncertainty-ranked area choice and the type-novelty bonus all stand exactly as ratified. Makes explicit an assumption D-030 and D-200 were already resting on, that the standing level handed to the learning block is accurate for the child rather than at a threshold, without changing what either decided. Leaves D-024 open. Does not touch R11's tunable cut or D-016's screener scope. Supersedes nothing.
### D-400 — The screener is the binding aim; program-effect demonstration becomes non-binding and is pursued through the screener

- **Date:** 2026-07-30
- **Status:** Approved
- **Decision:** Rescope the capstone from two co-equal aims to one binding aim with a non-binding second. Three parts, which the owner took together. (1) **The screener is primary.** The core success statement and the charter mission are rewritten so the binding aim is a scalable, tunable, GT-validated screening instrument (`R11`); the capstone is not complete without it. (2) **Program-effect demonstration becomes non-binding, and is pursued through the screener rather than in parallel.** `R2` (credible counterfactual), `R3` (prospective causal question), and `R6` (growth measurement without a ceiling) move out of the Required tier into a new, explicitly-labelled **conditionally-required** tier in `docs/product/project-requirements.md`. ### D-206 — The block's targeting rule aims at the child's fitted level corrected by observed accuracy, not at an extrapolation of the fitted learning rate

- **Date:** 2026-08-01 (part 2 added the same day, before sign-off, at owner instruction — see Status)
- **Status:** **Proposed**, awaiting owner sign-off. This changes statistical scoring behaviour and it changes which items a child is served, which `AGENTS.md` puts behind the full-mode bar and which D-201 and D-202 both establish is held for explicit sign-off rather than merged as a mechanical fix. It is written to be ratified or rejected in the same way D-200 was. **Part 2 was added while this entry was still Proposed**, on the owner's instruction to address the fast-learner cost recorded under Consequences (3) rather than accept it; the entry is amended rather than superseded because both parts are one targeting rule and neither has shipped.
- **Decision, part 1 — the anchor.** **`nextTargetTheta` chooses the next difficulty from a fit with no climb term — `estimateBlockLevel`, the posterior-mode ability under `theta(t) = theta`, plus the desirable-difficulty offset — instead of from `theta0 + lambda * t`, the ability projected for the next trial.** `estimateLearningCurve` is unchanged: the reported statistic, its priors, its floor and its numerics are byte-identical, so every stored trace re-scores to exactly the value it scored to before. What changes is the design, not the estimator. `estimateBlockLevel` is a new export using the same response model, floor, scale and `theta0` prior as the curve fit, solved by fixed-step bisection so a stored trace re-fits bit-for-bit.
- **Decision, part 2 — the correction.** **The aim is then corrected by the gap between OBSERVED accuracy over the last eight trials and the accuracy `targetOffset` is designed to produce**, both put through the same floored logistic and differenced on the logit scale, so the correction is in scale points with no gain constant to choose. Part 1 alone lags a climbing child by about half the block and under-serves the fastest learners badly; the correction closes that. **It is symmetric — it lowers the aim as readily as it raises it — and that is load-bearing rather than tidy.** A rectified version that only pushes forward was measured and rejected: under the null it turns response noise into a one-way ratchet. **The driver is deliberately not the fitted `lambda`, nor anything derived from it.** An offset scaling with the estimated slope would reinstate part 1's defect exactly, aiming higher precisely when the estimate is optimistic; an offset driven by accuracy is the opposite sign of feedback, because aiming too high lowers accuracy, which retracts the correction. The only constant introduced is the eight-trial window, and both 8 and 12 were measured with the same null result.
- **Requirements served:** R9/R10 (claim boundaries — the readout is shown about a child, and the pipeline was fitting a positive learning rate for a child who learned nothing on every bank it was tried on). R6 (measure growth without a gifted-student ceiling — this is the readout that requirement rests on, and it now recovers a real climb better than it did). R7 (auditability — the defect, its mechanism and every remedy cost are reproducible as commands: `bash scripts/stage2-lambda-loop-runs.sh`). H6 (design for enough statistical information — the change improves recovery and narrows the posterior rather than trading one for the other).
- **Alternatives considered:** Six were costed on the same cohorts at matched seeds before this one was chosen; the null column and the recovery column are always reported together, because a remedy that zeroes the null by making the estimator insensitive looks perfect in the first and is useless.
  1. *Do nothing and keep declaring the residual as a `contaminationFloor` (D-200 part 2).* This is the status quo and it is defensible only while the residual is believed irreducible. It is not: the measurement below shows it is a property of one line of the targeting rule.
  2. *Freeze the served difficulty (D-200's alternative (d), reconsidered).* Genuinely fixes the null — λ̄ = −0.0009 ± 0.0004 on `FLU-OPCHAIN-01` — and, contrary to D-200's expectation, does not cost recovery (r = 0.374 against the projecting rule's 0.332). Rejected because it costs precision where it matters: mean posterior SE 0.070 against 0.060, which takes the `indeterminate` rate against the widest reference from 5.8% to 28.0%. A child whose handover was 1.5 points off spends the whole block at a badly-aimed difficulty, and no adaptation ever corrects it. Its value is as the **open-loop bound** every other arm is judged against, not as the remedy.
  3. *Aim at `theta0` from the two-parameter fit — the intercept, dropping only the `* t`.* Measured, and it OVERCORRECTS to λ̄ = −0.0104 ± 0.0007, 15.8 SEs the other side of zero. `theta0` is the child's ability at trial 0, so a design aimed at it systematically under-serves anyone who moved. The lesson is that the level the design tracks has to be a level estimate, not a by-product of a slope estimate.
  4. *Settle for the first 10 trials then freeze.* Measured at 6, 10 and 15 trials of settling: λ̄ = −0.0073, −0.0083, −0.0077. All overcorrect, all introduce a settling-length constant nobody can justify, and none beats (5) on recovery.
  5. *A trailing-window level fit (last 10 or 15 trials) instead of all trials.* Measured: window 10 gives λ̄ = 0.0027 and r = 0.347, window 15 gives 0.0011 and 0.369, against the adopted all-trials fit's −0.0007 and 0.365. Window 15 is a near-tie on the numbers and is rejected on the tie-break that matters here — it adds a window length that would have to be chosen, and the choosing is exactly the "tune a constant until the number looks right" this decision is trying not to do.
  6. *Leave the design alone and bias-correct the estimator instead (delete-one jackknife on `lambda`).* Measured, because the artifact does decay with block length (λ̄ = 0.0117, 0.0085, 0.0070, 0.0047, 0.0034 at 15, 30, 45, 60 and 120 trials on the grid), which is what makes a finite-sample bias correction plausible. It does not work: on the projecting loop the jackknife makes the null WORSE (0.0122) and costs recovery (r 0.316 against 0.334), because it corrects a smooth small-sample bias and this is a design-induced one. On the level loop it is neutral-to-worse (−0.0038, r 0.349 against 0.365) — a 30-fold cost in compute for nothing. Rejected on measurement.

- **Alternatives considered for part 2:** Seven forward-offset rules were costed on all four banks and the bank-free grid at matched seeds, against part 1 alone as the baseline, and the exchange rate — null contamination bought per scale point of fast-learner headroom — is the column that decided it. Full table in E-206.

Being exogenous does not protect a rule that parks a static child two to three points above themselves. **Cost per point: 0.0143, the worst of the seven.**

  11. *The adopted correction, but rectified — push forward only, never pull back.* Measured because "only ever push fast learners" is the literal reading of the instruction. It costs: a static child's mean served difficulty rises 1.26 → 1.49 (the adopted symmetric form takes it DOWN to 1.10), `indeterminate` goes 6.3% → 11.1% and 12.2% → 22.1% on `VER-MORPHO-01`, recovery falls on every pool, and the feedback term grows by half. The rectifier converts response noise into a one-way ratchet. Rejected, and the symmetric form adopted in its place.
  12. *Accept the under-serving and change nothing.* This was a live option and the owner asked for it to stay live. It is rejected only because the adopted correction turned out to buy the headroom at no measurable price — had the exchange rate been anything like alternatives 7–11, accepting the lag would have been the right answer, and that is recorded so the reasoning is not mistaken for a preference for action.
  - **Part 2 introduces one constant, and it is disclosed rather than hidden:** the eight-trial accuracy window. It is not tuned against the null — 8 and 12 return λ̄ = −0.0005 and −0.0005 on `FLU-OPCHAIN-01`, indistinguishable — and it is chosen on the fast-learner column, where 8 recovers more of the lag. The continuity correction that bounds the inversion at an all-correct or all-incorrect window is standard rather than chosen.

- **Rationale:** **The defect was a feedback loop from the estimate of `lambda` onto the design that identifies `lambda`, and it is not the same thing as adaptivity.** Three controls separate them, and all three are measurements rather than arguments. (a) Replaying a *twin's* difficulty sequence — the same child, the same standing, an independent response stream — reproduces the rising ladder in full (drift +0.0176 scale points per trial against the shipped +0.0173) and returns λ̄ = −0.0034. So a rising served difficulty is not what the fit misreads; the ladder's SHAPE is innocent. (b) A design that follows the child's TRUE `lambda` returns −0.0039. So following a slope is not the defect either; following the slope *being estimated* is. (c) The artifact scales with the guessing floor even when the floor is CORRECTLY specified in both roles — λ̄ = 0.0014 at `c = 0`, 0.0037 at 0.1, 0.0073 at 0.2, 0.0152 at 0.33, 0.0290 at 0.5 — and opening the loop returns every one of them to zero. **Why the floor is required for the loop to bite** is the part worth writing down: a floored logistic carries about `4d^2` of information on an item `d` above the child and about `(1 - c) d` on one equally far below, so mis-aiming UPWARD destroys information several times faster than mis-aiming downward. A spuriously positive `lambda` aims the next items above the child, and the later trials that would contradict it therefore arrive down-weighted to nearly nothing, while a spuriously negative one aims low and is corrected at full weight. The loop has an asymmetric restoring force, and its resting point is above zero. That is why the sign surprised everyone, why it was invisible against the floorless simulated responder every pre-E-200 test used, and why D-200's floor correction reduced it without removing it: the floor sets the size of the asymmetry, and the loop is what turns an asymmetry into a bias.

### D-207 — §9.4's A1 stop rule is narrowed: an A1 failure indicts a bank only when it is worse than the bank-free bound

- **Date:** 2026-08-01
- **Status:** **Approved** (owner decision 2026-08-01). Whether a gate stops a track is a process rule the owner owns; this entry records a decision already taken and states the reasoning that supports it.
- **Decision:** **`STAGE2_QUESTION_DESIGN` §9.4's first stop rule — "A1 failure in U5 stops that type's track at U5" — is superseded by a narrowed trigger: a type's track stops when its null-cohort λ̄ is worse than the same measurement on the bank-free idealised grid, at matched seeds and at the bank's own option count.** An A1 failure at or below the bank-free bound is recorded, reported, and does not stop the track. The comparison is a command rather than a judgment: `pnpm exam:block-harness -- --gate-a --bank grid` now runs Gate A with no bank in it, and `--seeds` runs both sides at matched seeds. §4.1.2's claim that A1 "is a real, decisive test of the bank and its stated difficulties" is corrected in the same edit, because it is the sentence the old rule rested on.
- **Requirements served:** R7 (auditability and falsifiability — a stop rule whose stated premise has been falsified by the project's own measurement must be reopened rather than followed). R6 (the growth readout Stage 2 exists to produce — the old rule would have stopped all four type tracks for a defect none of the banks caused). H6 (design for enough statistical information — Gate A's job is to catch a bank that cannot support the estimator, and a trigger that also fires on estimator defects cannot do that job).
- **Alternatives considered:** (a) *Keep the rule as written.* Rejected on the measurement below, and it is worth being concrete about the cost: applied literally on 2026-08-01 it would have stopped `FLU-OPCHAIN-01`, `SPA-XFORM-01`, `QUANT-GLYPHNUM-01` and `VER-MORPHO-01` — every Stage 2 type in the plan — for an artifact that was located in `nextTargetTheta` and removed without changing a single bank item. (b) *Delete the stop rule.* Rejected. A bank whose stated difficulties correlate with trial index is the §1.1(d) failure the gate was built for, it is real, and it genuinely cannot be fixed downstream; removing the rule would remove the only cheap check that catches it before a renderer is written. (c) *Replace the absolute condition with a looser absolute one — say `|λ̄| ≤ 0.02`.* Rejected as the same mistake in a different costume: any absolute bound is a number chosen to admit the banks currently in hand, and it would go stale the moment the estimator changed again. The bank-free grid is a bound the project measures rather than picks. (d) *Require the bank to BEAT the grid rather than merely match it.* Rejected as unmeetable and pointless — the grid is 12 items per 0.5-point rung across the whole scale with no generator constraints, so it is an upper bound on what a bank can be, and a rule no artifact can satisfy is not a gate. (e) *Fire on A1 but route the failure to a review rather than a stop.* Effectively what the narrowed trigger does, stated less precisely; the version above is preferred because it names the comparison that decides.
- **Evidence:** `STAGE2_BANK_RECOVERY_MEASUREMENT.md` §1, §3.1 and §10 for the falsification — A1 failing at λ̄ = 0.0085 on an idealised grid containing no bank at all, at 0.0097 on `FLU-OPCHAIN-01`, with the paired between-pool contrast at 30 trials −0.002 ± 0.006 — and E-205 for the confirmation from the other direction: the same failure removed downstream, with every bank byte-identical.
- **Rationale:** **The rule's premise was "a bank that manufactures λ from a static child cannot be fixed downstream", and that premise is false as stated.** It conflates two claims. The true one is that *a bank whose own difficulty grid manufactures λ* cannot be fixed downstream — nothing outside the bank can un-correlate its difficulties from trial index. The false one is that *any* A1 failure identifies such a bank. A1 measures the whole administration path, so an estimator defect, a targeting defect and a bank defect all fail it identically, and the measured evidence is that the failure it was firing on came from the targeting rule: three banks in three domains built by three different agents returned the same value as a pool containing no items, paired contrasts between them were null, and the artifact then vanished on all four when one line of `nextTargetTheta` changed. The narrowed trigger is the original argument stated at the strength the evidence supports — an excess a bank is responsible for is an excess a different bank could remove — and it keeps the check that matters while removing the one that would have burned the project's remaining time on the wrong object.
- **Consequences:** §9.4's stop rule (1), §4.1.2's characterisation of A1, and §9.2's U5 acceptance row are amended in `docs/product/STAGE2_QUESTION_DESIGN.md`, with the superseded text preserved in the note rather than deleted. `scripts/exam-learning-block-harness.ts` no longer prints "a bank that manufactures λ from a static child cannot be fixed downstream" on an A1 failure; it prints the comparison the narrowed rule requires. A1 still fails the run's exit code, and a bank-attributable A1 failure still stops a track. **What this does not relax:** stop rule (2) is unchanged — types 2–4 still do not start until `FLU-OPCHAIN-01` reaches U8 and do not start at all on a Verdict 3 or 4 from Gate B — and D-S2-3's Gate B, the gate that actually decides whether a type ships, is untouched. **A standing exposure this creates:** the narrowed trigger no longer catches a defect that is common to the estimator AND to every bank, because the grid would carry it too. That is the correct trade for a rule about banks, and the residual −0.001 to −0.003 recorded in D-206 is exactly such a defect: it is at the bank-free bound, it is real, and nothing in Gate A will ever flag it. It is carried by `contaminationFloor` instead.
- **Owner:** **Team lead**, deciding 2026-08-01. The exam scoring workstream supplied the falsification and drafted the replacement trigger; the choice to narrow rather than keep or delete is the owner's.
- **Relationship to prior decisions:** Amends the D-S2-3 build plan's stop rule without touching what D-S2-3 decided — the scrambled-system control is still a binding acceptance criterion and Gate B is still the ship gate. Depends on D-206 for the fix but is deliberately a separate entry: D-206 changes scoring behaviour and awaits sign-off, while this narrows a process rule and is decided; if D-206 were rejected, the falsification recorded here would still stand, because it rests on the bank-free grid measurement rather than on the fix. Supersedes no decision-log entry — the rule it narrows lives in the design document, not in this log.

### D-209 — A Stage 2 trial is reviewable and self-paced: it shows what the machine made and what the child picked, the child ends it, and the time that takes is recorded but never scored

- **Date:** 2026-08-03
- **Status:** Proposed by the exam workstream (session 2026-08-03). **Awaiting team-lead sign-off**, because it reverses a constraint the owner set.
- **Decision:** **Two changes to every Stage 2 learning-block trial, both implementing `STAGE2_REDESIGN_SPEC` §4.2 and §4.3.** (1) **The fixed post-commit hold is replaced by a Next control in Stage 2.** The reveal is shown and the trial stays on screen until the child chooses to continue; nothing else ends it, and time-to-next is recorded per trial in a ledger no estimator can read. (2) **The trial becomes reviewable in colour.** It shows the option the mechanism resolved to AND the option the child picked, distinguished on four channels — colour, shape, position and border style. **This deliberately reverses the outcome-free property `STAGE2_QUESTION_DESIGN` §1.5 imposed and `stage2-reveal.test.ts` asserted:** the rendered trial now differs between a child who picked the machine's option and one who did not. Two things do not change, and they are what the reversal is bounded by. The reveal PAYLOAD stays byte-identical across outcomes, so the browser is still never told; and the reveal still says nothing about the child — no verdict word, no tick, no cross, no good/bad colour, no tally, no streak. Phase 1 is untouched and keeps its fixed hold.
- **Requirements served:** R11 (the screener instrument — Stage 2 measures a hidden system induced across non-repeating trials, and a trial a child cannot see the outcome of gives them nothing to induce from, so the construct is not being measured). R9 (protect students and families — a fixed hold sets a tempo the test never told the child about and hides the one thing they were meant to look at; a self-paced advance and a WCAG-audited, not-colour-alone presentation are accessibility and dignity properties, not polish). R7 (auditability — the departure is recorded here rather than made silently in a renderer, and the replaced assertions are named). R10 (boundaries — time-to-next is recorded as an unscored process signal and is not reported to anyone as a finding about a child). H10 (minimise gaming and burden — the leak surface is unchanged, and the burden change is measured rather than assumed). H1, H9.
- **Alternatives considered:** (a) *Keep the outcome-free reveal.* Rejected on the construct: §1.5's own text concedes the block has to give the child something to learn FROM, and a reveal that is identical whatever they did tells them what the mechanism made without letting them see how that related to their answer — which is the one comparison induction across trials requires. Category 7.8 of the test-structure BrainLift is the reason §1.5 was written, and it is about feedback that points at the PERSON; it does not support withholding the work. (b) *Show the outcome in words — "the machine made C, you picked A".* Rejected: a sentence naming both is a verdict a child reads in one step, and it puts the comparison in the copy rather than leaving it to them. Colour and position state the two facts without ranking them. (c) *Keep a fixed hold but make it longer.* Rejected — any fixed number is a guess at how long a child needs, and the child is the only party who knows. It also does not solve the real problem, which is that a hold is a tempo the test imposes silently. (d) *Advance on any input (tap anywhere, keypress).* Rejected as indistinguishable from an accident; a named control the child aims at is what makes the advance a choice. (e) *Score the dwell, or feed it to item selection.* Rejected on SPOV 3 of the test-structure BrainLift: effort telemetry validates and filters, it never gates. A child who thinks longer would be charged for thinking, on an instrument that has never measured what dwell means. (f) *Do not record the dwell at all.* Rejected — §7.4 already lists the session budget as obsolete and open, and it cannot be re-estimated from a design document. (g) *Distinguish the two marks by colour only, using the existing navy/blue pair.* Rejected on measurement: those two differ by 1.69:1 in luminance, and the pair now shipped needed a second and third channel regardless, because colour alone is not a channel (WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.1).

The re-keying and leak measurement in D-206 / PR #51 and `STAGE2_ANTILEAK_COMPARISON.md` for why the payload's outcome-independence is kept rather than relaxed: a richer reveal makes a block more attackable as well as more learnable, and per-session keying — not a thinner reveal — is what prices that.
- **Rationale:** §1.5 answered a real question with a stronger rule than the question needed. The failure it was built on is feedback ABOUT THE CHILD, which is what the cited evidence measures; it generalised that to feedback about the OUTCOME, which the evidence does not reach. Separating the two is what this decision does: the trial now says what happened and still says nothing about who it happened to, which is the distinction Category 7.8 actually draws. The self-paced advance follows from the same reasoning — a hold that hides the outcome after 1.6 seconds is the design deciding how long the evidence is worth looking at, for a child it has never met.
- **Consequences:** `apps/web/src/lib/exam/reveal.ts` is unchanged, which is the point: the payload and its leak surface are exactly as before. The Next control, the gate and the ledger are new (`apps/web/src/lib/exam/review-dwell.ts`, `exam-runner.tsx`); the review palette is one shared block in `exam-skin.css` rather than four copies; the four Stage 2 renderers change only in that a second review mark can now coexist with the first. **Two `stage2-reveal.test.ts` suites are replaced, not deleted, and both replacements are stricter in the direction that still matters:** the suite that required an outcome-independent HOLD is replaced by one requiring that what ends a trial branch on the phase and never on the outcome, that Stage 2 have no timer at all, and that the only release paths be the child's control and the researcher's emulator; the suite that required the RENDERED reveal to be identical across outcomes is replaced by one requiring the machine's own statement to be identical across outcomes, the trial as a whole to differ (asserted positively, so a silent collapse back fails), both marks to survive on the tile that carries both, and no evaluative language or running tally in either rendering. **What this creates and does not solve:** the session budget is now unbounded by construction. The ledger measures it; nothing yet acts on it, and §7.4's re-estimate remains open and now has an instrument. **What it does not do:** it does not make a learning rate reportable, it does not change grading, generators, banks or checkers, and it does not touch Phase 1.
- **Owner:** **Team lead.** Reversing an owner-set constraint on what a child may be shown is the owner's call; the workstream supplies the construct argument, the contrast measurements and the replacement assertions.
- **Relationship to prior decisions:** **Amends `STAGE2_QUESTION_DESIGN` §1.5, which lives in the design document rather than in this log, so it supersedes no entry here.** Depends on nothing in D-200/D-206/D-207 and changes nothing they decided — the estimator, the floor and the Gate A trigger are untouched, and the review-dwell boundary is specifically what keeps this change out of their scope. D-017's text-only constraint is unaffected. ### D-210 — A Stage 2 bank record becomes a template, and the server materialises the item: options, key and difficulty are all session facts

- **Date:** 2026-08-03
- **Status:** **Proposed.** Implemented behind `EXAM_SERVE_TIME_MATERIALISATION`, off by default, on `FLU-OPCHAIN-01` only. Needs owner sign-off on three things named under *What this asks the owner to decide*.
- **Decision:** **`FLU-OPCHAIN-01`'s bank record becomes a TEMPLATE and the finished item is materialised at serve time.** Four parts.
  1. **The template contract.** A record carries the input state; the chain as references to symbol **slots** rather than to meanings; **distractor rationales** — how to construct each wrong option, as transformations of the chain ("apply the chain but drop step 2", "apply the steps in reverse order"), in a fixed priority order; and the structural facts that price difficulty. It carries **no key, no difficulty, and no name of any meaning.** The contract is defined once for all four Stage 2 types in `research/exam-question-types/stage2-item-template.mjs`.
  2. **Options are materialised under the drawn mapping.** The server resolves the chain's slots to operators, computes the key, then builds the option slate from the rationales under that same mapping, taking the first four that give distinct, relabelling-reachable, non-key figures. The correct answer is therefore on screen **by construction**, at every chain depth, under every mapping.
  3. **Difficulty is computed per session from the four levers in `STAGE2_REDESIGN_SPEC` §3** — chain length, vocabulary in play, evidence so far, and residual ambiguity — and **recorded** on the served item. Residual ambiguity is computed by the PR #48 learnability oracles, imported and used as the difficulty function rather than re-implemented.
  4. **Determinism moves from "the bank is fixed" to "the session seed is fixed."** The seed is `HMAC(server secret, browser-visible sessionId)`, so the mapping is not derivable from anything the browser holds; the served options, key slot, option order and difficulty are written to the append-only trace per trial; and a replay from the seed reproduces all of them exactly.
- **Requirements served:** R7 (auditable and falsifiable — determinism has moved, so the record and the reconstruction are both defined and are checked against each other rather than assumed equal). R5/R6 (a bank whose correct answer is off screen on four fifths of mappings cannot measure growth; it measures whether the child was lucky). R8, R10 (every figure below is one command, and the costs are stated as plainly as the gains). H1, H6, H10.
- **Alternatives considered:** (a) *Keep baked options and re-key anyway.* Rejected on measurement: admissibility falls to 18.4% of mappings at chain depth 4, and depth is the ladder. This is the option D-206 already found unshippable. (b) *Restrict the mapping family to class-preserving relabellings,* which holds the shipped `geom` difficulty exactly fixed. Measured and rejected in D-206: 36 systems rather than 720, mean 2.11 reachable options, and a brute-forcing client scores 57.5% against a 20% floor. (c) *Keep the shipped difficulty model and recompute the rung per session.* Rejected in D-206: it removes the drift, does not restore the ladder, and opens a channel — on 45.3% of served items the served pair (chain length, recomputed difficulty) admits only one `geom` value. Under this decision no `geom` value is priced at all, so that channel has nothing to leak. (d) *Store the options for every mapping.* 720 slates per item; rejected as a bank the size of the mapping space, which also puts every possible key in one file. (e) *Move item selection server-side instead of re-fetching the index.* Viable and deliberately not taken here — it is a larger change to the engine boundary than the one property this branch exists to prove, and it can be taken later without changing the template contract. (f) *Price difficulty on the structural levers alone, leaving evidence and residual ambiguity out.* Rejected because it is not §3, and because the resulting model cannot say that a depth-2 item is easy for a child who has resolved both its symbols — which is the thing an adaptive block wants to target. The cost of not taking it is recorded below and is real.
- **Evidence:** **E-208** for the whole measurement. Relies on E-205 and D-206 for the failure being fixed, on E-074/E-075/E-076 for the served-item contract and answer-key derivability, on E-094 for key-position balance, and on `STAGE2_ANTILEAK_COMPARISON.md` §7.1–§7.2 for the two attacks this closes.
- **Rationale:** **Failure B and Failure A have one cause and therefore one fix.** The options are baked while the mapping is drawn, so the key is on screen by luck; and difficulty is priced on a proxy for the composition (a count of one operator sub-class) rather than on the composition, so a relabelling moves it.
