# GT Application Process — User Stories

## Purpose

These stories define the breadth of people and situations the application process should support. They are not personas, quotas, eligibility rules, or evidence that any product concept is approved.

The minimal feature set and complete story-to-feature mapping live in `USER_STORY_FEATURE_MAP.md`.

Each story should be tested against:

- equal access to the application and common evidence opportunities;
- domain pathways aligned to a service GT actually offers;
- structured, observable evidence rather than advocacy quality;
- accommodations and missing-evidence handling;
- clear explanations, corrections, and appeals; and
- separation of eligibility, seat allocation, and research participation.

## Applicant and family stories

### Domain-specific and asynchronous strengths

1. **Mathematical and STEM reasoning**
   - As a parent whose fifth-grade child demonstrates exceptional mathematical and STEM reasoning, I want to submit direct evidence of advanced problem solving, transfer, and current readiness, so that unrelated weaknesses do not erase a clear quantitative/STEM strength.

2. **Musical prodigy with high-average reasoning**
   - As a parent whose third-grade child is an exceptional musician but has high-average general reasoning scores, I want the application to state whether musical talent maps to a real GT service and provide a structured domain-evidence route if it does, so that my child is neither excluded by unrelated academic criteria nor admitted under a pathway GT cannot actually serve.

3. **Exceptional verbal and literary ability**
   - As a parent whose fourth-grade child reads far above grade level, writes complex stories, and makes sophisticated verbal connections, I want a literacy/verbal pathway that evaluates depth and transfer rather than vocabulary polish alone, so that the child's capability is judged in the relevant domain.

4. **Visual-spatial and design strength**
   - As a parent whose sixth-grade child builds complex models and understands three-dimensional systems but has less exceptional verbal scores, I want spatial/design evidence to be considered independently, so that an asynchronous profile is preserved.

5. **Scientific inquiry without advanced coursework**
   - As a parent whose child independently designs experiments and investigates natural phenomena but has not had access to advanced science classes, I want a common science-inquiry task, so that prior course access is not required to demonstrate potential.

6. **Advanced coding and systems thinking**
   - As a parent whose child creates software, automations, or complex technical systems outside school, I want a provenance-aware way to share the work and a supervised task to verify ownership and transfer, so that production polish or adult/AI assistance is not mistaken for capability.

7. **Creative producer**
   - As a parent whose child generates unusually original solutions, stories, designs, or inventions, I want originality to be evaluated through a domain-specific task rather than a generic creativity score, so that the evidence remains service-aligned.

8. **Strength across several domains**
   - As a parent whose child is advanced in several areas, I want each domain retained as a profile instead of collapsed into one average, so that meaningful peaks and support needs remain visible.

### Twice-exceptional and accommodated applicants

9. **Gifted student with dyslexia**
   - As a parent whose child reasons at an advanced level but has dyslexia, I want accessible response modes and separation of domain reasoning from reading mechanics where reading is not the construct, so that disability does not mask capability.

10. **Gifted student with ADHD**
    - As a parent whose child shows exceptional insight but has inconsistent attention and output, I want breaks and other approved accommodations to be treated as access supports rather than evidence of low motivation, so that the process measures the intended capability.

11. **Autistic gifted student**
    - As a parent whose child demonstrates deep expertise and unusual reasoning but communicates atypically, I want eye contact, affect, sociability, and interview style excluded from capability ratings, so that autistic presentation is not treated as lack of giftedness.

12. **Slow processing with deep reasoning**
    - As a parent whose child solves complex problems accurately but works slowly, I want speed scored only when the offered service genuinely requires it, so that thoughtful reasoning is not lost to an irrelevant time demand.

13. **Motor, speech, sensory, or physical disability**
    - As a parent whose child needs an alternate response method or testing environment, I want a predeclared construct-equivalent route, so that the accommodation does not lower confidence in the evidence or trigger an adverse review.

14. **Anxiety or unfamiliar-setting effects**
    - As a parent whose child performs inconsistently in unfamiliar or high-pressure settings, I want repeated or alternative common evidence opportunities under the locked policy, so that one distressed performance does not become permanent exclusion.

### Language, culture, and opportunity

15. **Multilingual or English-learning applicant**
    - As a parent whose child reasons deeply in a home language but is still developing academic English, I want translated instructions and language-appropriate response routes, so that English proficiency affects only evidence where English is the actual construct.

16. **Parent with limited English proficiency**
    - As a parent who is not fluent in English, I want translated, audio, and interpreter-supported application options, so that my writing ability does not determine how much evidence admissions receives.

17. **Low-income family without paid enrichment**
    - As a parent whose child has had little access to tutoring, camps, competitions, private lessons, or expensive materials, I want free common evidence tasks and opportunity context, so that purchased enrichment is not treated as capability.

18. **Rural or geographically isolated family**
    - As a parent living far from GT or testing centers, I want a low-bandwidth, remote, or locally proctored equivalent route, so that travel and geography do not determine access.

19. **Homeschool, unschool, or nontraditional education**
    - As a parent whose child lacks conventional grades, teacher nominations, or school test records, I want common direct evidence routes and neutral missing-evidence handling, so that nontraditional schooling is not scored as zero.

20. **Frequent school changes or interrupted education**
    - As a parent whose child has incomplete or inconsistent records because of moves, illness, displacement, or interrupted schooling, I want the process to distinguish unavailable records from low performance, so that administrative history does not become a hidden gate.

21. **High potential with limited prior opportunity**
    - As a parent whose child starts below an advanced-readiness threshold but learns rapidly after brief instruction, I want learning response studied separately from attained level, so that potential and current readiness are not conflated.

22. **High achievement with extensive opportunity**
    - As a parent whose child has exceptional scores and extensive enrichment, I want the child's achievement recognized while assistance and opportunity are documented, so that accomplishment is neither dismissed nor mistaken for opportunity-independent potential.

### Evidence and decision-boundary cases

23. **No available recommender**
    - As a parent whose child does not have a teacher, coach, or other adult who can write a recommendation, I want recommendations to remain optional and non-penalizing, so that adult-network access does not affect eligibility.

24. **Conflicting home and school evidence**
    - As a parent whose child shows advanced behavior at home but not at school, I want the disagreement treated as a contextual question rather than averaged into rejection, so that masking, boredom, language, disability, and setting can be examined.

25. **Near a decision boundary**
    - As a parent whose child's plausible score range crosses a pathway boundary, I want a transparent pending/boundary process with a free retest or equivalent route, so that a one-point difference is not treated as certain.

26. **Invalid or interrupted administration**
    - As a parent whose child's evidence was affected by illness, technology failure, incorrect instructions, or a denied accommodation, I want the invalid result excluded and the evidence opportunity repeated, so that process failure is not attributed to the child.

27. **Optional artifact with adult or AI assistance**
    - As a parent submitting an artifact that involved adult, teacher, or AI support, I want to disclose that assistance without automatic rejection, so that admissions can evaluate ownership and use supervised evidence rather than guess.

28. **Applicant with no polished portfolio**
    - As a parent whose child has strong capability but no curated portfolio, awards, or public products, I want optional artifacts to remain non-decisional until validated, so that production resources do not control admission.

29. **Younger child with unstable development**
    - As a parent of a kindergarten or early-elementary child, I want provisional decisions, repeated entry, and scheduled reassessment, so that one early snapshot does not permanently define the child.

30. **Late-developing or newly discovered strength**
    - As a parent whose older child developed or revealed an advanced strength after an earlier nonqualification, I want a clear re-entry path with no penalty, so that the process recognizes developmental change.

### Choice, privacy, and communication

31. **Family declining research participation**
    - As a parent who does not want my child in research or secondary data use, I want that decision separated from ordinary eligibility and seat allocation, so that consent is meaningful.

32. **Family requesting data rights**
    - As a parent, I want to see, correct, export, and understand the permitted retention or deletion of my child's information, so that sensitive evidence is not a black box.

33. **Family challenging a factual or scoring error**
    - As a parent who identifies a record, accommodation, provenance, or rubric-application error, I want an accessible appeal reviewed by someone independent of the disputed action, so that the locked process can be corrected without requiring more advocacy.

34. **Family whose child is not currently qualified**
    - As a parent whose child does not currently qualify through an evaluated pathway, I want a non-stigmatizing explanation of the evidence, uncertainty, service fit, and re-entry options, so that “not currently qualified” is not presented as “not gifted.”

35. **Student choosing a domain**
    - As a student applicant, I want to identify interests and choose among available domain tasks without receiving points for confidence or enthusiasm, so that self-nomination routes opportunity rather than measuring personality.

## Recommender stories

36. **Teacher with repeated direct evidence**
    - As a teacher who has repeatedly observed advanced domain behavior, I want short behavior-specific prompts with clear context and assistance fields, so that I can report evidence without making a global giftedness judgment.

37. **Teacher with insufficient observation**
    - As a teacher who has not observed the relevant domain or has known the student only briefly, I want an explicit “not observed/insufficient opportunity” option, so that uncertainty is not converted into a low rating.

38. **Community recommender**
    - As a librarian, employer, community leader, coach, childcare provider, or other directly observing adult, I want the same structured evidence form available without prestige weighting, so that relevant out-of-school evidence can be documented consistently.

39. **Paid tutor or coach**
    - As a paid instructor, I want to disclose the financial relationship, instruction provided, comparison group, and assistance level, so that admissions can interpret the evidence without automatically dismissing or privileging it.

## Admissions and review stories

40. **Admissions officer checking completeness**
    - As an admissions officer, I want missing evidence coded by reason rather than as zero, so that access barriers and low capability are not conflated.

41. **Admissions officer explaining a decision**
    - As an admissions officer, I want a pathway-specific decision trace citing the evidence and rule version, so that I can explain outcomes without inventing a post-hoc holistic narrative.

42. **Calibrated evidence rater**
    - As a rater, I want behaviorally anchored research codes, benchmark cases, independent scoring, and adjudication rules, so that my judgment is constrained and drift can be detected.

43. **Domain specialist**
    - As a mathematics, science, spatial/design, literacy, or arts specialist, I want to score only evidence in my defined domain against a service-aligned rubric, so that domain expertise does not become authority over unrelated traits.

44. **Accessibility coordinator**
    - As an accessibility coordinator, I want preapproved construct-preserving accommodations and equivalent response routes, so that support is available before evidence collection and does not trigger an adverse label.

45. **Privacy and data steward**
    - As a data steward, I want separate permissions, minimum necessary access, provenance, retention, deletion, and audit records, so that minors' evidence is protected and every use is reconstructable.

46. **Psychometric or evaluation reviewer**
    - As an evaluator, I want direct and optional evidence kept separate, uncertainty retained, rater/task effects measured, and outcomes distinct from admissions evidence, so that local reliability and incremental validity can be studied honestly.

47. **Operations owner**
    - As the person responsible for running admissions, I want workload, translation, accommodation, double-scoring, appeal, and deadline requirements visible, so that a well-intentioned process does not fail operationally.

48. **School leader deciding what GT can serve**
    - As a school leader, I want each recognized domain mapped to an actual differentiated service and capacity, so that the school does not promise to serve talents its program does not develop.

49. **Independent critic or auditor**
    - As an independent reviewer, I want to replay decisions, inspect optional-evidence firewalls, test privilege proxies, and verify claims against the documented maturity level, so that “holistic” does not become unauditable discretion.

## Coverage checklist

The user-story set covers:

- academic, STEM, verbal, spatial, creative, artistic, technical, and multi-domain strengths;
- synchronous and asynchronous profiles;
- twice-exceptionality and varied accommodations;
- ELL, multilingual, low-income, rural, homeschool, and interrupted-opportunity applicants;
- missing, conflicting, invalid, boundary, coached, and optional evidence;
- young, late-developing, and repeated-entry applicants;
- privacy, research refusal, explanation, correction, and appeal;
- parents, students, teachers, community recommenders, paid instructors, raters, domain experts, accessibility/privacy staff, operators, leaders, evaluators, and critics.

## Prototype priority

For the four-week prototype, implement and test stories:

- 1, 2, 5, 9–18, 21, 23–28, 31–35, and 40–49.

The remaining stories should stay in the regression library even if their domain pathway is not implemented during the capstone.
