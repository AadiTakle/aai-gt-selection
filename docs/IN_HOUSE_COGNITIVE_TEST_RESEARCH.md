# In-House Cognitive / Giftedness Test — Research Aggregation

**Purpose:** Mentor-directed pivot. GT School / Alpha School currently screen K-8 applicants with the **CogAT** (and have looked at the **CCAT**), both third-party instruments that carry a **~$100 per-student fee** paid to the vendor (Riverside owns CogAT; the "CCAT" line is a separate publisher). We want to **build our own K-8 cognitive-ability / giftedness screener** that (a) removes the third-party fee, and (b) is **~2x better than CogAT** on the dimensions that matter (measurement accuracy, thoroughness of construct coverage, adaptivity, and fairness).

**This doc is the running aggregation** of a recurring background research loop. Each sweep appends findings under the matching research question. Do NOT duplicate prior findings. This is a raw, labeled evidence pile to curate — NOT a design decision or approval to deploy a live test.

**Owner:** Team. **Started:** 2026-07-21. **Related prior work:** `docs/COGAT_GAPS_AND_TEST_SUITE_REPORT.md` (which *external* tests to use), `docs/EVIDENCE_DOSSIER.md` (admissions-overhaul thesis), `docs/HOLISTIC_GIFTEDNESS_EVIDENCE_REPORT.md`.

> **CRITICAL LEGAL/IP NOTE (carry into every sweep):** CogAT, CCAT, NNAT, OLSAT, WISC, etc. are copyrighted. We can research *what constructs they measure and what psychometric properties make them defensible*, and build an original instrument on the same public science (item-response theory, reasoning constructs, norming). We must NOT copy items, item formats verbatim, or normed content. Flag any finding that edges toward copying.

> **Evidence labeling** (keep the discipline even in a fast scrape):
> **[VERIFIED]** peer-reviewed / official technical manual / publisher psychometric doc · **[PRESS]** reputable journalism · **[ESTIMATE]** vendor marketing / advocacy, weaker · **[INFERENCE]** our reasoning · **[UNVERIFIED]** claimed, source not yet confirmed.
> Reliability ≠ validity ≠ fairness. A number defends a claim only if it measures what the claim asserts. "2x better" must be defined on a specific, measurable axis — name it.

## Q1 — What are CogAT and CCAT (structure & constructs)

- **[VERIFIED]** CogAT (Riverside Insights) is organized into three batteries — Verbal, Quantitative, Nonverbal — each with three subtests (nine total), using three different subtest formats per battery to increase fairness/validity. Subtests: Verbal = Verbal Classification, Sentence Completion, Verbal Analogies; Quantitative = Number Analogies, Number Series, Number Puzzles; Nonverbal = Figure Matrices, Paper Folding, Figure Classification. (Source: EBSCO Research Starters, "Cognitive Abilities Test (CogAT)" — https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/health-and-medicine/cognitive-abilities-test-cogat)
- **[VERIFIED]** CogAT Form 7 was introduced in 2011, Form 8 in 2017; the forms are parallel/equivalent (same item counts, timing, scoring) allowing immediate retesting by interchanging forms. The line descends from the 1954 Lorge-Thorndike Intelligence Tests. (Source: EBSCO Research Starters — https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/health-and-medicine/cognitive-abilities-test-cogat)
- **[VERIFIED]** CogAT spans ten levels covering K-12. A Primary Edition (K-grade 2, Levels 5/6-8) uses picture-based items for the verbal subtests (no reading required); a Multilevel Edition (Levels 9-17/18) uses text-based verbal items. (Source: EBSCO Research Starters — https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/health-and-medicine/cognitive-abilities-test-cogat)
- **[VERIFIED]** Total administration is ~140-170 min (Primary Edition) and ~145 min (Multilevel); CogAT is a "power" test with generous per-subtest limits rather than strict speeding. (Source: EBSCO Research Starters — https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/health-and-medicine/cognitive-abilities-test-cogat)
- **[PRESS]** At Level 10 (grade 4) and above, each of the nine subtests is allotted 10 minutes, so a full administration runs ~2.5-3 hours including setup. (Source: TestingMom, "CogAT Test" — https://www.testingmom.com/tests/cogat-test/)
- **[PRESS]** The CCAT (Canadian Cognitive Abilities Test, Form 7; publisher Nelson) is the Canadian counterpart to CogAT — same three batteries and nine subtests, normed on Canadian students; Primary Edition ~118 questions, Multilevel Edition ~170 (grade 3) to ~176 (grades 4+) items. (Source: TestingMom "CCAT Test" — https://www.testingmom.com/tests/ccat-test/ ; GiftedReady — https://www.giftedready.com/canadian-cognitive-abilities-test-ccat/)

### Q1 — Sweep 4: competitor benchmarking (the alternatives we must beat)
- **[VERIFIED]** WISC-V (individually administered "gold standard"): FSIQ reliability **0.96**, index reliabilities .88-.93, test-retest .92 — but requires a trained psychologist ~1h15m 1-on-1, which is why it is a *confirmation* tool, not a universal screen. Kit ~$1,300-$1,500 [UNVERIFIED price]; no per-student fee, but the psychologist's administration time is the dominant real cost. (Source: Pearson WISC-V Efficacy Research Report, 2018 — https://www.pearson.com/content/dam/one-dot-com/one-dot-com/global/Files/efficacy-and-research/reports/efficacy-assessment-reports/WISC-V-research-report.pdf)
- **[VERIFIED]** NAGC advises the WISC-V FSIQ should NOT be *required* for gifted/2e ID — any of several indices (GAI, Nonverbal, Verbal, Expanded Fluid) within its CI should suffice. Supports our multi-signal, CI-band design over a single hard composite. (Source: Pearson/NAGC guidance — https://support.pearson.com/usclinical/s/article/WISC-V-Use-with-Gifted-and-Talented-Students)
- **[VERIFIED]** NNAT3 is nonverbal-only (matrix/pattern), K-12, single Naglieri Ability Index; NNAT3 reuses NNAT2 items with only re-normed scores. (Source: Pearson NNAT3 Manual Levels A-D — https://www.pearsonassessments.com/content/dam/school/global/clinical/us/assets/nnat3/nnat3-manual-levels-a-d.pdf)
- **[VERIFIED]** *Key weakness of the leading "equity" screener:* independent research found the NNAT's SEM was roughly **TWICE** that of Raven's or CogAT (median SD 17.2 for ELL children), and its norming overestimates both high and low scorers. (Source: Lohman, Korb & Lakin, *Gifted Child Quarterly*, 2008 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0016986208321808)
- **[VERIFIED]** OLSAT 8 (2003) measures verbal + nonverbal reasoning K-12 → School Ability Index (mean 100, **SD 16** like CogAT, not Wechsler's 15); internal consistency ~0.80-0.90. Package/per-document scoring fees, no public flat per-student price. (Source: Pearson OLSAT 8 — https://www.pearsonassessments.com/en-us/Store/Professional-Assessments/Academic-Learning/Otis-Lennon-School-Ability-Test-|-Eighth-Edition/p/100000003 ; https://www.cogn-iq.org/learn/tests/olsat/)
- **[VERIFIED]** InView (DRC/TerraNova): group-administered, verbal + quantitative reasoning + sequences + analogies, grades 2-12 (K-12 online), 5 subtests → Cognitive Skills Index (mean 100), 2007 norms, online form available; DRC publishes no reliability or per-student pricing publicly. (Source: TerraNova Next / DRC — https://terranovanext.com/inview-cognitive-abilities/)
- **[VERIFIED]** Raven's 2 (2018, Pearson): nonverbal matrix reasoning for g, re-normed for the Flynn effect, offered in paper, digital, AND **adaptive-digital** forms with automated scoring; split-half >0.85 (ages 9+), high-.90s on the digital long form — BUT normative sample is thin (~50-70 per age band). Confirms an adaptive nonverbal g test is commercially viable. (Source: Pearson Raven's 2 — https://www.pearsonassessments.com/en-us/Store/Professional-Assessments/Cognition-&-Neuro/Raven's-Progressive-Matrices-Second-Edition-|-Raven's-2/p/100001960)
- **[VERIFIED]** *No existing screener cleanly solves the equity gap:* Naglieri & Ford (2003) claimed the NNAT identified near-equal proportions (White 5.6%, Black 5.1%, Hispanic 4.4% at SS 125), but Lohman (2005) showed the sample was unrepresentative, and Lohman/Korb/Lakin (2008) + Carman et al. (2020) found BOTH CogAT-Nonverbal and NNAT were LESS likely to identify underrepresented students (ELL Hispanic scoring 6-10 pts lower; nonverbal the weakest achievement predictor, ELL reading r=0.35 vs non-ELL 0.49). (Source: Lohman, *GCQ* 2005 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/001698620504900103 ; Lohman/Korb/Lakin 2008 — http://www.korbedpsych.com/LinkedFiles/Identifying_Academically_Gifted_ELL.pdf)
- **[INFERENCE]** Benchmarking takeaway: our accuracy bar is CogAT/WISC-tier reliability (~.90+ composite, SEM ≤ CogAT's ~3.6-4.8 SAS pts — explicitly NOT the NNAT's ~2× SEM), and the equity bar is unmet by every nonverbal-only competitor — so a *multi-signal + local-norms* design is where we can plausibly beat all of them, not by copying the nonverbal-only "equity" shortcut.

## Q2 — What makes them "accurate" (reliability, validity, norming)

- **[VERIFIED]** The Standard Age Score (SAS) is a normalized standard score with mean = 100, SD = 16, reported per battery and for the composite; age norms cover ages 4 yr 11 mo to 21 yr 7 mo. (Source: EBSCO Research Starters — https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/health-and-medicine/cognitive-abilities-test-cogat)
- **[VERIFIED]** KR-20 internal-consistency reliabilities run in the high 0.90s for Verbal and Nonverbal and low 0.90s for Quantitative; across the standardization sample composite reliability estimates ranged .91-.95 (Verbal), .91-.94 (Nonverbal), .91-.94 (Quantitative). (Source: EBSCO Research Starters — https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/health-and-medicine/cognitive-abilities-test-cogat)
- **[VERIFIED]** CogAT Form 7 was co-normed with the Iowa Assessments (Form E) on the same population, enabling direct ability-achievement comparison; norms available for 2011 or 2017 norm year (2017 is scoring default). (Source: Riverside Insights, CogAT 7/8 Complete Norms & Score Conversions Guide — https://onlinehelp.riversideinsights.com/Help/Elevate/assets/docs/CogAT_78_COMP_NandSC_Guide_091820.pdf)
- **[VERIFIED]** Validity evidence includes confirmatory factor analysis plus convergent correlations with WISC-V and Woodcock-Johnson IV Cognitive (WJ-IV COG), and strong correlation with the co-normed Iowa achievement tests (ITBS/ITED). (Source: EBSCO Research Starters — https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/health-and-medicine/cognitive-abilities-test-cogat)
- **[VERIFIED]** *(Sweep 3 — confirms the prior UNVERIFIED figure)* CogAT Form 7 standardization sample = **65,350 U.S. K-12 students**, "representing all areas and ethnic groups," standardized 2011 (Lohman/Riverside) — confirmed by an independent peer-reviewed test review. (Source: Warne, R.T., "Test Review: Cognitive Abilities Test, Form 7," *Journal of Psychoeducational Assessment* 33(2):188-192, 2015 — https://doi.org/10.1177/0734282914548324)
- **[VERIFIED]** *(Sweep 3)* Independent peer-reviewed internal-consistency reliabilities (standardization sample): Verbal composite .91-.95, Nonverbal .91-.94, Quantitative .91-.94 — matching the vendor KR-20 ranges. (Source: Warne 2015, *J Psychoeducational Assessment* — https://doi.org/10.1177/0734282914548324)
- **[VERIFIED]** *(Sweep 3)* Meta-analytic criterion-related validity: CogAT correlates with criterion instruments at weighted mean **r = .63, 95% CI [.57, .69]** (24 studies, 33 effect sizes, screened from 1,480) — higher than NNAT's comparable r = .44. This is the independent benchmark our instrument's validity coefficient must approach or beat. (Source: Ozen, Pereira, Karatas, Castillo-Hermosilla & Maeda, "A Meta-Analytic Evaluation... Validity of the Cognitive Abilities Test," *Gifted Child Quarterly*, 2025 — https://doi.org/10.1177/00169862241285593)
- **[INFERENCE]** *(Sweep 3)* No discrete CogAT SEM is published in any accessible independent source; derived from SD=16 and reliability .91-.95, SEM = SD·√(1−r) ≈ **3.6-4.8 SAS points**. This is a derived estimate (a defensible target for our own SEM), not a cited value. (Source: derivation from EBSCO SD=16 + Warne 2015 reliability)
- **[VERIFIED]** *(Sweep 3)* Independent Buros MMY reviews of CogAT exist and are favorable ("an excellent K-12 ability test battery… large, representative normative sample… good evidence for reliability and validity"), though Warne separately flagged published *validity* evidence as "limited." Prior-form review: Rogers, B.G., "Review of CogAT Form 6," *16th Mental Measurements Yearbook*, 2005; Form 7 in 21st MMY (2021). Specific SEM numbers inside are paywalled. (Source: Buros Center for Testing — https://buros.org/tests-reviewed-mental-measurements-yearbook-series/ ; Warne 2015)
- **[UNVERIFIED]** *(Sweep 3)* No independent, citable concordance giving actual r values between the CogAT composite and a specific IQ test (WISC-V/WAIS) or the co-normed Iowa Assessments was found outside Riverside's materials — convergent validity vs. WISC-V/WJ-IV is described only qualitatively. Closest confirmed independent figure is the meta-analytic composite r = .63 (Ozen et al. 2025).
- **[UNVERIFIED]** Exact SEM values, test-retest coefficients, per-subtest item counts, and the composite (VAI) weighting formula were NOT located in accessible sources; they reside in the CogAT Form 7 Research & Development Guide / technical manual (Riverside), which was not directly readable. CogAT report confidence intervals are explicitly built from the SEM. (Source: EBSCO Research Starters notes CI use of SEM — https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/health-and-medicine/cognitive-abilities-test-cogat) — **GAP: pull the R&D Guide next sweep.**
- **[VERIFIED]** *(Sweep 2)* CogAT reports three battery scores plus a composite on the continuous Universal Scale Score (USS); each student's profile carries a confidence interval built from the SEM — but no single SEM constant is published in accessible secondary sources. (Source: EBSCO Research Starters — https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/health-and-medicine/cognitive-abilities-test-cogat)
- **[INFERENCE]** *(Sweep 2)* The composite and partial composites (e.g., VAI = Verbal+Nonverbal; VQN) appear to be derived by averaging constituent battery USS/SAS values, not summing raw items — consistent with USS-scale reporting; exact weighting still unconfirmed against the primary R&D Guide.
- **[UNVERIFIED]** *(Sweep 2)* A CogAT-specific test-retest/stability coefficient still could not be confirmed from a primary source (a "0.91 test-retest" figure found in search belongs to a different instrument, the Turkish BNV-II, not CogAT). Closest confirmed fact remains the KR-20 internal-consistency range (.91-.95). **GAP persists: needs the R&D Guide.**
- **[VERIFIED]** *(Sweep 2)* NWEA MAP Growth overall reliability averages ~0.95 across subjects/grades (lower in Science and early grades), and reports a per-student conditional SEM on the RIT scale (documented example: RIT 214, CSEM 3.2 → true-score band ~210.8-217.2); the event with the lowest CSEM is designated the growth test event. (Source: NWEA MAP Growth Technical Report 2024-25, Meyer et al. — https://www.nwea.org/uploads/MAP-Growth-Technical-Report-2025.pdf ; RIT help — https://teach.mapnwea.org/impl/maphelp/Content/AboutMAP/WhatRITMeans.htm)

## Q3 — How they gauge adaptability during the test (CAT / adaptive mechanics)

- **[VERIFIED]** The CAT cycle re-estimates ability (maximum likelihood or Bayesian) after each response and selects the next item via the IRT item information function — "maximum information" picks the item with maximum Fisher information at the current ability estimate; Bayesian selection minimizes expected posterior variance. (Source: Song, *Korean J Med Educ* review, 2017 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5676016/)
- **[VERIFIED]** CAT test length averages ~50% shorter than paper-and-pencil at equal precision; multidimensional CAT with content balancing achieves equal precision with 25-40% fewer items than unidimensional CAT. (Source: Song, PMC review, 2017 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5676016/)
- **[VERIFIED]** Stopping rules fall in two families: variable-length (terminate when the SE of the ability estimate / posterior variance drops below a threshold) and fixed-length/classification (continue until the ability CI clears a cut-score or max item count is hit). (Source: Song, PMC review, 2017 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5676016/)
- **[VERIFIED]** Exposure control (avoid overusing items) includes randomly selecting the first few items from a subset of the bank. (Source: Song, PMC review, 2017 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5676016/)
- **[VERIFIED]** Response latency is modeled with van der Linden's lognormal response-time model (2006), enabling speededness control; in adaptive testing, shadow-test constraints kept total testing time within <10 seconds of a reference test across conditions. (Source: van der Linden & Xiong, *J Educ & Behav Stat* 38(4):418-438, 2013 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/1076998612466143 ; van der Linden, JEBS 31:181-204, 2006)
- **[VERIFIED]** MAP Growth (NWEA) is built on the Rasch (1-parameter IRT) model and a vertical RIT scale (~100-350); it selects each item for maximum information, targeting the difficulty where the student has ~50% chance correct, re-adjusts after each response, and terminates when SE falls below a set value, final RIT via maximum-likelihood. (Source: NWEA MAP Growth Technical Report, 2019 — https://www.nwea.org/uploads/2021/11/MAP-Growth-Technical-Report-2019_NWEA.pdf ; NWEA RIT help — https://teach.mapnwea.org/impl/maphelp/Content/AboutMAP/WhatRITMeans.htm)
- **[INFERENCE]** The paper/standard CogAT is NOT adaptive — fixed forms by level/grade — whereas MAP Growth adapts item-by-item; the trade-off is that CAT reaches a target SE with far fewer items because every item is targeted near current ability rather than spread across a fixed difficulty range. **Design implication: an adaptive in-house test is the single biggest lever for beating CogAT on efficiency + precision simultaneously.**

## Q4 — Difficulty level & construct calibration for K-8

- **[VERIFIED]** CogAT Form 7 spans 10 levels across K-12, three batteries × three subtests; the K-2 primary edition is teacher-paced and orally administered, and Form 7 shifted the two verbal primary subtests to a picture-based format (Picture Analogies, Picture Classification) to remove the need to read words to young children. (Source: Riverside CogAT Form 7 Short Guide for Teachers — https://www.aacs.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/CogAT-A-Short-Guide-for-Teachers.pdf ; EBSCO Research Starters — https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/health-and-medicine/cognitive-abilities-test-cogat)
- **[VERIFIED]** The picture/oral-to-text transition occurs at Level 9 (grade 3): Level 9 moves from picture-based teacher-paced verbal/quant subtests to text/numeric timed subtests; Level 8 mixes some upper-level-format items. (Source: Riverside CogAT Form 7 Score Interpretation Guide — https://www.riversidedatamanager.com/BalancedManagement/DigitalResources/Baggage_Files/CogAT/CogAT_7_SIG_v.2-1_092220.pdf)
- **[VERIFIED]** CogAT scoring uses a Universal Scale Score (USS) converted to age/grade norms (e.g., USS 155 = 53rd grade percentile in fall of Grade 1); Form 8 raw scores cross-walk to Form 7 norms — evidence of a common vertical scale across grade bands. (Source: CogAT 7/8 Norms & Score Conversions Guide, Riverside — https://onlinehelp.riversideinsights.com/Help/Elevate/assets/docs/CogAT_78_COMP_NandSC_Guide_091820.pdf)
- **[VERIFIED]** Grade-level standardized tests produce ceiling effects for gifted students (max score = apparent 99th percentile while masking true level); the field remedy is above-level/out-of-level testing, which raises the ceiling, increases score variability, improves reliability, and reduces regression to the mean. (Source: Warne, "Using Above-Level Testing to Track Growth," *Gifted Child Quarterly*, 2014 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0016986213513793)
- **[VERIFIED]** Above-level testing originated with SMPY (administered the SAT to students ≤13 to escape grade-level ceilings) and remains standard in talent searches (Belin-Blank Exceptional Student Talent Search). (Source: Belin-Blank / University of Iowa — https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro ; Keating & Stanley 1972; Warne 2012)
- **[UNVERIFIED]** NWEA typical test length (~40-50 items) and specific SEM target, and CogAT's exact IRT b-parameter calibration values, could not be confirmed (technical PDFs returned as binary / R&D Guide not retrievable as text). **GAP: extract these from the primary technical manuals next sweep.**

## Q5 — How to make ours 2x better (what to test + how to test it)

- **[VERIFIED]** Spatial ability at age 13 predicts STEM degrees, patents, and technical publications with incremental validity beyond SAT-V/SAT-M; in ~400,000 students tracked 11 years it predicted STEM accomplishment at 38 even after partialling verbal/math talent — a "sleeping giant" missed by verbal/quant-only screens. (Source: Wai, Lubinski & Benbow, 2009, *J Educ Psychology* 101(4):817-835 — https://cdn.vanderbilt.edu/t2-my/my-prd/wp-content/uploads/sites/826/2013/02/Wai2009SpatialAbility.pdf)
- **[VERIFIED]** Across CHC studies g dominates (mean r² ≈ 0.54 with achievement), and specific abilities' incremental R² beyond g is usually small — but a 2024 study found Reasoning (Gf) added ΔR² = 0.07-0.09 for math grades and Verbal added ΔR² = 0.05-0.09 for language grades, so a few specific factors carry real incremental signal. (Source: specific-abilities incremental-validity study, *Intelligence*, 2024 — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160289624000102)
- **[VERIFIED]** Working memory adds unique variance beyond IQ; in a 6-year longitudinal study WM at school entry out-predicted IQ for later achievement, and n-back/complex-span factors each explained unique Gf variance (24% and 14%) beyond shared variance (30%). (Source: National Academies, *Measuring Human Capabilities*, 2015 — https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/19017/chapter/6)
- **[VERIFIED]** In IRT, SE(θ) = 1/√(test information); a stopping rule of SE = 0.32 corresponds to reliability 0.90. CAT reaches target precision with far fewer items by selecting maximally-informative items near current θ. (Source: Choi et al., "A New Stopping Rule for CAT," PMC3028267 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3028267/)
- **[INFERENCE]** Because SE ∝ 1/√(information), **halving SEM requires ~4x the test information** (~4x items on a fixed form). CAT partly evades this by raising information-per-item. Practical rule: frame "2x better" per-axis (e.g., "SEM halved in the 90th-98th percentile decision band"), NOT globally, and achieve it via adaptivity, not brute test length.
- **[VERIFIED]** Universal 2nd-grade nonverbal screening (cut 130, or 116 disadvantaged/ELL) raised gifted ID with no standards change: ~180% (disadvantaged), ~130% (Hispanic), ~80% (Black); traditional referral under-referred Black, Hispanic, FRL, ELL, and female students. (Source: Card & Giuliano, *PNAS* 2016, 113(48):13678-13683 — https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1605043113) *(cross-ref EVIDENCE_DOSSIER C4; here it is the strongest fairness lever for our design)*
- **[VERIFIED]** Nonverbal screening reduces but does not eliminate subgroup gaps: Naglieri & Ford (2003, N≈20,000) found similar ≥125 rates for White (5.6%), Black (5.1%), Hispanic (4.4%); Giessman et al. (2013) found CogAT6 Nonverbal produced smaller gaps than NNAT2; a 2021 meta-analysis still found students of color underrepresented. (Source: Naglieri & Ford 2003, *Gifted Child Quarterly* 47(2) — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/001698620304700206 ; Giessman et al. 2013 — https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ996235)
- **[VERIFIED]** Response-time process data can add predictive validity beyond accuracy (shown for reading comprehension) but only when RT and accuracy are modeled jointly to handle the speed-accuracy trade-off; authors call the evidence "suggestive," needing larger-scale confirmation. (Source: "Use of Response Time for Measuring Cognitive Ability," *J Intelligence* 4(4):14, 2016 — https://www.mdpi.com/2079-3200/4/4/14)
- **[INFERENCE] Design blueprint for a measurably-better screener:** (1) add spatial/visualization + a WM/Gf task to the verbal/quant/nonverbal core (documented incremental validity); (2) deliver via CAT to cut SEM in the decision band; (3) apply universal screening with local/subgroup norms (largest, best-evidenced equity lever — Card & Giuliano); (4) treat RT/process data as a secondary, jointly-modeled signal, not a primary score.

### Q5 — Sweep 2: concrete, buildable K-8 task types (public-domain vs. copyrighted)
- **[VERIFIED]** ICAR (International Cognitive Ability Resource) is a public-domain item bank: Matrix Reasoning = 11 Raven's-style 3×3 items (α ≈ 0.79), 3D Rotation α = 0.93; 16-item short form α = 0.81, 60-item α = 0.94; full ICAR corrected r = 0.75 with Raven's APM. **FREE/public-domain — usable seed for our matrix + rotation subtests.** (Source: Condon & Revelle, *Intelligence* 43:52-64, 2014 — https://www.personality-project.org/revelle/publications/condon.icar.14.pdf ; catalogue https://icar-project.com/ICAR_Catalogue.pdf)
- **[VERIFIED]** A Children's ICAR (Ch-ICAR) has been developed and validated for youth, extending the public-domain matrix/reasoning items to younger ages — directly relevant to K-8. (Source: *Behavior Research Methods*, 2024 — https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13428-024-02591-1)
- **[VERIFIED]** Raven's Coloured Progressive Matrices (fluid reasoning) targets ages ~5¼-11½, split-half reliability ~0.81-0.91 (Omani norms, N=1,042), r=0.405 with achievement. **COPYRIGHTED (Pearson)** — use ICAR/Ch-ICAR for free matrix items instead. (Source: ERIC EJ1157840 — https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1157840.pdf)
- **[VERIFIED]** Mental Folding Test for Children (MFTC; Harris et al., 2013) is a validated paper-folding spatial task for ages 4-7 (mental folding reliably emerges ~5.5 yrs) — covers the K-2 spatial floor. (Source: Harris et al., *Mind, Brain, and Education*, 2013 — https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/mbe.12007)
- **[VERIFIED]** Mental rotation is reliably measurable in 6-9-year-olds; a 2023 study (N=96) of four MR tasks found a single factor (good construct validity), α acceptable-to-excellent (paper-pencil > computer-based). (Source: Krüger et al., *J Intelligence* 11(8):165, 2023 — https://www.mdpi.com/2079-3200/11/8/165)
- **[VERIFIED]** Corsi Block-Tapping (visuospatial WM span) was standardized ages 11-16 (N=1,122) against WISC-R; forward = short-term memory, backward = working memory. Method is FREE/public (open PsyToolkit/Inquisit versions); only branded norms are copyrighted. Caveat: administration/scoring lacks cross-study standardization. (Source: Orsini, *Perceptual & Motor Skills* 79:1547, 1994 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2466/pms.1994.79.3f.1547)
- **[VERIFIED]** n-back (WM updating) is validated in school children: 1/2/3-back normed on N=3,722 Spanish children ages 7-13; split-half r≈0.83, 2-week test-retest r≈0.73. 1-back suits youngest elementary; 2/3-back discriminate at older ages. FREE/public method. (Source: PMC4597481 — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4597481/)
- **[INFERENCE]** Buildable K-8 battery from public-domain/open tasks: matrix reasoning (ICAR/Ch-ICAR), 2D/3D rotation (ICAR 3D Rotation), paper folding (MFTC K-2, PFT upper grades), Corsi span, backward digit span, 1-3-back. Only branded norm-referenced instruments (Raven's, WISC digit/Corsi norms) are copyrighted — the *tasks* themselves are free to reimplement with original stimuli.

### Q5 — Sweep 2: fairness / DIF methodology (defensibility pipeline)
- **[VERIFIED]** ETS delta-scale Mantel-Haenszel DIF classification: ΔMH = −2.35 × ln(αMH), flagged as A (negligible, |ΔMH|<1.0 → retain), B (moderate, 1.0≤|ΔMH|<1.5 + significant χ² → content review), C (large, |ΔMH|≥1.5 + significance → revise/remove). Requires BOTH significance and effect size, preventing large-sample false alarms. (Source: Dorans & Holland 1993 / Zwick & Ercikan 1989, ETS review — https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1109842.pdf)
- **[VERIFIED]** The ΔMH=1.0 (B) threshold corresponds to a common odds ratio αMH ≈ 1.53 — one group ~1.53× as likely (in odds) to answer correctly — a concrete reporting interpretation. (Source: "Refining Effect-Size Measures for DIF," arXiv — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21531)
- **[VERIFIED]** Zumbo's (1999) logistic-regression DIF framework detects both uniform (group term) and non-uniform (group×ability interaction) DIF via a 3-step nested R² comparison; Jodoin & Gierl (2001) R² cutoffs map to A/B/C letters (R²<0.035=A, 0.035-0.070=B, >0.070=C). Logistic regression inflates Type I error, so the effect-size gate (not just significance) is essential. (Source: Zumbo, UBC — https://faculty.educ.ubc.ca/zumbo/talks/Zumbo_ITC.PDF ; Jodoin & Gierl summary — https://www.cogn-iq.org/learn/theory/differential-item-functioning/)
- **[VERIFIED]** The AERA/APA/NCME *Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing* (2014) devote a dedicated foundational chapter to fairness, defining it as valid score interpretation "for all examinees" and requiring minimization of construct-irrelevant variance by culture, language, gender, disability, and SES — the authoritative standard our instrument must cite. (Source: APA — https://www.apa.org/science/programs/testing/standards ; NCME — https://ncme.org/resources/books/testing-standards/)
- **[VERIFIED]** *Local norms are the highest-leverage equity lever:* Peters et al. (2019, 3rd-graders, 10 states, 10 years) found switching from national to building-level norms raised Black representation ~300% (math)/238% (reading) and Hispanic ~170% (math)/157% (reading), with White/Asian identification falling correspondingly — without changing the construct bar. (Source: Peters, Rambo-Hernandez, Makel, Matthews & Plucker, *AERA Open*, 2019 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/2332858419848446)
- **[INFERENCE]** Two-stage defensibility pipeline for our build: (1) run every item through Mantel-Haenszel with ETS A/B/C flagging (drop/revise C, review B), cross-checked with Zumbo logistic-regression R² for non-uniform DIF; (2) score against **building-level local norms**, not national norms (the single highest-leverage equity lever, 300%/170% subgroup gains). Requires collecting group-membership variables (race, SES, EL, sex) at pilot so DIF is computable.

## Q6 — Feasibility, cost, IP & operational reality

- **[VERIFIED]** Copyright protects the specific expression of test items, NOT the underlying construct or psychometric methods (idea-expression distinction); IRT and CAT are unpatentable methods/theory — so we can freely use them and write original items measuring the same constructs CogAT measures. (Source: Campbell, "'Testing' the Copyright Clause," 69 Neb. L. Rev., 1990 — https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1762&context=nlr ; APA test-security FAQ — https://www.apa.org/science/programs/testing/test-security-faq)
- **[VERIFIED]** Public-domain item resources can seed a bank without infringement: ICAR (International Cognitive Ability Resource) is a public-domain cognitive-ability item pool (Condon & Revelle, 2014, *Intelligence* 43:52-64); IPIP offers 3,000+ non-cognitive items — both explicitly non-copyrighted. (Source: Condon & Revelle 2014 — https://www.sciencedirect.com/ ; IPIP — https://ipip.ori.org/)
- **[VERIFIED]** Mature open-source IRT/CAT tooling is free: R packages mirt/mirtCAT (Chalmers 2016, *J Stat Software* 71(5); uni- and multidimensional CAT, Shiny, shadow-testing) and catR (Magis & Raîche; rich item-selection/stopping rules), plus the Concerto CAT platform (Univ. of Cambridge) for deployment. (Source: Chalmers 2016 — https://www.jstatsoft.org/article/view/v071i05 ; mirtCAT CRAN — https://cran.r-project.org/package=mirtCAT)
- **[VERIFIED]** Norming is the real cost/time driver: commercial national norms use census-stratified samples of ~2,000-10,000+ (WISC-V: 2,200 children across 11 age groups matched to 2012 US census on race/ethnicity, parent education, region), and validation can take years post-publication because rare high-scoring cases are hard to collect. (Source: Pearson WISC-V Technical Report 6 — https://www.pearsonassessments.com/content/dam/school/global/clinical/us/assets/wisc-v/wisc-v-technical-report-6-extended-norms.pdf)
- **[PRESS]** Large-scale standardized test development runs into the hundreds of millions (Pearson paid $360M in federal funds to build PARCC; its Texas contract alone ~$500M); US statewide testing costs ~$1.7B/year — indicating norming/validation, not item authoring, is where the money goes. (Source: Education Week, 2012 — https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/standardized-testing-costs-states-1-7-billion-a-year-study-says/2012/11)
- **[INFERENCE] Feasibility read:** items + IRT/CAT engine are legally and technically cheap (original items, ICAR seed, mirtCAT/catR/Concerto — near-zero licensing). The binding constraint — and the bulk of what the ~$100 CogAT fee buys — is a secure, nationally representative, validated **norm**. A credible "2x better" replacement must budget primarily for norming + DIF/fairness + multi-year validation, and can realistically **start with defensible local norms** (per Card & Giuliano) while building toward national norms.

### Q6 — Sweep 2: norming sample sizes, cost anchors, and build-your-own precedents
- **[VERIFIED]** Norming N is set by tolerable sampling error: practical floor ~100 examinees per grade/age band for stable central tendency, 200+ for a commercially defensible test, and much larger to pin the extreme percentiles (1st/5th/95th/99th) that gifted cut-scores depend on. A well-matched N=500 beats a poorly representative N=2,000. (Source: Crocker & Algina framework / LEADERS Project — https://www.leadersproject.org/2013/03/01/understanding-the-normative-sample/ ; PMC7885019 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7885019/)
- **[ESTIMATE]** Custom-assessment cost anchors: a fully tailored, validated, web-enabled assessment system "can easily exceed $500,000"; a single organization-specific Situational Judgment Test was estimated at $60,000-$120,000 (2008 dollars) — lower/upper bounds for a custom screener build, well below multimillion-dollar state contracts. (Source: Workforce.com — https://workforce.com/news/estimating-the-financial-value-of-staffing-assessment-tools)
- **[VERIFIED]** *Precedent — a university-built, validated, FREE screener at modest N:* the HOPE Teacher Rating Scale (Peters & Gentry, Purdue GERI), an 11-item equity-focused K-12 screener, was validated on ~5,995 students / ~350 teachers, revised on ~1,700 students across 3 districts, reaching α=.96 with measurement invariance across low-income/diverse groups; funded by the Jack Kent Cooke Foundation and released as a free download. (Source: Peters & Gentry, *Gifted Child Quarterly* 54:298-313, 2010 — https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1049831 ; Purdue GERI — https://purduegeri.wixsite.com/instrument/hope)
- **[VERIFIED]** *Precedent — district/university-built identification effort with real N/cost/timeline:* Project Bright IDEA (NC DPI + Duke AAGC) piloted in 5 Title I schools / 900+ students (2001-04), then scaled on a $2.5M federal Javits grant to 28 schools/11 districts (2004-10); Bright IDEA 3 received $1,864,699 (2014) for 32 Wake County schools. (Source: Duke AAGC — https://sites.duke.edu/aagc/bright-idea-1/ ; ERIC ED606974 — https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED606974.pdf)
- **[VERIFIED]** *Precedent — near-zero-cost "build your own" via existing data:* an Arkansas study found ~30% of students scoring in the top 5% on state achievement tests were NOT identified for gifted services; repurposing already-collected state achievement tests as a universal screener with context-matched local norms is a near-zero-marginal-cost alternative to buying CogAT/NNAT. (Source: EDRE/Univ. of Arkansas WP 2025-03 — https://edre.uark.edu/_resources/pdf/edrewp2025-03.pdf)
- **[INFERENCE]** Cost/feasibility synthesis: a defensible in-house screener does NOT require CogAT's national norming budget up front. A staged path — original items (ICAR-seeded) + open CAT engine + building-level local norms + Mantel-Haenszel/logistic DIF screening, piloted at ~100-200/grade — is achievable in the ~$60k-$500k band and mirrors HOPE/Bright IDEA precedents, while national norms remain a later, optional scale-up.

### Q6 — Sweep 3: concrete pilot-design parameters (calibration N, bank size, stop rules, cut-score)
- **[VERIFIED]** IRT calibration sample rules of thumb: Rasch/1PL ~200 examinees estimate item difficulty to ±0.5 logits (Linacre: 30 exploratory min, 50 for ±1 logit at 99%, 250 polytomous); 2PL ~500; 3PL ~1,000 (De Ayala), with simulation work arguing 2,000-3,000+ for high-precision 3PL. (Source: Linacre, *Rasch Measurement Transactions* — https://www.rasch.org/rmt/rmt74m.htm ; Schroeders et al., *AMPPS* 2025 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/25152459251314798)
- **[VERIFIED]** Rasch precision formula ties N to target SE: 4/SE² < N < 9/SE², so an item SE of 0.20 logits needs ~100-225 examinees. (Source: Linacre — https://www.rasch.org/rmt/rmt74m.htm)
- **[VERIFIED]** CAT item-bank floor ≈ 3× the intended test length (e.g., ~60 calibrated items for a 20-item CAT), larger with content-balancing/exposure control or measurement at extreme ability. (Source: Assessment Systems / ASC — https://assess.com/computerized-adaptive-testing/)
- **[VERIFIED]** CAT stop rule for 0.90 reliability = SE(θ) ≤ 0.316 on the theta metric (0.387 = .85, 0.447 = .80), via Reliability = 1 − SE². Operational pediatric CATs (PROMIS/NIH Toolbox) use combined rules, e.g., SE(θ) < 0.40 OR a 12-item cap. (Source: *Perspectives on Medical Education* 2023 — https://pmejournal.org/articles/10.5334/pme.855 ; NIH Toolbox, PMC11951945 — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11951945/)
- **[VERIFIED]** Test length trades against SE nonlinearly: because SE = 1/√information, halving SE (0.5→0.15 logits) needs ~10× the items — the quantitative form of the "2× better" caution. (Source: Linacre, *RMT* — https://www.rasch.org/rmt/rmt202f.htm)
- **[VERIFIED]** Field-test seeding inflates the total sample: guaranteeing 1,000 / 500 / 250 valid responses per pretest item required total samples of ~11,000 / 6,000 / 3,000 (Rasch). Small-sample calibration is still feasible — fixed-item-parameter calibration (FIPC) gave stable parameters at n=250/group in PISA 2015. (Source: *GJHSS* — https://globaljournals.org/GJHSS_Volume15/8-CAT-Field-Test-Item-Calibration.pdf ; Guo, ETS RR 2024 — https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ets2.12376)
- **[VERIFIED]** Regression-based/continuous norming needs 2.5-5.5× smaller samples per age subgroup than traditional binned norming for equal precision — the cost-efficient choice across 9 K-8 grades. (Source: Oosterhuis, van der Ark & Sijtsma, *Assessment*, 2016 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1073191115580638)
- **[VERIFIED]** Gifted cut-scores should be SEM confidence bands, not hard lines: Maine defines eligibility as 2 SD above mean MINUS the SEM; Florida requires the SEM be applied symmetrically. Read a score as observed ±1 SEM (68%) or ±1.96 SEM (95%). (Source: Maine DOE — https://www.maine.gov/doe/sites/maine.gov.doe/files/inline-files/Combined-Factsheet-for-Identification-November-2014.pdf ; Florida DOE — https://www.fldoe.org/core/fileparse.php/7567/urlt/y1996-7.pdf)
- **[INFERENCE] STRAW-MAN PILOT for our build:** (1) **Model:** Rasch/1PL per subtest → each item needs only ~200-250 valid responses (vs. 500-1,000 for 2PL/3PL). (2) **Sample:** spiraled field-test booklets, ~N≈500-800 children/grade to net ~250 responses/item after seeding inflation. (3) **Bank:** ~36 calibrated items per construct (3× a 12-item CAT cap), weighted to the upper tail since gifted screening measures at extreme θ. (4) **Stop rule:** SE(θ) ≤ 0.316 (reliability .90) OR 12-item cap. (5) **Cut-score:** apply the 95th-percentile gift cut as a 95% SEM band (observed ±1.96 SEM), flagging near-threshold children for confirmatory testing — matching Maine/Florida defensible practice. (6) **Norms:** regression-based continuous norming (smaller N) + building-level local norms for equity.
