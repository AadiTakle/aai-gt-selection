# In-House Cognitive / Giftedness Test — Research Aggregation

**Purpose:** Mentor-directed pivot. GT School / Alpha School currently screen K-8 applicants with the **CogAT** (and have looked at the **CCAT**), both third-party instruments that carry a **~$100 per-student fee** paid to the vendor (Riverside owns CogAT; the "CCAT" line is a separate publisher). We want to **build our own K-8 cognitive-ability / giftedness screener** that (a) removes the third-party fee, and (b) is **~2x better than CogAT** on the dimensions that matter (measurement accuracy, thoroughness of construct coverage, adaptivity, and fairness).

**This doc is the running aggregation** of a recurring background research loop. Each sweep appends findings under the matching research question. Do NOT duplicate prior findings. This is a raw, labeled evidence pile to curate — NOT a design decision or approval to deploy a live test.

**Owner:** Team. **Started:** 2026-07-21. **Related prior work:** `docs/COGAT_GAPS_AND_TEST_SUITE_REPORT.md` (which *external* tests to use), `docs/EVIDENCE_DOSSIER.md` (admissions-overhaul thesis), `docs/HOLISTIC_GIFTEDNESS_EVIDENCE_REPORT.md`.

> **CRITICAL LEGAL/IP NOTE (carry into every sweep):** CogAT, CCAT, NNAT, OLSAT, WISC, etc. are copyrighted. We can research *what constructs they measure and what psychometric properties make them defensible*, and build an original instrument on the same public science (item-response theory, reasoning constructs, norming). We must NOT copy items, item formats verbatim, or normed content. Flag any finding that edges toward copying.

> **Evidence labeling** (keep the discipline even in a fast scrape):
> **[VERIFIED]** peer-reviewed / official technical manual / publisher psychometric doc · **[PRESS]** reputable journalism · **[ESTIMATE]** vendor marketing / advocacy, weaker · **[INFERENCE]** our reasoning · **[UNVERIFIED]** claimed, source not yet confirmed.
> Reliability ≠ validity ≠ fairness. A number defends a claim only if it measures what the claim asserts. "2x better" must be defined on a specific, measurable axis — name it.

## SYNTHESIS — Commonalities: what the best cognitive assessments test for

*Cross-cutting read of all 150 findings (Q1-Q7). This distills where the strongest instruments — CogAT, WISC-V, MAP, Raven's, SMPY above-level testing, validated game-based assessments — and the giftedness literature CONVERGE. Two separate questions: (A) WHAT construct to measure, and (B) HOW to measure it well. The "best" assessment sits at the intersection of both.*

### A. WHAT to measure — the constructs that recur across the best instruments
- **1. Fluid reasoning (Gf) is the non-negotiable core.** Every serious ability test centers on abstract/inductive reasoning — CogAT's Figure Matrices/analogies, Raven's/ICAR matrices, WISC-V fluid index, MAP's reasoning items. It carries the most predictive weight: g dominates achievement prediction (mean r²≈0.54), and Gf adds incremental ΔR²=0.07-0.09 for math even beyond g (findings Q5). *Commonality: measure reasoning on novel material, not learned content.*
- **2. Measure reasoning through MULTIPLE modalities, not one.** The best batteries triangulate the same underlying ability across verbal + quantitative + nonverbal/figural formats (CogAT's 3 batteries × 3 formats; WISC-V's multiple indices; OLSAT verbal+nonverbal). Nonverbal-only tests (NNAT) are demonstrably weaker — ~2× the SEM and they still don't close the equity gap (Q1/Q4). *Commonality: the strongest tests deliberately use several item formats so no single format's noise or bias dominates.*
- **3. Spatial/visualization ability — the highest-value ADDITION most tests underweight.** Spatial ability predicts STEM outcomes (degrees, patents) *beyond* verbal+math and is the "sleeping giant" verbal/quant screens miss (Wai/Lubinski/Benbow, Q5). It recurs as the top candidate for making a test more thorough. *Commonality among the BEST-predicting assessments: they capture spatial reasoning.*
- **4. Working memory / executive function — an independent, achievement-predictive signal.** WM adds unique variance beyond IQ (out-predicted IQ for later achievement in one 6-yr study; n-back/span each add unique Gf variance) and is exactly what the validated children's *games* measure best (CoCon r≈0.45 with WISC WM; MEFS; NIH Toolbox). *Commonality: WM is both predictive and the most game-able construct.*
- **5. Measure at the right ceiling.** For a gifted-range test, the recurring lesson (SMPY above-level testing, MAP's vertical scale, Warne 2014) is that grade-level ceilings flatten the very variance you care about. The best gifted assessments deliberately reach above grade level. *Commonality: high ceiling / above-level content is what separates "measures gifted range" from "everyone maxes out."*
- **6. Complementary NON-reasoning signals broaden coverage — as supplements, not gates.** Creativity/divergent thinking (only r≈.17 with IQ = genuinely new information) and conscientiousness (ρ≈.19-.27, IQ-independent) predict real achievement beyond ability; Renzulli's three-ring model (ability × creativity × task commitment) is the theoretical basis (Q5). *Commonality: the most thorough identification systems are multi-signal — but treat self/teacher-report as inclusionary, never as an exclusionary cutoff (fakeability/rater-bias caveat).*

### B. HOW to measure it well — the quality properties that recur
- **7. High reliability with a small, honest error band.** The bar is composite reliability ~.90+ (CogAT KR-20 .91-.95; WISC-V FSIQ .96; MAP ~.95) and a SEM small enough to make a defensible cut (CogAT SEM ≈3.6-4.8 SAS pts; explicitly NOT NNAT's ~2×). *Commonality: the best tests report a confidence band, not a single number.*
- **8. Cut-scores as confidence bands, not hard lines.** Maine/Florida practice, NAGC, and WISC-V guidance all converge: apply the gifted cut as observed ±1.96 SEM and flag near-threshold students for confirmation. *Commonality: no defensible test treats one score at one sitting as destiny.*
- **9. Adaptivity is the efficiency multiplier.** CAT reaches the same precision with ~50% fewer items by targeting each item near current ability (Q3). It is the single biggest lever for being simultaneously shorter AND more precise than fixed-form CogAT. *Commonality among modern best-in-class tests (MAP, Raven's 2 digital): item-adaptive delivery.*
- **10. Fairness is engineered in, not assumed.** The best instruments run item-level bias analysis (Mantel-Haenszel ETS A/B/C, logistic-regression DIF), minimize construct-irrelevant variance (language, reading load, gaming familiarity), and — the highest-leverage equity move of all — score against LOCAL norms (Peters 2019: +300%/+170% Black/Hispanic identification with no change to the bar). *Commonality: strong tests separate ability from advantage by design.*
- **11. Resistance to coaching/practice inflation.** Practice effects run ~⅓ SD (~5 IQ pts) and DOUBLE with reused forms; even matrices are coachable (Q4). The best defenses: large rotating item banks, novel item types, adaptive delivery, never reusing forms. *Commonality: durable tests don't let a wealthy family's prep purchase a score.*
- **12. Process data (response time, strategy) as a SECONDARY, jointly-modeled signal.** RT can add validity but is ambiguous alone (fast = mastery or disengagement?); the game-based/stealth-assessment literature (Shute ECD, Roblox telemetry) shows telemetry needs an explicit evidence model, and off-task detection, to be trustworthy. *Commonality: behavior is evidence only inside a validity argument.*

### The one-sentence synthesis
**The best cognitive assessment measures fluid reasoning across multiple formats, extended to include spatial ability and working memory, reaching above grade level for the gifted range — delivered adaptively, scored as a confidence band against local norms, engineered against bias and coaching, and (optionally) enriched by well-modeled process data and complementary non-reasoning signals used only to include, never to exclude.**

> **The unavoidable caveat (from Q6 legal/standards findings):** none of these properties transfers automatically to a self-built test. Validity attaches to the *use*, not the instrument — so whatever we build, we must independently assemble the five AERA/APA/NCME validity-evidence sources for OUR K-8 selection use before it drives real admissions decisions.

## Q1 — What are CogAT and CCAT (structure & constructs)

- **[VERIFIED]** CogAT (Riverside Insights) is organized into three batteries — Verbal, Quantitative, Nonverbal — each with three subtests (nine total), using three different subtest formats per battery to increase fairness/validity. Subtests: Verbal = Verbal Classification, Sentence Completion, Verbal Analogies; Quantitative = Number Analogies, Number Series, Number Puzzles; Nonverbal = Figure Matrices, Paper Folding, Figure Classification. (Source: EBSCO Research Starters, "Cognitive Abilities Test (CogAT)" — https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/health-and-medicine/cognitive-abilities-test-cogat)
- **[VERIFIED]** CogAT Form 7 was introduced in 2011, Form 8 in 2017; the forms are parallel/equivalent (same item counts, timing, scoring) allowing immediate retesting by interchanging forms. The line descends from the 1954 Lorge-Thorndike Intelligence Tests. (Source: EBSCO Research Starters — https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/health-and-medicine/cognitive-abilities-test-cogat)
- **[VERIFIED]** CogAT spans ten levels covering K-12. A Primary Edition (K-grade 2, Levels 5/6-8) uses picture-based items for the verbal subtests (no reading required); a Multilevel Edition (Levels 9-17/18) uses text-based verbal items. (Source: EBSCO Research Starters — https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/health-and-medicine/cognitive-abilities-test-cogat)
- **[VERIFIED]** Total administration is ~140-170 min (Primary Edition) and ~145 min (Multilevel); CogAT is a "power" test with generous per-subtest limits rather than strict speeding. (Source: EBSCO Research Starters — https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/health-and-medicine/cognitive-abilities-test-cogat)
- **[PRESS]** At Level 10 (grade 4) and above, each of the nine subtests is allotted 10 minutes, so a full administration runs ~2.5-3 hours including setup. (Source: TestingMom, "CogAT Test" — https://www.testingmom.com/tests/cogat-test/)
- **[PRESS]** The CCAT (Canadian Cognitive Abilities Test, Form 7; publisher Nelson) is the Canadian counterpart to CogAT — same three batteries and nine subtests, normed on Canadian students; Primary Edition ~118 questions, Multilevel Edition ~170 (grade 3) to ~176 (grades 4+) items. (Source: TestingMom "CCAT Test" — https://www.testingmom.com/tests/ccat-test/ ; GiftedReady — https://www.giftedready.com/canadian-cognitive-abilities-test-ccat/)

### Q1 — Sweep 4: competitor benchmarking (the alternatives we must beat)
- **[VERIFIED]** WISC-V (individually administered "gold standard"): FSIQ reliability **0.96**, index reliabilities .88-.93, test-retest .92 — but requires a trained psychologist ~1h15m 1-on-1, which is why it is a *confirmation* tool, not a universal screen. Kit ~$1,300-$1,500 [UNVERIFIED price]; no per-student fee, but the psychologist's administration time is the dominant real cost. (Source: Pearson WISC-V Efficacy Research Report, 2018 — https://www.pearson.com/content/dam/one-dot-com/one-dot-com/global/Files/efficacy-and-research/reports/efficacy-assessment-reports/WISC-V-research-report.pdf)
- **[VERIFIED]** NAGC advises the WISC-V FSIQ should NOT be *required* for gifted/2e ID — any of several indices (GAI, Nonverbal, Verbal, Expanded Fluid) within its CI should suffice. Supports our multi-signal, CI-band design over a single hard composite. (Source: Pearson/NAGC guidance — https://support.pearson.com/usclinical/s/article/WISC-V-Use-with-Gifted-and-Talented-Students)
- **[VERIFIED]** NNAT3 is nonverbal-only (matrix/pattern), K-12, single Naglieri Ability Index; NNAT3 reuses NNAT2 items with only re-normed scores. (Source: Pearson NNAT3 Manual Levels A-D — https://www.pearsonassessments.com/content/dam/school/global/clinical/us/assets/nnat3/nnat3-manual-levels-a-d.pdf)
- **[VERIFIED]** *Key weakness of the leading "equity" screener:* independent research found the NNAT's SEM was roughly **TWICE** that of Raven's or CogAT (median SD 17.2 for ELL children), and its norming overestimates both high and low scorers. (Source: Lohman, Korb & Lakin, *Gifted Child Quarterly*, 2008 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0016986208321808)
- **[VERIFIED]** OLSAT 8 (2003) measures verbal + nonverbal reasoning K-12 → School Ability Index (mean 100, **SD 16** like CogAT, not Wechsler's 15); internal consistency ~0.80-0.90. Package/per-document scoring fees, no public flat per-student price. (Source: Pearson OLSAT 8 — https://www.pearsonassessments.com/en-us/Store/Professional-Assessments/Academic-Learning/Otis-Lennon-School-Ability-Test-|-Eighth-Edition/p/100000003 ; https://www.cogn-iq.org/learn/tests/olsat/)
- **[VERIFIED]** InView (DRC/TerraNova): group-administered, verbal + quantitative reasoning + sequences + analogies, grades 2-12 (K-12 online), 5 subtests → Cognitive Skills Index (mean 100), 2007 norms, online form available; DRC publishes no reliability or per-student pricing publicly. (Source: TerraNova Next / DRC — https://terranovanext.com/inview-cognitive-abilities/)
- **[VERIFIED]** Raven's 2 (2018, Pearson): nonverbal matrix reasoning for g, re-normed for the Flynn effect, offered in paper, digital, AND **adaptive-digital** forms with automated scoring; split-half >0.85 (ages 9+), high-.90s on the digital long form — BUT normative sample is thin (~50-70 per age band). Confirms an adaptive nonverbal g test is commercially viable. (Source: Pearson Raven's 2 — https://www.pearsonassessments.com/en-us/Store/Professional-Assessments/Cognition-&-Neuro/Raven's-Progressive-Matrices-Second-Edition-|-Raven's-2/p/100001960)
- **[VERIFIED]** *No existing screener cleanly solves the equity gap:* Naglieri & Ford (2003) claimed the NNAT identified near-equal proportions (White 5.6%, Black 5.1%, Hispanic 4.4% at SS 125), but Lohman (2005) showed the sample was unrepresentative, and Lohman/Korb/Lakin (2008) + Carman et al. (2020) found BOTH CogAT-Nonverbal and NNAT were LESS likely to identify underrepresented students (ELL Hispanic scoring 6-10 pts lower; nonverbal the weakest achievement predictor, ELL reading r=0.35 vs non-ELL 0.49). (Source: Lohman, *GCQ* 2005 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/001698620504900103 ; Lohman/Korb/Lakin 2008 — http://www.korbedpsych.com/LinkedFiles/Identifying_Academically_Gifted_ELL.pdf)
- **[INFERENCE]** Benchmarking takeaway: our accuracy bar is CogAT/WISC-tier reliability (~.90+ composite, SEM ≤ CogAT's ~3.6-4.8 SAS pts — explicitly NOT the NNAT's ~2× SEM), and the equity bar is unmet by every nonverbal-only competitor — so a *multi-signal + local-norms* design is where we can plausibly beat all of them, not by copying the nonverbal-only "equity" shortcut.

### Q2/Q5 — Sweep 4: coaching, practice effects, and test security (validity + equity threats)
- **[VERIFIED]** Retest/practice effect across cognitive tests (meta-analysis, 174 samples, 122 studies, N=153,185): average gain ~⅓ SD (~5 IQ points) from first to second sitting, rising through the third before plateauing. (Source: Scharfen, Peters & Holling, *Intelligence*, 2018 — https://gwern.net/doc/iq/2018-scharfen.pdf)
- **[VERIFIED]** Reused/identical forms produce ~2× the retest gain of alternate forms; gains grow with shorter intervals and more prior exposures — so form reuse is the single biggest avoidable inflation. (Source: Scharfen et al. 2018 — https://www.cogn-iq.org/learn/theory/practice-effects/)
- **[VERIFIED]** Nonverbal matrices are NOT coach-proof: Raven's APM scores rose across three untrained sittings (F(2,132)=33.67, p<.001; up to ~10-point individual gains), attributed to strategy learning, not item memorization. (Source: Bors & Vigneau, *Learning & Individual Differences*, 2003 — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1041608003000153)
- **[ESTIMATE]** Commercial prep is cheap and targets the exact screeners: TestingMom advertises 100,000+ K-8 practice questions (~$8-$28/mo, ~$120/yr, one plan per family) and vendors bundle CogAT/NNAT2/OLSAT prep marketing "noticeable CogAT score gains" — a direct wealth-linked advantage our design must blunt. (Source: TestingMom/EduReviewer 2026 — https://www.testingmom.com/how-it-works/ ; TestPrep-Online — https://www.testprep-online.com/cogat-practice-tests)
- **[INFERENCE]** Anti-coaching design levers for us: large calibrated item bank, novel/rotating item types, adaptive delivery, and NEVER reusing published forms — directly countering the reused-form doubling effect and the commercial question banks.
- **[VERIFIED]** Reliable computer administration is feasible from ~age 7+: group tablet-based testing of children 7-13 (12 tasks, ~1 hr, self-administered) reached moderate-to-high internal consistency and explained >50% of variance in teacher-rated achievement. (Source: "Collecting big data with small screens," PMC7710155, 2020 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7710155/)
- **[VERIFIED]** Mode effect (paper vs. computer) is small but real and larger for elementary students: K-12 reading meta-analysis d = −0.077 (95% CI [−.094, −.060], slight paper advantage), no significant math mode effect; worse on scrolling/open-ended items. (Source: Wang, Jiao et al., *Educational & Psychological Measurement*, 2007/2008 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0013164406288166)
- **[VERIFIED]** Device matters for young children: touchscreen beats mouse for preschoolers on all selection/manipulation tasks; keyboard/mouse impractical below ~age 3. Implies tablets for K-2. (Source: "Mouse vs. touchscreen for children," Springer — https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/3-540-55578-1_88)
- **[VERIFIED]** CAT item-exposure/security: greedy selection overexposes the best items (leakage risk); Sympson-Hetter (1985) caps exposure (e.g., 20% of test-takers), and a 2026 simulation on a 160-item abstract-reasoning bank found Progressive-Restricted gave lowest exposure + fullest bank use without precision loss. (Source: Assessment Systems — https://assess.com/sympson-hetter-item-exposure-control/ ; *Frontiers in Education* 2026 — https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2026.1769909/full)
- **[INFERENCE]** Digital-delivery design rules: touchscreen tablets for K-2, every item fully on-screen (no scrolling), validate paper-vs-digital comparability before any mode switch (the d≈−0.08 elementary effect could shift a gifted cut), and build exposure control (Sympson-Hetter/Progressive-Restricted) into the CAT engine from day one.

### Q5 — Sweep 5: complementary NON-reasoning signals (the "more thorough than CogAT" case)
- **[VERIFIED]** TTCT is the most-used creativity measure (2,000+ studies, 35 languages); Torrance's longitudinal work found childhood TTCT predicted adult creative achievement at r≈.2-.3 over 40 years — comparable to or better than IQ for the same outcomes. (Source: Kim, *Frontiers in Psychology*, 2022 — https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.1000385/full)
- **[VERIFIED]** In Kim's (2008) meta-analysis (274 correlations), divergent thinking predicted real-world creative achievement (r=.22) better than IQ (r=.17); TTCT specifically r=.33. The IQ-creativity correlation is only r≈.17 — creativity is a largely distinct construct. (Source: Kim, *J Creative Behavior*, 2008 — https://www.researchgate.net/publication/237792387)
- **[VERIFIED]** Threshold hypothesis (creativity-IQ link below ~IQ 120, flat above) is empirically contested: Jauk et al. (2013) found breakpoints as low as IQ 104 for creative *potential* but NO threshold for creative *achievement*; a German sample (n=1,328) found no support. (Source: Jauk et al., *Intelligence*, 2013 — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S016028961300024X)
- **[VERIFIED]** Grit (Duckworth 2007) added ~4% incremental variance in success outcomes over IQ and conscientiousness — BUT Credé et al. (2016) meta-analysis found grit largely redundant with conscientiousness (only "perseverance of effort" facet adds ~4%). Treat grit skeptically as a distinct signal. (Source: Duckworth, *JPSP*, 2007 — https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/personality/conscientiousness/2007-duckworth.pdf ; Credé 2016 via *Front. Psych.* 2020)
- **[VERIFIED]** Conscientiousness is the strongest Big Five predictor of achievement (corrected ρ≈.19-.27; Poropat 2009, n>70,000), predicting largely independently of IQ; in one Austrian sample IQ + conscientiousness together explained ~40% of school-achievement variance. (Source: Poropat, *Psychological Bulletin*, 2009 — https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/personality/conscientiousness/2021-mammadov.pdf)
- **[VERIFIED]** Renzulli's Three-Ring Conception (above-average ability × creativity × task commitment) is the foundational theoretical basis for multi-signal gifted ID, explicitly rejecting reasoning-only identification. (Source: Renzulli, UConn/ERIC ED249728 — https://gifted.uconn.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/961/2021/05/The_Three-Ring_Conception_of_Giftedness.pdf)
- **[VERIFIED]** Renzulli-Hartman SRBCSS teacher checklist (14 domains) has published construct validity but reviews flag nonrepresentative norms, low interrater reliability, and uneven subscale psychometrics. (Source: SRBCSS Technical Manual, ERIC ED461245 — https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED461245)
- **[VERIFIED]** HOPE Teacher Rating Scale (11-13 items, built to surface low-income/diverse gifted): NO DIF by income or ethnicity (only gender) across 12,000+ students in five studies, and accounted for ~52-58% of achievement-test variance — equitable across income groups. (Source: Peters & Gentry, *Gifted Child Quarterly*, 2013 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0016986212469253)
- **[VERIFIED]** Dynamic assessment (test-teach-retest of learning potential) is proposed to reveal latent ability static IQ tests underestimate in disadvantaged/ELL students; a Spanish study (n=127, ages 6-11) found it discriminated high- from average-IQ children with high predictive power — but validated on non-disadvantaged children, so targeted validity for disadvantaged groups remains a gap. (Source: Calero et al., *Learning & Individual Differences*, 2011 — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1041608010001676)
- **[INFERENCE]** To be genuinely "2× more thorough" than CogAT, add ≥2 near-orthogonal-to-reasoning signals — figural divergent thinking (TTCT-style, r≈.17 with IQ = real incremental info) and a teacher behavioral checklist (HOPE, which is DIF-clean) — since reasoning alone captures only part of what predicts creative/academic achievement.
- **[INFERENCE]** Weight self/teacher-report against a fakeability tradeoff: grit/conscientiousness self-reports are coachable/fakeable in high-stakes K-8 selection (and grit ≈ conscientiousness); teacher ratings carry rater bias/low interrater reliability. Use these as INCLUSIONARY supplements (to catch students tests miss), NOT exclusionary gates; prefer performance-based/dynamic tasks over questionnaires where feasible (harder to game).

## Q2 — What makes them "accurate" (reliability, validity, norming)

- **[VERIFIED]** The Standard Age Score (SAS) is a normalized standard score with mean = 100, SD = 16, reported per battery and for the composite; age norms cover ages 4 yr 11 mo to 21 yr 7 mo. (Source: EBSCO Research Starters — https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/health-and-medicine/cognitive-abilities-test-cogat)
- **[VERIFIED]** KR-20 internal-consistency reliabilities run in the high 0.90s for Verbal and Nonverbal and low 0.90s for Quantitative; across the standardization sample composite reliability estimates ranged .91-.95 (Verbal), .91-.94 (Nonverbal), .91-.94 (Quantitative). (Source: EBSCO Research Starters — https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/health-and-medicine/cognitive-abilities-test-cogat)
- **[VERIFIED]** CogAT Form 7 was co-normed with the Iowa Assessments (Form E) on the same population, enabling direct ability-achievement comparison; norms available for 2011 or 2017 norm year (2017 is scoring default). (Source: Riverside Insights, CogAT 7/8 Complete Norms & Score Conversions Guide — https://onlinehelp.riversideinsights.com/Help/Elevate/assets/docs/CogAT_78_COMP_NandSC_Guide_091820.pdf)
- **[VERIFIED]** Validity evidence includes confirmatory factor analysis plus convergent correlations with WISC-V and Woodcock-Johnson IV Cognitive (WJ-IV COG), and strong correlation with the co-normed Iowa achievement tests (ITBS/ITED). (Source: EBSCO Research Starters — https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/health-and-medicine/cognitive-abilities-test-cogat)
- **[VERIFIED]** *(Sweep 3 — confirms the prior UNVERIFIED figure)* CogAT Form 7 standardization sample = **65,350 U.S. K-12 students**, "representing all areas and ethnic groups," standardized 2011 (Lohman/Riverside) — confirmed by an independent peer-reviewed test review. (Source: Warne, R.T., "Test Review: Cognitive Abilities Test, Form 7," *Journal of Psychoeducational Assessment* 33(2):188-192, 2015 — https://doi.org/10.1177/0734282914548324)
- **[VERIFIED]** *(Sweep 3)* Independent peer-reviewed internal-consistency reliabilities (standardization sample): Verbal composite .91-.95, Nonverbal .91-.94, Quantitative .91-.94 — matching the vendor KR-20 ranges. (Source: Warne 2015, *J Psychoeducational Assessment* — https://doi.org/10.1177/0734282914548324)
- **[VERIFIED]** *(Sweep 3)* Meta-analytic criterion-related validity: CogAT correlates with criterion instruments at weighted mean **r = .63, 95% CI [.57, .69]** (24 studies, 33 effect sizes, screened from 1,480) — higher than NNAT's comparable r = .44. This is the independent benchmark our instrument's validity coefficient must approach or beat. (Source: Ozen, Pereira, Karatas, Castillo-Hermosilla & Maeda, "A Meta-Analytic Evaluation... Validity of the Cognitive Abilities Test," *Gifted Child Quarterly*, 2025 — https://doi.org/10.1177/00169862241285593)
- **[INFERENCE]** *(Sweep 3)* No discrete CogAT SEM is published in any accessible independent source; derived from SD=16 and reliability .91-.95, SEM = SD·√(1−r) ≈ **3.6-4.8 SAS points**. This is a derived estimate (a defensible target for our own SEM), not a cited value. (Source: derivation from EBSCO SD=16 + Warne 2015 reliability)
- **[VERIFIED]** *(Sweep 3)* Independent Buros MMY reviews of CogAT exist and are favorable ("an excellent K-12 ability test battery… large, representative normative sample… good evidence for reliability and validity"), though Warne separately flagged published *validity* evidence as "limited." Prior-form review: Rogers, B.G., "Review of CogAT Form 6," *16th Mental Measurements Yearbook*, 2005; Form 7 in 21st MMY (2021). Specific SEM numbers inside are paywalled. ## Q3 — How they gauge adaptability during the test (CAT / adaptive mechanics)

- **[VERIFIED]** The CAT cycle re-estimates ability (maximum likelihood or Bayesian) after each response and selects the next item via the IRT item information function — "maximum information" picks the item with maximum Fisher information at the current ability estimate; Bayesian selection minimizes expected posterior variance. (Source: Song, *Korean J Med Educ* review, 2017 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5676016/)
- **[VERIFIED]** CAT test length averages ~50% shorter than paper-and-pencil at equal precision; multidimensional CAT with content balancing achieves equal precision with 25-40% fewer items than unidimensional CAT. (Source: Song, PMC review, 2017 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5676016/)
- **[VERIFIED]** Stopping rules fall in two families: variable-length (terminate when the SE of the ability estimate / posterior variance drops below a threshold) and fixed-length/classification (continue until the ability CI clears a cut-score or max item count is hit). (Source: Song, PMC review, 2017 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5676016/)
- **[VERIFIED]** Exposure control (avoid overusing items) includes randomly selecting the first few items from a subset of the bank. (Source: Song, PMC review, 2017 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5676016/)
- **[VERIFIED]** Response latency is modeled with van der Linden's lognormal response-time model (2006), enabling speededness control; in adaptive testing, shadow-test constraints kept total testing time within <10 seconds of a reference test across conditions. (Source: van der Linden & Xiong, *J Educ & Behav Stat* 38(4):418-438, 2013 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/1076998612466143 ; van der Linden, JEBS 31:181-204, 2006)
- **[VERIFIED]** MAP Growth (NWEA) is built on the Rasch (1-parameter IRT) model and a vertical RIT scale (~100-350); it selects each item for maximum information, targeting the difficulty where the student has ~50% chance correct, re-adjusts after each response, and terminates when SE falls below a set value, final RIT via maximum-likelihood. (Source: NWEA MAP Growth Technical Report, 2019 — https://www.nwea.org/uploads/2021/11/MAP-Growth-Technical-Report-2019_NWEA.pdf ; NWEA RIT help — https://teach.mapnwea.org/impl/maphelp/Content/AboutMAP/WhatRITMeans.htm)
- **[INFERENCE]** The paper/standard CogAT is NOT adaptive — fixed forms by level/grade — whereas MAP Growth adapts item-by-item; the trade-off is that CAT reaches a target SE with far fewer items because every item is targeted near current ability rather than spread across a fixed difficulty range. **Design implication: an adaptive in-house test is the single biggest lever for beating CogAT on efficiency + precision simultaneously.**

## Q5 — How to make ours 2x better (what to test + how to test it)

- **[VERIFIED]** Spatial ability at age 13 predicts STEM degrees, patents, and technical publications with incremental validity beyond SAT-V/SAT-M; in ~400,000 students tracked 11 years it predicted STEM accomplishment at 38 even after partialling verbal/math talent — a "sleeping giant" missed by verbal/quant-only screens. (Source: Wai, Lubinski & Benbow, 2009, *J Educ Psychology* 101(4):817-835 — https://cdn.vanderbilt.edu/t2-my/my-prd/wp-content/uploads/sites/826/2013/02/Wai2009SpatialAbility.pdf)
- **[VERIFIED]** Across CHC studies g dominates (mean r² ≈ 0.54 with achievement), and specific abilities' incremental R² beyond g is usually small — but a 2024 study found Reasoning (Gf) added ΔR² = 0.07-0.09 for math grades and Verbal added ΔR² = 0.05-0.09 for language grades, so a few specific factors carry real incremental signal. (Source: specific-abilities incremental-validity study, *Intelligence*, 2024 — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160289624000102)
- **[VERIFIED]** Working memory adds unique variance beyond IQ; in a 6-year longitudinal study WM at school entry out-predicted IQ for later achievement, and n-back/complex-span factors each explained unique Gf variance (24% and 14%) beyond shared variance (30%). (Source: National Academies, *Measuring Human Capabilities*, 2015 — https://nap.nationalacademies.org/read/19017/chapter/6)
- **[VERIFIED]** In IRT, SE(θ) = 1/√(test information); a stopping rule of SE = 0.32 corresponds to reliability 0.90. CAT reaches target precision with far fewer items by selecting maximally-informative items near current θ. (Source: Choi et al., "A New Stopping Rule for CAT," PMC3028267 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3028267/)
- **[INFERENCE]** Because SE ∝ 1/√(information), **halving SEM requires ~4x the test information** (~4x items on a fixed form). CAT partly evades this by raising information-per-item. Practical rule: frame "2x better" per-axis (e.g., "SEM halved in the 90th-98th percentile decision band"), NOT globally, and achieve it via adaptivity, not brute test length.
- **[VERIFIED]** Universal 2nd-grade nonverbal screening (cut 130, or 116 disadvantaged/ELL) raised gifted ID with no standards change: ~180% (disadvantaged), ~130% (Hispanic), ~80% (Black); traditional referral under-referred Black, Hispanic, FRL, ELL, and female students. (Source: Card & Giuliano, *PNAS* 2016, 113(48):13678-13683 — https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1605043113) *(cross-ref EVIDENCE_DOSSIER C4; here it is the strongest fairness lever for our design)*
- **[VERIFIED]** Nonverbal screening reduces but does not eliminate subgroup gaps: Naglieri & Ford (2003, N≈20,000) found similar ≥125 rates for White (5.6%), Black (5.1%), Hispanic (4.4%); Giessman et al. (2013) found CogAT6 Nonverbal produced smaller gaps than NNAT2; a 2021 meta-analysis still found students of color underrepresented. (Source: Naglieri & Ford 2003, *Gifted Child Quarterly* 47(2) — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/001698620304700206 ; Giessman et al. 2013 — https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ996235)
- **[VERIFIED]** Response-time process data can add predictive validity beyond accuracy (shown for reading comprehension) but only when RT and accuracy are modeled jointly to handle the speed-accuracy trade-off; authors call the evidence "suggestive," needing larger-scale confirmation. (Source: "Use of Response Time for Measuring Cognitive Ability," *J Intelligence* 4(4):14, 2016 — https://www.mdpi.com/2079-3200/4/4/14)
- **[INFERENCE] Design blueprint for a measurably-better screener:** (1) add spatial/visualization + a WM/Gf task to the verbal/quant/nonverbal core (documented incremental validity); (2) deliver via CAT to cut SEM in the decision band; (3) apply universal screening with local/subgroup norms (largest, best-evidenced equity lever — Card & Giuliano); (4) treat RT/process data as a secondary, jointly-modeled signal, not a primary score.

### Q5 — Sweep 2: concrete, buildable K-8 task types (public-domain vs. copyrighted)
- **[VERIFIED]** ICAR (International Cognitive Ability Resource) is a public-domain item bank: Matrix Reasoning = 11 Raven's-style 3×3 items (α ≈ 0.79), 3D Rotation α = 0.93; 16-item short form α = 0.81, 60-item α = 0.94; full ICAR corrected r = 0.75 with Raven's APM. **FREE/public-domain — usable seed for our matrix + rotation subtests.** (Source: Condon & Revelle, *Intelligence* 43:52-64, 2014 — https://www.personality-project.org/revelle/publications/condon.icar.14.pdf ; catalogue https://icar-project.com/ICAR_Catalogue.pdf)
- **[VERIFIED]** A Children's ICAR (Ch-ICAR) has been developed and validated for youth, extending the public-domain matrix/reasoning items to younger ages — directly relevant to K-8. (Source: *Behavior Research Methods*, 2024 — https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13428-024-02591-1)
- **[VERIFIED]** Raven's Coloured Progressive Matrices (fluid reasoning) targets ages ~5¼-11½, split-half reliability ~0.81-0.91 (Omani norms, N=1,042), r=0.405 with achievement. **COPYRIGHTED (Pearson)** — use ICAR/Ch-ICAR for free matrix items instead. (Source: ERIC EJ1157840 — https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1157840.pdf)
- **[VERIFIED]** Mental Folding Test for Children (MFTC; Harris et al., 2013) is a validated paper-folding spatial task for ages 4-7 (mental folding reliably emerges ~5.5 yrs) — covers the K-2 spatial floor. (Source: Harris et al., *Mind, Brain, and Education*, 2013 — https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/mbe.12007)
- **[VERIFIED]** Mental rotation is reliably measurable in 6-9-year-olds; a 2023 study (N=96) of four MR tasks found a single factor (good construct validity), α acceptable-to-excellent (paper-pencil > computer-based). (Source: Krüger et al., *J Intelligence* 11(8):165, 2023 — https://www.mdpi.com/2079-3200/11/8/165)
- **[VERIFIED]** Corsi Block-Tapping (visuospatial WM span) was standardized ages 11-16 (N=1,122) against WISC-R; forward = short-term memory, backward = working memory. Method is FREE/public (open PsyToolkit/Inquisit versions); only branded norms are copyrighted. Caveat: administration/scoring lacks cross-study standardization. (Source: Orsini, *Perceptual & Motor Skills* 79:1547, 1994 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.2466/pms.1994.79.3f.1547)

- **[INFERENCE]** Buildable K-8 battery from public-domain/open tasks: matrix reasoning (ICAR/Ch-ICAR), 2D/3D rotation (ICAR 3D Rotation), paper folding (MFTC K-2, PFT upper grades), Corsi span, backward digit span, 1-3-back. Only branded norm-referenced instruments (Raven's, WISC digit/Corsi norms) are copyrighted — the *tasks* themselves are free to reimplement with original stimuli.

### Q5 — Sweep 2: fairness / DIF methodology (defensibility pipeline)
- **[VERIFIED]** ETS delta-scale Mantel-Haenszel DIF classification: ΔMH = −2.35 × ln(αMH), flagged as A (negligible, |ΔMH|<1.0 → retain), B (moderate, 1.0≤|ΔMH|<1.5 + significant χ² → content review), C (large, |ΔMH|≥1.5 + significance → revise/remove). Requires BOTH significance and effect size, preventing large-sample false alarms. (Source: Dorans & Holland 1993 / Zwick & Ercikan 1989, ETS review — https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1109842.pdf)
- **[VERIFIED]** The ΔMH=1.0 (B) threshold corresponds to a common odds ratio αMH ≈ 1.53 — one group ~1.53× as likely (in odds) to answer correctly — a concrete reporting interpretation. (Source: "Refining Effect-Size Measures for DIF," arXiv — https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.21531)
- **[VERIFIED]** Zumbo's (1999) logistic-regression DIF framework detects both uniform (group term) and non-uniform (group×ability interaction) DIF via a 3-step nested R² comparison; Jodoin & Gierl (2001) R² cutoffs map to A/B/C letters (R²<0.035=A, 0.035-0.070=B, >0.070=C). Logistic regression inflates Type I error, so the effect-size gate (not just significance) is essential. (Source: Zumbo, UBC — https://faculty.educ.ubc.ca/zumbo/talks/Zumbo_ITC.PDF ; Jodoin & Gierl summary — https://www.cogn-iq.org/learn/theory/differential-item-functioning/)
- **[VERIFIED]** The AERA/APA/NCME *Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing* (2014) devote a dedicated foundational chapter to fairness, defining it as valid score interpretation "for all examinees" and requiring minimization of construct-irrelevant variance by culture, language, gender, disability, and SES — the authoritative standard our instrument must cite. (Source: APA — https://www.apa.org/science/programs/testing/standards ; NCME — https://ncme.org/resources/books/testing-standards/)
- **[VERIFIED]** *Local norms are the highest-leverage equity lever:* Peters et al. (2019, 3rd-graders, 10 states, 10 years) found switching from national to building-level norms raised Black representation ~300% (math)/238% (reading) and Hispanic ~170% (math)/157% (reading), with White/Asian identification falling correspondingly — without changing the construct bar. (Source: Peters, Rambo-Hernandez, Makel, Matthews & Plucker, *AERA Open*, 2019 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/2332858419848446)
- **[INFERENCE]** Two-stage defensibility pipeline for our build: (1) run every item through Mantel-Haenszel with ETS A/B/C flagging (drop/revise C, review B), cross-checked with Zumbo logistic-regression R² for non-uniform DIF; (2) score against **building-level local norms**, not national norms (the single highest-leverage equity lever, 300%/170% subgroup gains). Requires collecting group-membership variables (race, SES, EL, sex) at pilot so DIF is computable.

## Q6 — Feasibility, cost, IP & operational reality

- **[VERIFIED]** Copyright protects the specific expression of test items, NOT the underlying construct or psychometric methods (idea-expression distinction); IRT and CAT are unpatentable methods/theory — so we can freely use them and write original items measuring the same constructs CogAT measures. (Source: Campbell, "'Testing' the Copyright Clause," 69 Neb. L. Rev., 1990 — https://digitalcommons.unl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1762&context=nlr ; APA test-security FAQ — https://www.apa.org/science/programs/testing/test-security-faq)
- **[VERIFIED]** Public-domain item resources can seed a bank without infringement: ICAR (International Cognitive Ability Resource) is a public-domain cognitive-ability item pool (Condon & Revelle, 2014, *Intelligence* 43:52-64); IPIP offers 3,000+ non-cognitive items — both explicitly non-copyrighted. (Source: Condon & Revelle 2014 — https://www.sciencedirect.com/ ; IPIP — https://ipip.ori.org/)
- **[VERIFIED]** Mature open-source IRT/CAT tooling is free: R packages mirt/mirtCAT (Chalmers 2016, *J Stat Software* 71(5); uni- and multidimensional CAT, Shiny, shadow-testing) and catR (Magis & Raîche; rich item-selection/stopping rules), plus the Concerto CAT platform (Univ. of Cambridge) for deployment. (Source: Chalmers 2016 — https://www.jstatsoft.org/article/view/v071i05 ; mirtCAT CRAN — https://cran.r-project.org/package=mirtCAT)

(Source: Education Week, 2012 — https://www.edweek.org/teaching-learning/standardized-testing-costs-states-1-7-billion-a-year-study-says/2012/11)
- **[INFERENCE] Feasibility read:** items + IRT/CAT engine are legally and technically cheap (original items, ICAR seed, mirtCAT/catR/Concerto — near-zero licensing). The binding constraint — and the bulk of what the ~$100 CogAT fee buys — is a secure, nationally representative, validated **norm**. A credible "2x better" replacement must budget primarily for norming + DIF/fairness + multi-year validation, and can realistically **start with defensible local norms** (per Card & Giuliano) while building toward national norms.

### Q6 — Sweep 2: norming sample sizes, cost anchors, and build-your-own precedents
- **[VERIFIED]** Norming N is set by tolerable sampling error: practical floor ~100 examinees per grade/age band for stable central tendency, 200+ for a commercially defensible test, and much larger to pin the extreme percentiles (1st/5th/95th/99th) that gifted cut-scores depend on. A well-matched N=500 beats a poorly representative N=2,000. (Source: Crocker & Algina framework / LEADERS Project — https://www.leadersproject.org/2013/03/01/understanding-the-normative-sample/ ; PMC7885019 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7885019/)

(Source: Workforce.com — https://workforce.com/news/estimating-the-financial-value-of-staffing-assessment-tools)
- **[VERIFIED]** *Precedent — a university-built, validated, FREE screener at modest N:* the HOPE Teacher Rating Scale (Peters & Gentry, Purdue GERI), an 11-item equity-focused K-12 screener, was validated on ~5,995 students / ~350 teachers, revised on ~1,700 students across 3 districts, reaching α=.96 with measurement invariance across low-income/diverse groups; funded by the Jack Kent Cooke Foundation and released as a free download. (Source: Peters & Gentry, *Gifted Child Quarterly* 54:298-313, 2010 — https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1049831 ; Purdue GERI — https://purduegeri.wixsite.com/instrument/hope)

(Source: Duke AAGC — https://sites.duke.edu/aagc/bright-idea-1/ ; ERIC ED606974 — https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED606974.pdf)

### Q6 — Sweep 5: LEGAL & PROFESSIONAL-STANDARDS RISK (building our own shifts validation liability onto us)
> **This is the highest-stakes finding of the whole project.** Replacing CogAT means WE become the test author and must independently prove validity for a high-stakes use involving children. Read before any go-decision.
- **[VERIFIED]** The AERA/APA/NCME *Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing* (2014) define validity as "the degree to which evidence and theory support the interpretations of test scores for proposed uses" — validity attaches to the *use*, not the test; Ch. 1 requires a validity argument assembled for each intended use before consequential decisions. (Source: AERA/APA/NCME 2014 — https://www.aera.net/publications/books/standards-for-educational-psychological-testing-2014-edition)
- **[VERIFIED]** The 2014 Standards enumerate FIVE required sources of validity evidence for a high-stakes program: (1) test content, (2) response processes, (3) internal structure, (4) relations to other variables, (5) consequences of testing — a cumulative, argument-based process, not one study. (Source: AERA/APA/NCME 2014 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standards_for_Educational_and_Psychological_Testing)
- **[VERIFIED]** APA Ethics Code 9.02 requires assessments be used only for purposes supported by validity/reliability evidence *for the population tested* (and limitations described where not established); 9.05 requires developers use appropriate psychometric procedures "for test design, standardization, validation, reduction or elimination of bias." (Source: APA Ethical Principles — https://www.apa.org/ethics/code)
- **[VERIFIED]** *Larry P. v. Riles*, 495 F. Supp. 926 (N.D. Cal. 1979): IQ tests could NOT be used to place Black students in EMR classes because not validated for that specific purpose/population and producing discriminatory effect (Title VI, Rehabilitation Act, EAHCA) — the court put the burden of proving validity on the test USER. (Source: Civil Rights Litigation Clearinghouse — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_P._v._Riles)
- **[VERIFIED]** *PASE v. Hannon*, 506 F. Supp. 831 (N.D. Ill. 1980) reached the OPPOSITE result on similar facts (examined items individually, found most unbiased) — showing courts scrutinize validity/bias item-by-item and outcomes are fact-specific and unpredictable. (Source: Justia — https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp/506/831/1654128/)
- **[VERIFIED]** *GI Forum v. TEA*, 87 F. Supp. 2d 667 (W.D. Tex. 2000) upheld a high-stakes test despite disparate impact because the state proved "educational necessity": manifest test-goal relationship, curricular validity, reliability, adherence to professional standards, and notice/opportunity to learn — cited as the "road map" for a legally defensible high-stakes test. (Source: Justia — https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp2/87/667/2307263/)
- **[VERIFIED]** EEOC Uniform Guidelines on Employee Selection Procedures (1978, 29 CFR Part 1607): any selection procedure with adverse impact must be justified by documented validity evidence AND consideration of less-discriminatory alternatives — the federal analog courts import into education testing cases. (Source: eCFR — https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-XIV/part-1607)
- **[VERIFIED]** NAGC requires ALL gifted-ID instruments — checklists, nomination forms, AND assessments — be "valid, reliable, and culturally and linguistically sensitive," with no single test at one time dictating identification; a locally built, unvalidated sole/primary screen runs directly counter to this. (Source: NAGC position statement — https://cdn.ymaws.com/portal.nagc.org/resource/resmgr/knowledge-center/position-statements/Identifying_and_Serving_Cult.pdf)
- **[VERIFIED]** Data-privacy: COPPA (16 CFR Part 312) requires verifiable parental consent to collect data from under-13s (school-authorization exception is educational-purpose-only, no commercial/secondary use); FERPA (34 CFR Part 99) treats school-held cognitive test data as protected "education records," barring third-party-vendor disclosure absent consent or the school-official/contractor exception. (Source: US DoE Student Privacy — https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/ferpa)
- **[INFERENCE]** Building our own instrument transfers the FULL validation burden onto us: under the 2014 Standards Ch. 1 we must independently assemble all five validity-evidence sources FOR THE SPECIFIC USE (K-8 admissions/gifted selection) before any real decision — the exact evidence a vendor already documents and that *Larry P.* found dispositive when absent.
- **[INFERENCE]** "Validation is for a use, not a test": borrowing items/norms from a validated instrument does NOT transfer validity to our new use. Combined case law (*Larry P.* burden + *GI Forum* road map) means if our test shows disparate impact by race/sex/ELL/disability, WE (not a vendor) must produce a documented validity/educational-necessity argument and evidence that less-discriminatory alternatives were considered — or face Title VI/EEOC-analog liability. **The ~$100 CogAT fee is partly insurance: it buys someone else's validation liability. Removing it means budgeting for our own defensible validity study.**

- **[INFERENCE]** Cost/feasibility synthesis: a defensible in-house screener does NOT require CogAT's national norming budget up front. A staged path — original items (ICAR-seeded) + open CAT engine + building-level local norms + Mantel-Haenszel/logistic DIF screening, piloted at ~100-200/grade — is achievable in the ~$60k-$500k band and mirrors HOPE/Bright IDEA precedents, while national norms remain a later, optional scale-up.

### Q6 — Sweep 3: concrete pilot-design parameters (calibration N, bank size, stop rules, cut-score)
- **[VERIFIED]** IRT calibration sample rules of thumb: Rasch/1PL ~200 examinees estimate item difficulty to ±0.5 logits (Linacre: 30 exploratory min, 50 for ±1 logit at 99%, 250 polytomous); 2PL ~500; 3PL ~1,000 (De Ayala), with simulation work arguing 2,000-3,000+ for high-precision 3PL. (Source: Linacre, *Rasch Measurement Transactions* — https://www.rasch.org/rmt/rmt74m.htm ; Schroeders et al., *AMPPS* 2025 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/25152459251314798)
- **[VERIFIED]** Rasch precision formula ties N to target SE: 4/SE² < N < 9/SE², so an item SE of 0.20 logits needs ~100-225 examinees. (Source: Linacre — https://www.rasch.org/rmt/rmt74m.htm)
- **[VERIFIED]** CAT item-bank floor ≈ 3× the intended test length (e.g., ~60 calibrated items for a 20-item CAT), larger with content-balancing/exposure control or measurement at extreme ability. (Source: Assessment Systems / ASC — https://assess.com/computerized-adaptive-testing/)
- **[VERIFIED]** CAT stop rule for 0.90 reliability = SE(θ) ≤ 0.316 on the theta metric (0.387 = .85, 0.447 = .80), via Reliability = 1 − SE². Operational pediatric CATs (PROMIS/NIH Toolbox) use combined rules, e.g., SE(θ) < 0.40 OR a 12-item cap. (Source: *Perspectives on Medical Education* 2023 — https://pmejournal.org/articles/10.5334/pme.855 ; NIH Toolbox, PMC11951945 — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11951945/)
- **[VERIFIED]** Test length trades against SE nonlinearly: because SE = 1/√information, halving SE (0.5→0.15 logits) needs ~10× the items — the quantitative form of the "2× better" caution. (Source: Linacre, *RMT* — https://www.rasch.org/rmt/rmt202f.htm)
- **[VERIFIED]** Field-test seeding inflates the total sample: guaranteeing 1,000 / 500 / 250 valid responses per pretest item required total samples of ~11,000 / 6,000 / 3,000 (Rasch). Small-sample calibration is still feasible — fixed-item-parameter calibration (FIPC) gave stable parameters at n=250/group in PISA 2015. (Source: *GJHSS* — https://globaljournals.org/GJHSS_Volume15/8-CAT-Field-Test-Item-Calibration.pdf ; Guo, ETS RR 2024 — https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ets2.12376)
- **[VERIFIED]** Regression-based/continuous norming needs 2.5-5.5× smaller samples per age subgroup than traditional binned norming for equal precision — the cost-efficient choice across 9 K-8 grades. (Source: Oosterhuis, van der Ark & Sijtsma, *Assessment*, 2016 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1073191115580638)
- **[VERIFIED]** Gifted cut-scores should be SEM confidence bands, not hard lines: Maine defines eligibility as 2 SD above mean MINUS the SEM; Florida requires the SEM be applied symmetrically. Read a score as observed ±1 SEM (68%) or ±1.96 SEM (95%). (Source: Maine DOE — https://www.maine.gov/doe/sites/maine.gov.doe/files/inline-files/Combined-Factsheet-for-Identification-November-2014.pdf ; Florida DOE — https://www.fldoe.org/core/fileparse.php/7567/urlt/y1996-7.pdf)
- **[INFERENCE] STRAW-MAN PILOT for our build:** (1) **Model:** Rasch/1PL per subtest → each item needs only ~200-250 valid responses (vs. 500-1,000 for 2PL/3PL). (2) **Sample:** spiraled field-test booklets, ~N≈500-800 children/grade to net ~250 responses/item after seeding inflation. (3) **Bank:** ~36 calibrated items per construct (3× a 12-item CAT cap), weighted to the upper tail since gifted screening measures at extreme θ. (4) **Stop rule:** SE(θ) ≤ 0.316 (reliability .90) OR 12-item cap. (5) **Cut-score:** apply the 95th-percentile gift cut as a 95% SEM band (observed ±1.96 SEM), flagging near-threshold children for confirmatory testing — matching Maine/Florida defensible practice. (6) **Norms:** regression-based continuous norming (smaller N) + building-level local norms for equity.

## Q7 — Game-based / gamified cognitive assessment

### Q7 — Sweep 6: the Roblox Kaiju Cats precedent (primary sources)
- **[PRESS/VERIFIED]** Roblox built and operationalized a **game-based hiring assessment in 2021** for entry-level software-engineer and PM roles, designed to measure higher-order cognitive skills — "creative problem-solving and systems thinking" — rather than a narrow candidate profile; it scores the *observed decision-making process*, not just outcomes ("no right or wrong answers"). (Source: Roblox Newsroom, "Fair Play: Roblox's Game-Based Talent Assessment," 2025 — https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/07/fair-play-robloxs-game-based-talent-assessment)
- **[PRESS]** "Kaiju Cats" is the **practice** assessment Roblox launched in 2023 (not the scored instrument) — it mirrors the real assessment's UI, tutorial, and real-time timer to reduce format novelty, leveraging evidence that practice tests improve both candidate and organizational outcomes. (Source: Roblox Newsroom, 2025 — https://about.roblox.com/newsroom/2025/07/fair-play-robloxs-game-based-talent-assessment)
- **[VERIFIED]** The Roblox assessment and its telemetry are documented in a peer-reviewed venue: an EDM 2024 paper analyzes the large-scale, game-based practice assessment and the problem of **identifying off-task users** from behavioral/process data — evidence that gameplay telemetry is the measurement signal and that data quality (off-task/disengaged play) is a known challenge. (Source: Educational Data Mining 2024 proceedings, "Identifying Off-Task Users in a Large-Scale, Game-Based Practice Assessment" — https://educationaldatamining.org/edm2024/proceedings/2024.EDM-posters.96/index.html)
- **[PRESS]** Candidate accounts of the *scored* assessment: ~70 min, building robots/cars from parts to traverse an obstacle course (generate as many working solutions as possible) and optimizing a factory-network revenue simulation — open-ended, hard to cram for, emphasizing solution generation over a single correct answer. (Source: Exponent Roblox interview guide, 2026 — https://www.tryexponent.com/guides/roblox-software-engineer-interview ; candidate reports via TeamBlind) — treat as anecdotal, not official.
- **[INFERENCE]** The Roblox model maps cleanly onto our goals: it (a) measures process/decision-making (harder to coach than fixed items), (b) uses a free *practice* game to remove UI novelty (a fairness move — equalizing format familiarity), and (c) targets creative problem-solving/systems thinking, i.e., constructs beyond CogAT's verbal/quant/nonverbal reasoning. BUT it is a hiring tool for adults with no published validity coefficients — adopting the *format* for K-8 requires independent validity evidence (see Q6 legal risk).

### Q7 — Sweep 6: game-based cognitive assessment in CHILDREN (validity, anxiety, fairness)
- **[VERIFIED]** ACE-X (UCSF Neuroscape mobile executive-function game): test-retest ICC = 0.45-0.79 (p<.001) in a validation cohort of 6,052 (89.8% children/adolescents, mean age 12.78); concurrent validity vs. the Inquisit lab battery was MIXED (r = −.05 to .62). (Source: Laughlin et al., *JMIR*, 2025 — https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e60041)
- **[VERIFIED]** Systematic review/meta-analysis of game-based cognitive assessments in children/adolescents (19 articles, 20 studies, 18 games, 378 correlations): 75% of game-vs-traditional correlations were significant, and 80% of those fell in the low-to-medium range (r = 0.3-0.69). (Source: Ang, Tan et al., *Progress in Neurobiology*, 2023 — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0079612323000080)
- **[VERIFIED]** CoCon mobile cognitive-control game vs. K-WISC-IV: working-memory game score r = 0.458 with Digit Span and 0.450 with the Working Memory Index (p<.001); processing speed r = 0.337, verbal r = 0.304-0.359 — single game tasks converge only moderately with WISC subtests. (Source: Kim et al., *PLOS ONE*, 2020 — https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0230498)
- **[VERIFIED]** Composite game scores reach much higher convergent validity than single tasks: gamified WM/processing tasks correlated r = 0.29-0.45 with a Raven's short form individually, but a 12-game battery correlated r = 0.93 with an 11-test cognitive-ability battery. (Source: Leutner et al. review, *Frontiers in Psychology*, 2023 — https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.942662/full)
- **[VERIFIED]** Zoo U game-based "stealth" SEL assessment (grades 2-4) showed significant concordance between its six in-game scene scores and teacher ratings of parallel social-emotional skills — criterion validity for school outcomes with young children. (Source: DeRosier & Thomas, *J Applied Developmental Psychology* 55:52-61, 2018 — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0193397316301514)
- **[VERIFIED]** Stealth assessment (Shute) embeds measurement invisibly in gameplay and uses Evidence-Centered Design (competency/evidence/task models) to build a defensible validity argument, validated against external measures (Physics Playground, a calculus game, Plants vs. Zombies 2). This is the methodological backbone for scoring gameplay. (Source: Shute, Lu & Rahimi, ERIC ED612156, 2019 — https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED612156.pdf)
- **[VERIFIED]** Direct young-child engagement evidence: in a gamified eye-tracking battery for 5-year-olds born preterm, 79% (11/14) preferred the gamified version, and gamification reduced missing data and increased retention without lengthening the test. (Source: PMC12603587, 2025 — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12603587/)
- **[VERIFIED]** A Kahoot! gamified-assessment quasi-experiment significantly increased engagement and reduced test anxiety vs. control. (Source: *RMLE Online*, 2025 — https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/19404476.2025.2551476)
- **[VERIFIED]** ML-scored game-based cognitive-ability assessment (11,574 completions; validated on 3,107 applicants): convergent validity r = 0.5, test-retest r = 0.68, adverse-impact ratios >0.8 and Cohen's d <|0.20| across age/gender/ethnicity, no significant gender difference in test anxiety — fairness-optimized gamified scoring can shrink group gaps (ADULT sample). (Source: Leutner et al., *Frontiers in Psychology*, 2023 — https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.942662/full)
- **[VERIFIED]** Nonverbal/performance-based gifted-ID tools (DISCOVER, Naglieri) identify higher proportions of underrepresented/CLD/disadvantaged students than traditional tests by cutting language/prior-knowledge load — but reviewers caution matrices are "no silver bullet." Direct peer-reviewed evidence on *game-based* tools for gifted ID specifically is sparse. (Source: Alodat, *Gifted Child Quarterly*, 2025 — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00169862251392600)
- **[VERIFIED]** *Gaming-familiarity bias is real:* in a 3-D action-game cognitive task, proxies for prior gaming exposure independently predicted performance and were confounded with demographics (younger, male scored higher); the child meta-analysis flags prior gaming/tech experience, age, and gender as under-controlled validity moderators. (Source: *Scientific Reports*, 2022 — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-15679-5)
- **[VERIFIED]** Motor/device confounds documented for young children: fine-motor skill and cognition are intertwined early, and touchscreen dexterity varies — touch-interface demands can under/over-estimate cognitive scores (Pitchford & Outhwaite show tablets can still work in low-exposure children; other work shows tablet vs. non-tablet fine-motor/visual-perception differences). (Source: Pitchford & Outhwaite, *Frontiers in Psychology*, 2016 — https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01666/full)
- **[INFERENCE]** For our K-8 screener: gamifying n-back/Corsi/flanker is defensible for reliability + engagement, but expect single mini-games to correlate only r≈0.3-0.5 with WISC/Raven — so validity must rest on a MULTI-TASK COMPOSITE (which reaches higher convergent r), with every construct anchored to an external criterion via an ECD competency/evidence/task model.
- **[INFERENCE]** To capture gamification's equity upside (lower anxiety, less language load) WITHOUT importing gaming-familiarity bias against low-SES/ELL kids: include a scored familiarization phase, keep interaction mechanics minimal and identical across items, log and statistically control for prior gaming exposure + fine-motor/response-latency artifacts, and audit adverse-impact ratio (>0.8) and subgroup Cohen's d (<|0.20|) before operational use.

### Q7 — Sweep 6: general GBA science & validity (mostly adult/hiring, transferable principles)
- **[VERIFIED]** Definitional distinction on a "playfulness continuum": *gamified assessment* adds game elements to an existing test (a gamified multiple-choice test "is still fundamentally a multiple-choice assessment"), whereas a *game-based assessment (GBA)* is built ground-up as a core gameplay loop measuring traits from play. (Source: Landers et al., *Int. J. Selection & Assessment*, 2022 — https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ijsa.12376)
- **[VERIFIED]** Meta-analysis (52 samples, 44 papers, 6,100+ adults): game-related assessments correlate with traditional cognitive-ability measures at observed r = 0.30 (corrected r = 0.45, p<.001), high heterogeneity — substantial overlap but NOT interchangeable. (Source: Bipp, Wee, Walczok & Hansal, *J Intelligence* 12(12):129, 2024 — https://www.mdpi.com/2079-3200/12/12/129)
- **[VERIFIED]** A CHC-theory-driven GBA of general mental ability reached latent-factor convergence β = 0.97 with a cognitive-ability battery — a purpose-built GBA CAN hit traditional psychometric standards — but observed scores still carried gender-related variance that would contaminate composite selection scores. (Source: Landers et al., in Bipp et al. 2024 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11676581/)
- **[VERIFIED]** A Minecraft-based GBA (verbal/numerical/figural) correlated r = 0.51 with a matched paper-pencil test, but applicant reactions were consistently WORSE than the traditional test — GBAs do not automatically improve reactions. (Source: Ohlms, Melchers & Kanning, *IJSA*, 2024 — https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ijsa.12450)
- **[VERIFIED]** Construct-irrelevant variance confirmed: video-game usage was positively associated with GBA performance, and males/experienced gamers held more positive perceptions than females/low-experience players — gaming familiarity confers an advantage unrelated to the target construct. (Source: Ohlms et al., *Applied Psychology*, 2025 — https://iaap-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/apps.70038)
- **[INFERENCE]** Because immersive games impose high cognitive load, a GBA meant to measure *personality* can inadvertently also tap cognitive ability — a construct-validity threat where mechanics, not the target trait, drive variance. Keep each of our mini-games mechanically simple to isolate its target construct. (Source: reasoning from Landers 2022 + Bipp 2024)
- **[VERIFIED]** Adverse-impact test on 3,107 applicants (pass at 33rd pct): NO adverse impact — AI ratios 0.90-1.00, |Cohen's d| ≤ 0.18 across age/gender/ethnicity; one flag was a female-male passing gap (65% vs 69%, p=.03). (Source: Leutner et al., *Frontiers in Psychology*, 2023 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9891208/)
- **[VERIFIED]** Face-validity caveat: the same GBA scored a Net Promoter Score of 58 (industry ~35-43) and high ease-of-use (4.67/5) but LOW job-relevance (3.70) and "showcase my skills" (3.64) — abstract games raise face-validity concerns even when liked. (Source: Leutner et al. 2023 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9891208/)
- **[VERIFIED]** Anxiety: women reported higher baseline test anxiety than men (0.24 vs −0.37, p=.006) but NO significant gender difference in post-game anxiety change (p=.368), and anxiety didn't predict game performance — consistent with GBAs narrowing anxiety-driven gaps. A separate 2×2 crossover education study found a 3D game significantly reduced test anxiety AND improved exam performance vs. traditional examination. (Source: Leutner et al. 2023 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9891208/ ; Mavridis & Tsiatsos, *JCAL*, 2017 — https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1132241)
- **[VERIFIED]** Pymetrics behavioral games' fairness auditing is peer-reviewed: models are iteratively de-biased against EEOC-defined gender/race groups on a held-out "bias" dataset; >75% of players completed the demographic survey enabling adverse-impact testing — a concrete audit template. (Source: Wilson et al., ACM FAccT, 2021 — https://www.ccs.neu.edu/home/amislove/publications/Pymetrics-FAccT.pdf)
- **[VERIFIED]** Process/behavioral telemetry (latency, navigation, trajectories) is NOT automatically measurement evidence — a short latency is ambiguous (disengagement vs. proficiency) without an explicit validity argument; the GBA above scored from ML features (maxLevel, winRatio) via bias-penalized ridge regression. (Source: MDPI Encyclopedia 6(4):81, 2026 — https://www.mdpi.com/2673-8392/6/4/81 ; Leutner et al. 2023)
- **[VERIFIED]** Behavioral/stealth telemetry may resist faking: in a moral-choice game, an "anomalous" group differed on self-report socially-sensitive scales but NOT on game-based behavioral measures — gameplay signals appear more robust to response bias than self-report. (Source: Game-based psychological assessment review, 2022 — https://www.researchgate.net/publication/363461738_Game-based_psychological_assessment)
