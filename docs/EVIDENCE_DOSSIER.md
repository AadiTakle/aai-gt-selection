## The claims we must defend (map every piece of evidence to one of these)

| # | Claim | Serves | What would defend it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1** | Selected-cohort outcome reporting **cannot separate** who was admitted from what the program did. Observing high outcomes after admission is not evidence of program effect. | R2, R4, R10 | Stats on selection bias magnitude; cases where selective programs' raw outcomes collapsed under causal designs |
| **C2** | A single cognitive-test cutoff **misses capable students** and over-selects on family advantage — so the current process is both inaccurate and inequitable. | R5, H1, H2, H4 | Data on test-score gaps by income/race; measurement error at a single cutoff; predictive-validity ceilings of one test |
| **C3** | Broader, multi-signal capability measures (incl. spatial/ability breadth, talent evidence) **identify capable students a single test misses**, without lowering the bar. | R5, H1 | Validity data for spatial reasoning, universal screening, portfolio/work-sample assessment |
| **C4** | Universal / broadened screening **materially increases identification of underrepresented gifted students** (the Track B access case). | H4, H7 | District-level universal-screening natural experiments with effect sizes |
| **C5** | **Randomized admission among qualified applicants** (a lottery under genuine scarcity) is the strongest feasible way to estimate program effect while preserving a defensible capability bar. | R2, R3, H3 | Lottery/RCT education studies; oversubscription→lottery precedents; effect-size credibility vs. observational |
| **C6** | Program-effect claims from observational/matched designs are **routinely overturned** by randomized or quasi-experimental designs — i.e., the rigor is not optional. | R2, H3 | Head-to-head observational-vs-RCT comparisons; selection-bias magnitude estimates |
| **C7** | The design is **feasible and precedented** — real selective programs already run lottery-based evaluations (SSP), oversubscribed schools use admission lotteries, etc. | R8, H3, H5 | SSP comparator; charter/magnet lottery literature; sample-size/power feasibility |
| **C8** | A high-ceiling growth outcome (e.g., MAP) **can measure gain across the gifted range** without ceiling effects that hide program impact. | R6 | Psychometric data on above-grade/high-ceiling measures; ceiling-effect quantification |

Cross-cutting: **guardrails** (H7 equity monitoring, H8 protecting current high performers) and **claim boundaries** (R10) — evidence that broadening access need not harm existing students, and that access/reliability/outcome/causal claims are distinct.
