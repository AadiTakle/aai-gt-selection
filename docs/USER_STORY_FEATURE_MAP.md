# User Story to MVP Feature Map

## Purpose

This document converts the 49 user stories in `USER_STORIES.md` into a minimal reusable feature set.

“Fully solved” means the user's workflow need, access need, or decision-rights need is handled end to end without a manual exception or unwritten policy. It does not mean the four-week prototype establishes psychometric validity, legal approval, or production readiness.

This feature library remains broader than the ratified product. D-008 approves the Track A/Track B synthetic prototype; only feature behavior explicitly included in `GT_ADMISSIONS_APPLICATION_MVP_PRD.md` is approved for the four-week build.

## Minimal feature set

### F1 — Service and Domain Pathway Registry

Defines:

- every domain GT recognizes;
- the differentiated service offered for that domain;
- essential service prerequisites;
- common direct evidence available to every applicant;
- independent OR-based pathway rules;
- capacity and age/grade boundaries; and
- what happens when GT does not offer a matching service.

Every active pathway records its authorized source, approver, effective date, evidence rationale, age/grade range, service owner, and capacity owner. Unknown or unapproved values fail closed. Four-week configurations are visibly synthetic.

**Complete when:** Every recognized strength maps either to an authorized real service/pathway or an explicit “not served by this program” outcome with truthful explanation. No global giftedness score or unrelated-domain cancellation exists.

### F2 — Universal Accessible Application and Evidence Access

Provides:

- no parent/teacher nomination prerequisite;
- multilingual, audio, low-bandwidth, phone-assisted, paper, and accessible digital routes;
- common evidence opportunities available without paid enrichment or recommendations;
- explicit unavailable/not-applicable evidence codes; and
- status tracking and deadline support.

Language access includes translation version/lineage, qualified interpreter attribution, home-language administration/response where construct-appropriate, and qualified scoring. English mechanics are excluded outside language constructs.

**Complete when:** Every eligible family can enter and complete the same required process without a recommender, conventional school records, private services, travel, or high-speed technology.

### F3 — Structured Parent and Recommender Evidence

Replaces freeform essays and letters with:

- Parent Observable Evidence Form;
- optional Recommender Evidence Form;
- observation context, frequency, independence, assistance, novelty, opportunity, and conflict disclosures;
- “not observed/insufficient opportunity” options;
- support/masking fields; and
- explicit non-scoring of prose quality, prestige, enthusiasm, and optional-evidence absence.

Under D-008, the bounded structured narrative fallback is decision-used Track B evidence and always receives three independent blind reviews. Recommender access remains optional; a parent/guardian or other directly observing adult may use the same route.

**Complete when:** Parents and directly observing adults can provide specific evidence without making giftedness claims, and no family gains or loses eligibility because of prose quality, prestige, enthusiasm, or access to a third-party recommender.

### F4 — Common Direct Evidence and Domain Task Hub

Provides:

- student-selected service-domain routing;
- common, supervised, service-aligned tasks;
- equivalent domain and transfer prompts;
- equal materials and familiarization;
- learning-response/process capture;
- existing mastery evidence intake; and
- clear separation of attained readiness from learning response.

The task hub is a reusable schema instantiated for every configured active domain. Each instance defines a domain task, transfer task, accommodation route, provenance, and research-only/decision-used status.

**Complete when:** Every active pathway is completable through common direct evidence alone, with equivalent access, and the system retains domain performance, transfer, readiness, and process information separately.

### F5 — Artifact Provenance and Assistance Controls

Supports optional artifacts with:

- source date/age and original prompt;
- time, tools, materials, adult help, instruction, editing, and AI assistance;
- version history or oral/supervised verification where needed;
- privacy redaction;
- low-cost submission routes; and
- no scoring of production value, equipment, awards, or paid credentials.

F5 remains separate from F3 because artifacts require a distinct binary-media lifecycle: secure storage, malware/privacy controls, version history, assistance provenance, and supervised ownership verification. F3 owns structured text/context forms; F5 owns artifact integrity.

Under D-008, one or two existing artifacts per selected Track B domain are authorized as decision-used synthetic prototype evidence with required provenance and assistance metadata. No artifact is required because the bounded structured narrative fallback provides an equivalent eligibility route.

**Complete when:** Admissions can distinguish the student's demonstrated contribution from assistance without automatically rewarding polish or rejecting disclosed support, and F7 technically rejects artifact fields as eligibility inputs unless explicitly authorized.

### F6 — Accommodations and Administration Integrity

Provides:

- accessible accommodation request and approval;
- predeclared construct-preserving response routes;
- breaks, environment, timing, communication, motor, sensory, and technology supports;
- no adverse flag merely because an accommodation was used;
- administration-fidelity records; and
- invalidation and free repeat/equivalent evidence procedures.

If no construct-equivalent route is available, adverse scoring is prohibited and the applicant automatically receives an alternate common evidence route or pending status.

**Complete when:** Every required evidence opportunity has an authorized construct-equivalent route with recorded validation status or fails closed to alternate common evidence/pending, and illness, technology, instructions, language access, or denied accommodations can never become child-performance evidence.

### F7 — Profile-Preserving Evidence and Decision Engine

Implements:

- independent domain profiles and OR-based pathways;
- no global holistic average;
- common-direct-evidence-only qualification;
- optional-context evidence firewall;
- measurement uncertainty and boundary intervals;
- distinct missing-evidence reasons;
- readiness and learning-response separation;
- prohibited-proxy enforcement; and
- deterministic, versioned decision reasons.

Every evidence field is classified as `decision-used`, `optional research/context`, `routing`, or `operations-only`. The engine accepts decision-used fields only when an approved evidence-use record identifies the construct, pathway, evidence limits, effective version, approver, rater requirements, scoring rule, disagreement threshold, and adjudication procedure. D-008 authorizes synthetic artifact-route and narrative-fallback Snapshot evidence for Track B prototype decisions. F4 task, transfer, dynamic/process, commercial rating-scale, and all non-Snapshot optional outputs remain disconnected from eligibility fixtures.

The engine also contains a bounded seat/offer/waitlist allocation state machine. Allocation inputs and outputs are versioned and research-consent fields are technically excluded.

**Complete when:** Asynchronous strengths survive unrelated weaknesses, unauthorized or optional evidence cannot change eligibility/allocation, missingness never becomes zero, and identical valid inputs reproduce the same pathway and allocation result.

### F8 — Structured Domain Review and Rater Calibration

Provides:

- domain-specific analytic rubrics;
- two independent raters for all subjective evidence, whether research-only or future decision-used;
- benchmark cases and calibration;
- feasible identity/context masking;
- evidence citation for every rating;
- rater disagreement, adjudication, severity, and drift tracking; and
- review limited to a documented boundary, validity, accommodation, or provenance question.

Outside the D-008 Snapshot, review outputs are restricted to correcting a score, invalidating/repeating evidence, routing an accommodation, or retaining non-decisional context. For the Snapshot, reviewers produce independent classifications; F7 applies the locked majority rule and produces eligibility.

**Complete when:** No reviewer can perform an undocumented “whole child” override or directly award eligibility, domain experts stay within their domain, and material disagreement is detected and resolved through the locked process.

### F9 — Explainable Decision, Correction, Appeal, Retest, and Re-entry

Provides:

- pathway-specific evidence and reason summaries;
- non-stigmatizing statuses;
- factual correction;
- accommodation review;
- independent scoring appeal;
- free boundary retest/equivalent route;
- invalid-administration recovery;
- scheduled fresh re-entry; and
- no retaliation for correction, appeal, or prior nonqualification.

Trigger matrices define eligible error types, evidence allowed, owner, deadline, retry limit, interim rights, and permitted outputs. If a queue cannot meet its service level, affected deadlines pause automatically without reducing applicant rights.

**Complete when:** Every applicant can understand the decision, correct process errors, receive the prescribed uncertainty route, and re-enter later without needing exceptional advocacy or staff-created exceptions.

### F10 — Consent, Privacy, and Research Separation

Provides:

- separate permissions for operations, research, linkage, follow-up, recontact, secondary use, and results return;
- guardian permission and age-appropriate assent where applicable;
- child dissent and withdrawal handling;
- access, correction, export, deletion, retention, and exception explanations;
- role-based access and minimum data use; and
- a firewall preventing research choices from affecting eligibility or allocation.

F10 owns data-rights intake and authorization. F9 owns decision remedies and rule reruns. F11 propagates approved corrections across systems of record.

**Complete when:** A family can decline or withdraw from every optional data use without hidden eligibility/allocation consequences and can exercise all documented data rights through one routed request.

### F11 — Admissions Operations, Service Readiness, and Audit Console

Provides:

- canonical applicant/evidence records;
- owners, queues, deadlines, backups, and service levels;
- translation, accommodation, scoring, appeal, and re-entry workload;
- source-of-truth and correction propagation;
- rule, rubric, rater, evidence, notice, consent, and decision version history;
- capacity/service availability;
- role-based operations;
- replay, compliance, and critic exports; and
- security, incident, and recovery controls.

The console includes a versioned claim register with claim ID, exact wording, maturity level, evidence links, allowed/prohibited language, accountable owner, and effective version.

When workload, translation, accessibility, or review capacity cannot meet a protected service level, the console automatically pauses the affected deadline or pathway publication and preserves interim applicant rights.

**Complete when:** Operators can run every mapped story through a named owner, trigger, deadline, retry limit, and fail-safe action; every decision can be replayed; and GT cannot advertise a pathway it lacks the authorized service or capacity to deliver.

## Why this set is minimal

- F1–F2 establish scope and equal entry.
- F3–F6 collect evidence without turning privilege or disability into capability.
- F7–F8 make decisions reproducible while preserving domain peaks.
- F9–F10 protect explanation, correction, re-entry, consent, and data rights.
- F11 makes the other ten features operable and auditable.

Removing any feature leaves at least one user group dependent on an unwritten exception.

## Four-week MVP cut

Implement all eleven features as bounded prototype capabilities, but constrain depth:

- F1: configure two visibly synthetic example domains, each with complete activation metadata, plus one explicit unsupported-domain outcome.
- F2: prototype accessible/multilingual routes with synthetic submissions.
- F3: implement parent and optional recommender schemas without eligibility scoring.
- F4: implement one reusable hypothetical task/transfer/accommodation schema and instantiate it for every configured supported domain.
- F5: capture provenance and assistance metadata; no advanced authenticity detection.
- F6: implement accommodation states, equivalent-route configuration, and invalid-administration recovery.
- F7: implement pathway/allocation pseudocode, approved-evidence registry, optional-field rejection, missingness, and uncertainty using synthetic decision fixtures disconnected from F4 research outputs.
- F8: implement synthetic calibration/adjudication workflows, not production rater validation.
- F9: implement explanation, correction, appeal, retest, and re-entry state machines.
- F10: implement consent/data-rights state separation using synthetic records.
- F11: implement audit/replay, correction propagation, queue triggers, and automatic rights-preserving overload fail-safes rather than a production operations platform.

## MVP acceptance gate

The future MVP document should not approve a feature unless:

1. every primary story mapped to it passes its “fully solved when” condition;
2. no supporting story requires a manual workaround;
3. absence of an artifact or third-party recommender cannot reduce eligibility, and only the applicant’s selected authorized Snapshot route can affect Track B eligibility;
4. all required evidence has an accessible common route;
5. every decision and correction can be replayed;
6. unsupported domains receive truthful service-mismatch outcomes;
7. research, privacy, and appeal rights are enforceable in workflow state; and
8. synthetic success is not described as live validity or production readiness.
