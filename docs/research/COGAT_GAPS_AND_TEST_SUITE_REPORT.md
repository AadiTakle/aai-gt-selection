# CogAT Gaps and Test-Suite Options

**Research date:** 2026-07-17
**Status:** Deep-research synthesis; not approval for live admissions
**Requirements:** R5, R7–R10; H1, H2, H4, H7, H9, H10
**Evidence anchors:** E-005–E-008; new candidate findings require local validation

## Executive conclusion

CogAT Form 8 is a scalable group screen of developed verbal, quantitative, and nonverbal reasoning. Its largest shortcomings are not that it measures nothing useful or that it completely omits spatial reasoning. They are:

1. a one-day score can become an overly sharp admissions gate;
2. it does not measure demonstrated academic achievement or readiness;
3. its spatial coverage is narrower than a dedicated spatial battery;
4. it cannot separate reasoning weakness from working-memory, processing-speed, disability, language, or administration effects;
5. no test substitution by itself fixes opportunity, ELL, SES, referral, or tuition-related selection; and
6. predictive validity does not show who benefits from GT School.

### Primary two-test recommendation for grades 2–12

1. **CogAT Form 8 Complete Battery**, using current 2024 post-pandemic norms.
2. **One predeclared NWEA MAP Growth subject test**, using current 2025 norms.

This pairing fills the most consequential distinct gap—advanced academic readiness—while remaining group-scalable and auditable. CogAT supplies reasoning profiles; MAP supplies achievement in one program-relevant domain.

The MAP subject must follow the capability definition:

- use **MAP Growth Math** if advanced quantitative readiness is the declared criterion;
- use **MAP Growth Reading** if advanced literacy readiness is the declared criterion; and
- if GT requires both, the honest design is a three-test suite that needs explicit scope approval rather than relabeling two subject tests as one.

Current MAP documentation reports strong overall reliability and high adaptive reach, but does not establish precision at GT's eventual cutoff or incremental validity after CogAT. Those remain local validation questions.

This is a recommendation to prototype and validate, not a production decision. GT must demonstrate that MAP adds useful information after CogAT and must not reuse the admissions score as automatic proof of program impact.

### Conditional runner-up

For a small finalist pool with qualified examiner access:

- **CogAT Form 8 → age-appropriate selected Woodcock-Johnson V Cognitive clusters**

Selected WJ V clusters can add individual observation and targeted visual-processing, working-memory, processing-speed, or learning/retrieval evidence when a documented referral question requires it. The pathway is operationally heavier, repeats substantial reasoning content if the full general battery is used, and does not directly fill the achievement gap.

For ages 6:0–16:11 where gifted-tail confidence and twice-exceptional interpretation matter more than norm recency, **WISC-V** remains a strong finalist alternative with extended norms.

WISC-V extended norms are conditional tools, not blanket high-end assurance: they are most relevant when eligible subtests reach ordinary scaled-score ceilings, their utility varies by age/subtest, and the gifted validation samples are limited.

### Spatial-priority alternative

For a STEM/spatially specific program in grades 5–12:

- **CogAT Form 8 + Johns Hopkins CTY Spatial Test Battery**

The STB adds broader rotation, transformation, surface-development, perspective, and visual-memory content. Historical CTY samples show strong total-score reliability, but reliability does not establish incremental admissions validity beyond CogAT. It is not the default recommendation because administration takes roughly 1.5–2 hours, its norms come from selected talent-search populations, subgroup evidence is incomplete, and transfer to GT remains unknown.

## What CogAT Form 8 actually measures

The Complete Battery contains:

- **Verbal:** analogies, sentence completion, classification;
- **Quantitative:** number analogies, number puzzles, number series; and
- **Nonverbal/Figural:** figure matrices, paper folding, figure classification.

Therefore:

- CogAT is not merely a verbal/math test.
- CogAT does contain a genuine spatial-visualization task through Paper Folding.
- Its spatial gap is one of breadth and depth, not complete absence.
- It does not directly measure achievement, creativity, conscientiousness, motivation, executive function, or separately interpretable working memory and processing speed.

Public documentation does not provide enough independent Form 8 evidence to make strong claims about conditional error, differential validity, or precision at GT's intended cutoff. GT should obtain the proprietary technical tables for the exact level and norm year before high-stakes use.

## Why the decision rule matters more than merely adding a test

A second test can worsen selection if every applicant must clear both cutoffs. Research on multiple-criteria systems shows:

- **AND/conjunctive rules** accumulate false negatives.
- **OR rules** create larger pools but do not automatically improve equity at fixed capacity.
- **Compensatory rules** can preserve strong domain profiles when one measure is weaker.
- Two highly overlapping group reasoning tests can add burden and correlated error without adding useful construct coverage.

A defensible prototype should compare:

1. current single-score cutoff;
2. confidence-band boundary handling;
3. compensatory profile rule;
4. multiple-pathway/OR rule; and
5. national versus valid contextual reference groups.

Do not compute "local norms" from GT's self-selected applicant pool.

## Recommended suite by context

### Grades 2–12, moderate/high applicant volume

Use **CogAT Form 8 + one MAP Growth subject selected from the declared readiness criterion**.

- Administer universally within the applicant process.
- Use MAP Math for a quantitative-readiness pathway or MAP Reading for a literacy-readiness pathway.
- Do not add both without acknowledging and approving a three-test design.
- Use a compensatory/profile rule, not two mandatory cutoffs.
- Use confidence intervals and a predefined boundary process.
- Validate threshold-specific precision, incremental information, subgroup outcomes, burden, and score ceilings locally.

### Small pool with examiner access

Use **CogAT Form 8 as an inclusive screen + age-appropriate selected WJ V Cognitive clusters for documented complex/finalist questions**.

- Do not average CogAT and a WJ general-ability score as if independent.
- Select only clusters that answer a documented referral question and meet their test-specific minimum ages.
- Do not use under-six interpretations that lack sufficient current evidence.
- Add existing achievement evidence rather than a third test where possible.

### Grades 5–12 with an explicit spatial/STEM capability pathway

Pilot **CogAT Form 8 + CTY STB**.

- Treat STB as an alternative/additive pathway, not another universal hard gate.
- Require local incremental-validity and adverse-impact analysis.
- Verify institutional licensing and current technical documentation.

### Kindergarten and children under six

Do not use any 1–2 test suite as a permanent test-only gate.

Use provisional placement, multiple entry points, repeated assessment, and scheduled reassessment. Current evidence and developmental instability make permanent early exclusion difficult to defend.

## Tests not recommended as routine CogAT complements

- **NNAT or Raven-style matrix tests as universal additions:** They substantially overlap CogAT Nonverbal, remove verbal/quantitative breadth when used alone, and older independent evidence shows that reduced-language testing does not eliminate ELL differences.
- **A full second general-ability battery for every applicant:** It repeats substantial reasoning content and adds burden unless selected clusters answer a documented referral question.
- **Self-report grit or mindset scales:** Current meta-analytic evidence does not justify using these as standalone capability gates, especially within an already high-ability range.
- **Research-only or discontinued spatial instruments:** Do not use instruments without current child norms, secure delivery, accommodations, and high-stakes licensing.

## Process requirements that no extra test replaces

1. Define "capability to benefit from GT" before selecting measures.
2. Use universal consideration within the target population; separately track who never applies.
3. Do not require parent nomination before valid screening.
4. Use current national norms and valid opportunity-context comparisons for different questions.
5. Represent measurement uncertainty and define boundary-case treatment.
6. Require distinct incremental value before adding a second test.
7. Make all required testing, accommodations, confirmation, and appeals free to families.
8. Use an outcome distinct from the admissions rule.
9. Monitor subgroup completion, selection, prediction, burden, appeals, and missingness.
10. Preserve raw exports, norm versions, rules, overrides, and appeal records for audit.

## Four-week capstone recommendation

Prototype **CogAT + one predeclared MAP Growth domain** with synthetic data, but keep the architecture test-agnostic.

The prototype should demonstrate:

- CogAT V/Q/N profiles and one program-relevant MAP domain;
- one compensatory decision rule with explicit confidence-band boundary handling;
- false-negative, subgroup, gaming, burden, and score-ceiling simulations;
- accommodation and boundary-case handling;
- local incremental-validity and precision-analysis workflow;
- claim boundaries separating selection reliability from program impact.

Defer WJ V/WISC/STB pathways, additional MAP subjects, and alternate rule families until the primary two-test prototype passes its critic review. Do not procure tests, use copyrighted items, set GT cutoffs, use live student records, or claim production psychometric validation during the capstone.

## Corrections to the existing project research

1. Deary et al. used the UK Cognitive Abilities Test CAT2E, not CogAT.
2. CogAT Form 8 measures verbal, quantitative, and nonverbal reasoning; it is not primarily a math/science test.
3. CogAT includes Paper Folding and figural reasoning. Spatial reasoning is undercovered, not absent.
4. Wai/Lubinski estimates about students missed by math/verbal-only searches cannot be directly applied to CogAT, which has a Nonverbal battery.
5. Lohman's extreme-list overlap results do not directly estimate false negatives at GT's reported 90th-percentile threshold.
6. Older Form 6/7 reliability, ELL, ceiling, and error results must not be relabeled Form 8 evidence.
7. Conscientiousness predicts general academic performance but is not established as a valid gifted-admissions criterion within an already high-ability pool.
8. GT's current form, cutoff, norm year, retest policy, and combination rule remain unverified.

## Validation required before high-stakes use

1. Obtain complete current technical manuals for every candidate.
2. Independently review upper-tail conditional error, reliability, norm composition, DIF, and accommodation comparability.
3. Define the program-specific criterion that the suite is intended to predict.
4. Run a prospective local pilot comparing CogAT-only with candidate suites.
5. Preregister incremental-validity and decision-rule evaluation.
6. Report calibration, classification stability, subgroup differential prediction, false-negative recovery, burden, and cost.
7. Simulate measurement error and regression to the mean around cutoffs.
8. Use free universal access and contestable correction/retest procedures.
9. Keep the impact outcome distinct from admissions inputs.
10. Stop if applicant volume, examiner capacity, data permissions, accessibility, or GT authority is unresolved.

## Evidence-quality caveats

- Current Form 8 and 2024-norm technical details are substantially publisher-controlled.
- Much CogAT equity evidence uses Forms 6 or 7.
- MAP's strongest current technical evidence is publisher-produced.
- STB validity evidence is partly developer-affiliated and subgroup evidence is incomplete.
- WJ V is new; independent evidence is promising but still limited, especially under age six.
- No cited test has demonstrated that it predicts causal benefit from GT School.
