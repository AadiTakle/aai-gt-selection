# Psychometric Sweep — Learning-Rate Power, Selection, and Engagement Telemetry (SYNTHETIC)

**Status:** research evidence, not ratified product direction. Born-synthetic
(`synthetic_only: true`, `validated: false`; D-006, R9). Follows on from
`docs/PERSONA_SIM_RESULTS.md` and implements no product behaviour. Registered as
**E-073** in `docs/research/ASSUMPTIONS_AND_EVIDENCE.md`; extends E-067.

> **CLAIM BOUNDARY — READ THIS BEFORE ANY NUMBER BELOW.** Every persona in this
> study was generated by the 3PL in `packages/cat-engine/src/irt.ts` and then
> scored by estimators that invert that same 3PL. **All recovery here is
> CIRCULAR.** What that buys is exactly three things: (a) the estimators and
> selection rules are implemented correctly, (b) their bias and precision under
> known truth, and (c) the **statistical power** of a given test design — what a
> design *could* detect if the simulator's assumptions held. It is **not** evidence
> that any metric measures real ability, **not** convergent or predictive validity,
> and **not** program impact. Access, reliability, outcome change, and causal
> impact are separate milestones (R10). Tuning weights, cuts, or thresholds on
> these numbers tunes to the simulator's assumptions, not to children.
>
> One number in Experiment 1 needs a second, sharper caveat. The best-performing
> lambda estimator assumes ability climbs **linearly in trial index through a 3PL
> with known item parameters** — which is precisely how the data were generated.
> Its correlations are therefore an **upper bound under a correctly specified
> model**, not a forecast. A real novel block has estimated item parameters and no
> guarantee the climb is linear. `M-LEARNRATE` remains a labelled hypothesis
> (H-series), and nothing here moves it toward being a validated metric.

# Experiment 1 — Learning-rate power curve

**Question.** What novel-block design, if any, recovers the injected lambda? The
recorded run found r = 0.109 for an OLS score slope over a 6-trial ascending block
and diagnosed a power null. This sweep varies block length (6/10/15/20/30/40),
difficulty regime (ascending / constant at the estimated theta / adaptive), and
lambda effect size (1x / 2x / 4x the injected scale), and runs five estimators over
the identical response sequences.

## The answer

**At the current injected lambda scale, the minimum design that reaches r ≥ 0.50 is
30 trials in a single novel block at adaptive difficulty (r = 0.623), or 15 trials
per domain across all four domains (60 items, r = 0.548). The 6-trial block in the
recorded baseline cannot reach it under any estimator or regime. [V]**

The single-domain figure is the one to plan against: it is both cheaper (30 items
vs 60) and better (0.623 vs 0.548). Every four-domain number in this report averages
four independent blocks, which costs four times the items to halve estimator noise —
a bad trade at these lengths.

Notice what happens to the raw OLS slope at 4x under the constant and adaptive
regimes: it stops improving and then *declines* with block length (0.484 at 20
trials, 0.390 at 40), never reaching 0.50. A large climb against a fixed target
saturates the score at 1, and a slope on a saturated binary sequence loses the
signal. The MAP estimator, which knows the item's ceiling is `1` and its floor is
`c`, does not have this failure mode. **[V]**

## What the null actually was

The recorded 6-trial, ascending, raw-OLS cell reproduces here at **r = 0.118**
against the recorded 0.109 — a different administration order and seed, the same
answer. **[V]** The diagnosis in `docs/PERSONA_SIM_RESULTS.md` was correct in
substance and incomplete in one respect:

1. **It was a power null, and the power is quantified.** At 6 trials the
   per-persona OLS slope SD is 0.0569 while the slope variation actually
   attributable to lambda is 0.0067 — a signal-to-noise ratio of about 1:8.5.
   At 40 ascending trials those become 0.0052 and 0.0037, roughly 1:1.4. Trial
   count is the binding constraint, exactly as diagnosed. **[V]**
2. **It was also an estimator null, and that half was missed.** Across the 1x grid
   the joint MAP estimator beats the raw OLS slope in every one of the 18 cells,
   typically by 0.10–0.30 in r, and it is the only estimator that reaches the
   target at 15 trials per domain. Concluding "not recoverable from a block this
   short" from an OLS slope understated what the same responses contain. **[I]**
3. **Difficulty regime matters far less than either.** At 15 trials per domain the
   three regimes span r = 0.523 to 0.548. The ascending ramp was never the main
   problem; it mattered only for the raw score slope, whose mean it drives negative.
   **[V]**

Two estimators are worth naming as negatives. **Trials-to-criterion is weak**: it
peaks at |r| = 0.414 (constant, 40 trials) and is often the worst statistic in the
row, because a run of three correct answers is a coarse read of a curve. **[V]** The
**block-half residual contrast** tracks the residual slope closely but never beats
it, so it buys nothing. **[V]**

The MAP estimator carries a real bias. At 1x, the injected mean lambda is 0.030 and
the estimator's mean signed error runs from −0.029 at 6 trials to −0.013 (ascending)
or −0.018 (adaptive) at 40 — it recovers a mean climb of 0.001 to 0.017, shrinking
the truth toward zero by roughly 45–95% depending on block length. **[V]** That is
the prior doing its job on a short block, and it means the estimator supports
*ranking* children on lambda long before it supports *quoting* a lambda.

**Depends on:** A7 (lambda scale — the whole power curve is stated relative to it),
A8 (linear climb, correct functional form, known item parameters), A5 (`c = 0.25`
caps how much any single trial can say), A2/A3 (the engagement gate removes trials
from the block), A1 (learning-rate/ability separation), A6.

# Experiment 2 — Adaptive selection and the guessing floor

**Question.** Does adaptive item selection move the per-domain precision wall, or is
the wall the item format? Two pools are used: the **matched** pool is the 15
production rungs, and the **enriched** pool is 41 difficulties on a 0.15 grid — what
a real CAT pool would look like. On the matched pool at k = 15 every rule has
administered the same 15 items, so adaptive selection can only matter at k < 15;
that is a fact about pool size, not about adaptive testing, and the enriched pool is
the fair test.

## Harness check

The `matched / fixed ladder` row reproduces the recorded baseline: per-domain mean
SE 0.648 at 15 items (recorded 0.643–0.659), pooled mean SE 0.389 at 60 administered
items (recorded 0.388), and 32 pooled items for SE ≤ 0.50 (recorded 32). The
incremental posterior used to trace the curves agrees with the production
`estimateThetaEap` to **0.000000000** theta units across every persona-domain in
every configuration. **[V]**

## What `M-ENGAGE` must capture

**The largest single win requires no new telemetry at all. [V]** Adding a
response-pattern rule — flag runs of two or more incorrect responses on items the
working ability estimate says the child should have passed (P(correct) ≥ 0.75) —
closes **88.7%** of the RT-to-oracle bias gap (−0.199 → −0.081), improves composite
RMSE from 0.467 to 0.442, and costs 1.2 percentage points of extra exclusion
(15.6% → 16.8%). Its per-trial specificity is 0.987. It uses only the item
parameters and the responses, both of which the server already has. This should be
evaluated before any telemetry is specified. **[V]**

**A per-item interaction signal is worth collecting only if it is genuinely
separating.** RT + dwell at the full modelled separation reaches RMSE 0.404 and bias
−0.075 (93.2% of the gap) — the best composite in the table and within 0.008 of the
oracle. But degrade the instrument and the advantage disappears against the free
pattern rule: at 75% separation RMSE is 0.435 (vs 0.442 for pattern, essentially a
tie), and at 50% separation it is 0.449 — **worse than collecting nothing new**.
**[V]** In detector terms the requirement is:

> An interaction/dwell signal must reach **≥ 0.94 per-trial specificity at
> ≥ 0.94 sensitivity** for off-task detection before it beats the free
> response-pattern rule. Below roughly 0.88 specificity it is not worth the
> collection, storage, and privacy cost. **[I]**

**The within-session decline signal is a negative result. [V]** A latching decline
rule is a disaster: specificity 0.512, 57% of items excluded, composite RMSE 0.736 —
materially *worse* than the RT gate alone. The non-latching window version does
remove bias (−0.057, 107% of the gap) but does it by over-flagging: RMSE rises to
0.453 and Pearson r drops from 0.894 to 0.880. Drift is real in the generator
(A2 puts it there by construction) and is still not usefully detectable from the
residual stream, because the same residual pattern is produced by a child who
simply hit their limit. `M-DRIFT` should not be specified as an exclusion signal on
this evidence. **[V]**

**Bias reduction alone is the wrong acceptance criterion.** Three rows close ≥ 100%
of the bias gap (decline-window 1.072, all-four 1.050) while making the estimate
*worse* on RMSE and correlation. Over-flagging removes genuine hard misses along
with the disengaged ones, which pushes theta above the truth and throws away
precision. Any detector proposal must be judged on RMSE, correlation, and exclusion
rate together, not on bias. **[V]**

Finally, the RT gate is already doing most of the work it can: sensitivity 0.818 at
specificity 0.999. The remaining 18% of off-task trials are **slow** by
construction (A3 sets rapid-guess propensity at about 0.75 of off-task trials), so
any signal that closes the gap must be informative **at normal response times**.
That is the one-line specification. **[I]**

**Depends on:** A2 (engagement scale and drift), A3 (rapid-guess propensity and the
1500 ms floor — these set both the residual bias and the ceiling on what RT can
catch), A9 (the dwell signal's separation — the strongest assumption in this
experiment; no real instrument is known to achieve it), A11 (detector thresholds,
untuned), A4 (RT model), A5.

# What this means for test design

Concrete and falsifiable. Each recommendation states what would refute it.

### Learning rate

1. **A 6-item novel block cannot support `M-LEARNRATE`; a 30-item single-domain
   block at adaptive difficulty can support a ranking. [I from V]** Budget 30 items
   in one novel domain rather than 6–15 items in each of four; it costs half as many
   items and recovers better (r 0.62 vs 0.55). *Refuted if* a 30-item adaptive block
   fails to reach r ≥ 0.5 against injected lambda under a re-run at a different seed,
   or if the estimator's advantage vanishes when item parameters are estimated rather
   than known.
2. **Replace the OLS score slope with the joint MAP learning-curve estimator before
   concluding anything else about lambda. [V]** It beats OLS in all 18 cells at the
   current effect size. *Refuted if* the advantage does not survive misspecification
   — a non-linear (e.g. exponential-approach) climb, or item parameters carrying
   realistic calibration error.
3. **Difficulty regime is a second-order knob. [V]** Adaptive beats ascending by
   0.015 in r at 15 trials per domain. Choose the regime on child experience (an
   ascending ramp to failure is the thing the brainlift argues against), not on
   statistical power. *Refuted if* a regime difference larger than ~0.05 in r appears
   at a block length actually under consideration.
4. **Report lambda as a rank band, never as a number. [V]** The MAP estimator shrinks
   the recovered climb toward zero by roughly 60% even at 40 trials. *Refuted if* a
   calibration study shows the shrinkage is stable enough to invert.

### Item format and item count

5. **Per-domain SE ≤ 0.50 is not reachable with 15 four-option items under any
   selection rule tested. [V]** The best is 0.549. Either the composite is the
   reportable unit, or the per-domain form is ~23 items, or the item format changes.
   *Refuted if* a selection rule not tested here (content-balanced exposure-controlled
   CAT, polytomous scoring, or response-time-weighted scoring) reaches 0.50 at 15
   four-option items.
6. **Item format buys more precision than item count. [V]** Constructed response
   (`c = 0`) with MFI reaches per-domain SE 0.435 at 15 items and hits 0.50 at 10;
   four-option needs 23. Moving from four options to five (`c = 0.20`) alone cuts the
   requirement from 23 to 19. *Refuted if* constructed-response items in this domain
   carry enough scoring noise or `a` penalty to erase the gain — which this sweep
   cannot test, because it holds `a` fixed across formats.
7. **If the pool stays selected-response, adopt adaptive selection and use five
   options, not three. [I from V]** MFI plus `c = 0.20` reaches pooled SE ≤ 0.50 at
   16 administered items against 32 today. *Refuted if* exposure control or content
   balancing eats the gain on a real pool.
8. **Do not raise item discrimination without adopting adaptive selection. [V]** In
   the fixed ladder, raising `a` lowers mean SE (0.667 → 0.599) while RMSE gets worse
   (0.879 → 1.047): the form becomes confidently wrong. *Refuted if* the effect
   disappears when the fixed ladder is centred on a prior estimate of the child's
   level rather than spanning the full range.
9. **Use MFI, not the posterior-weighted criterion. [V]** They are within 0.006 SE of
   each other at every item count tested; the simpler rule wins on cost. *Refuted if*
   the two diverge on a pool with substantially varying discrimination, where the
   expected-information criterion has room to differ.

### Engagement telemetry

10. **Ship the response-pattern rule before specifying any new telemetry. [V]** It
    closes 88.7% of the residual bias gap for zero collection cost and improves RMSE.
    *Refuted if* real children produce runs of easy misses for reasons other than
    disengagement often enough to drop its specificity below ~0.95 — plausible for
    careless-but-engaged responding, and untestable here.
11. **`M-ENGAGE` must be informative at NORMAL response times. [V]** Everything the
    RT floor can catch, it already catches (sensitivity 0.818 at specificity 0.999).
    A telemetry proposal that only sharpens the RT threshold is not worth building.
12. **Specify a numeric acceptance bar for any interaction signal: ≥ 0.94 per-trial
    specificity at ≥ 0.94 sensitivity. [I from V]** Below ~0.88 specificity the signal
    is worse than the free pattern rule. Any `M-ENGAGE` instrument proposal should be
    required to demonstrate its operating point before collection is approved — this
    is a privacy-relevant decision, not only an engineering one.
13. **Do not build a drift/decline exclusion rule. [V]** Latching destroys the
    estimate (RMSE 0.736); non-latching trades RMSE and correlation for bias.
    *Refuted if* a drift rule tuned on held-out simulated data reaches ≥ 0.97
    specificity while retaining ≥ 0.90 sensitivity.
14. **Judge every future detector on RMSE, correlation, and exclusion rate together.
    [V]** Three variants here close the whole bias gap while making the score worse.

### What this sweep did not determine

- Whether any of it survives **estimated** rather than known item parameters. Every
  number here uses the true `a/b/c`. This is the single largest gap between these
  results and a real form. **[A]**
- Whether the linear-in-trial climb assumption holds. The MAP estimator's advantage
  is partly an artefact of matching the generator exactly (A8). **[A]**
- Whether a constructed-response item retains the same `a` as a selected-response
  item measuring the same thing. Recommendation 6 assumes it does. **[A]**
- Whether any dwell/interaction instrument can reach the separation modelled in A9.
  Nothing in this study is evidence that one can. **[A]**
- Anything at all about real children: no convergent validity, no predictive
  validity, no program impact (R10).
