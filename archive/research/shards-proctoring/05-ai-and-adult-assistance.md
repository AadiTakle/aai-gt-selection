# AI and Adult Assistance: The Two Threats Proctoring Was Never Designed For

*Shard compiled 2026-07-30. All model-capability figures are dated because they age quickly.*

**General idea.** Proctoring technology was built to answer one question — *is the right person alone in the room, and are they looking at anything they shouldn't be?* Neither of the two threats that matter most to an at-home children's reasoning assessment is visible to that question. A frontier model running on a phone eighteen inches outside the webcam frame produces no browser event, no network signature the exam host can see, and no gaze pattern distinguishable from thinking. A parent sitting beside the child produces no anomaly at all, because a supportive adult is the expected condition of at-home child testing rather than a violation of it. The published evidence complicates the alarm in a specific and useful way: on abstract *grid* puzzles delivered as text, frontier models now exceed average human performance by a wide margin, while on *image-based* matrix and figural-analogy problems — the actual format of nonverbal cognitive tests — the same models sit at or barely above chance, and the diagnostic work attributes roughly fifty times as many failures to perception as to reasoning. Meanwhile the strongest measured score inflation in the literature comes not from cheating at all but from coaching, which is legal, widespread, and in one 11-plus study substantial enough to shift means while leaving rank order intact. The disagreement worth surfacing is therefore not "is AI a threat" but *which* threat dominates, and whether at-home delivery of a high-stakes children's reasoning test is defensible before any of this is settled.

## 1. Frontier model performance on abstract and figural reasoning — and the shape of the failures

**Source:** ARC Prize verified leaderboards and technical report; peer-reviewed and preprint benchmark evaluations of vision-language models on Raven's-style matrix, visual-analogy, and abstract visual reasoning tasks (2024–2026).

**DOK 1 — Facts:**

- ARC Prize's independently verified testing lists GPT-5.6 Sol at maximum reasoning effort scoring 92.5% on ARC-AGI-2 and 96.5% on ARC-AGI-1, published 2026-07-09 ([ARC Prize, GPT-5.6 results](https://arcprize.org/results/openai-gpt-5-6)).
- At the time ARC Prize published the ARC-AGI-2 technical report in 2025, no leading model had surpassed a 5% success rate on the benchmark ([ARC Prize, ARC-AGI-2 technical report](https://arcprize.org/blog/arc-agi-2-technical-report)).
- Average human performance on the ARC-AGI-2 public evaluation tasks was 66% in the ARC Prize test sample, against the 92.5% now verified for a frontier model ([ARC-AGI-2 repository](https://github.com/arcprize/ARC-AGI-2)).
- ARC-AGI tasks are natively "a JSON lists of integers" between 0 and 9, not images, and are converted to text tokens for language-model evaluation ([ARC Prize guide](https://arcprize.org/guide/1); [ARC-AGI repository](https://github.com/fchollet/ARC-AGI)).
- The same GPT-5.6 Sol configuration scoring 92.5% on ARC-AGI-2 scores 7.78% on ARC-AGI-3, the interactive-environment benchmark ([ARC Prize, GPT-5.6 results](https://arcprize.org/results/openai-gpt-5-6)).
- On VRIQ's abstract puzzle set — sequence completion, matrix prediction, odd-one-out, figure rotation, 3D visualization delivered as images — models averaged 27.52% against a 25% random-guess baseline and a 90.36% human baseline, measured February 2026 ([Khezresmaeilzadeh et al., 2026, arXiv:2602.05382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05382)).
- In the same February 2026 evaluation, GPT-5.1 averaged 29.3% and Gemini-2.5-pro 28.7% on the abstract set, while the best standalone open-weight model reached 35.5% ([Khezresmaeilzadeh et al., 2026, arXiv:2602.05382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05382)).
- Human error attribution in that study assigned 55.4% of model failures to perception alone and 1.1% to reasoning alone, a roughly 50:1 ratio stable across model scale ([Khezresmaeilzadeh et al., 2026, arXiv:2602.05382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05382)).
- The one tool-augmented system tested, o3 with image cropping and magnification, reached 50.2% average on the abstract set and 68% on figure rotation, roughly doubling every non-tool model ([Khezresmaeilzadeh et al., 2026, arXiv:2602.05382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05382)).
- On the NeuroCognition adaptation of Raven's Advanced Progressive Matrices, Gemini 3 Pro scored 0.400 and GPT-5 scored 0.279 on the image multiple-choice variant, against a reported human average of 0.814, measured March 2026 ([Haznitrama, Ardi & Oh, 2026, arXiv:2603.02540](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02540)).
- The same models scored 0.585 and 0.540 respectively on text-symbolic multiple-choice versions of the same matrix task, roughly 19 and 26 points above their own image scores ([Haznitrama, Ardi & Oh, 2026, arXiv:2603.02540](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02540)).
- Documented failure modes on matrix items include fabricating visual features that contradict the input, and preferring complex global hypotheses over simple local rules such as XOR ([Haznitrama, Ardi & Oh, 2026, arXiv:2603.02540](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02540)).
- On MaRs-VQA, a matrix-reasoning benchmark modeled on Raven's and the WISC, GPT-4o reached 33.96% and o1 reached 52.29% against an 81% adult human baseline ([Cao et al., 2024/2025, arXiv:2406.10424](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10424)).
- A two-stage pipeline that converts each image to a natural-language description before reasoning significantly outperforms the same vision-language model applied end-to-end on three ARC-style datasets `T2` ([Your Reasoning Benchmark May Not Test Reasoning, ACL 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.826.pdf)).
- On the CARV compositional-analogy benchmark, the best multimodal model reached 40.4% on compositional tasks, with decomposition rather than perception the dominant failure for closed-source models ([Du, Zou & Cheng, 2026, arXiv:2603.27958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27958)).
- Reformulating abstract graphics into fine-grained perceptual descriptions is sufficient to move models from near-random to accurate on abstract visual reasoning items ([Yan et al., 2025, arXiv:2506.02537](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.02537)).
- An independent commentary argues ARC-AGI-2's headline "100%" human figure denotes only that two of roughly nine participants solved each task, and estimates average human performance on the semi-private set at 53% or lower — a contested reinterpretation, not a peer-reviewed finding ([LessWrong, ARC-AGI-2 human baseline surpassed](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/DX3EmhmwZjTYp9PBf/arc-agi-2-human-baseline-surpassed-updated)).

**DOK 2 — Summary:** Frontier models now beat average humans on abstract grid puzzles delivered as integer text, and did so within roughly fifteen months of scoring under 5%. On the same abstraction problems delivered as *images* — the format of an actual nonverbal reasoning item — the best models sit within a few points of chance and roughly 60 points below humans, and the diagnostic work locates the bottleneck in perception rather than reasoning by about 50 to 1. The two findings are consistent: give a model a clean symbolic description of a figural item and the reasoning is largely there. Note that the near-chance figures come from independent academic benchmarks while the 92.5% comes from ARC Prize's own verified testing, and neither is a developer self-report.

## 2. The second device, the adult beside the child, and whether either can be detected

**Source:** Peer-reviewed meta-analysis and experimental work on unproctored ability testing; person-fit and keystroke detection literature; AI-text-detector fairness research; primary institutional announcements from LSAC and ACCA; vendor threat reporting.

**DOK 1 — Facts:**

- A three-level random-effects meta-analysis of 109 effect sizes from 49 studies (N = 100,434) found scores Δ = 0.20 higher in unproctored than proctored ability assessments, 95% CI [0.10, 0.31] `T2` ([Steger, Schroeders & Gnambs, 2020, *European Journal of Psychological Assessment*](https://doi.org/10.1027/1015-5759/a000494)).
- The same meta-analysis found significantly smaller inflation for measures difficult to research on the internet, and rank-order correlation of only ρ = .58 between proctored and unproctored administrations `T2` ([Steger, Schroeders & Gnambs, 2020](https://doi.org/10.1027/1015-5759/a000494)).
- In a randomized experiment where 178 participants were instructed and incentivized to cheat on an unproctored intelligence battery, self-rated effective cheaters gained d = .59 on the full test `T2` ([Bloemers, Oud & van Dam, 2016, *Personnel Assessment and Decisions*](https://doi.org/10.25035/pad.2016.003)).
- In that experiment cheating significantly raised Vocabulary (d = .54), Numbers (d = .47), Calculations (d = .42) and Cubes (d = .38) but produced no significant gain on Figures, Analogies, or Syllogisms `T2` ([Bloemers, Oud & van Dam, 2016](https://doi.org/10.25035/pad.2016.003)).
- Successful cheaters in that study used "help from others" at roughly twice the rate of unsuccessful ones, 75.9% versus 38.0%, making human assistance the single strongest differentiator between effective and ineffective cheating `T2` ([Bloemers, Oud & van Dam, 2016](https://doi.org/10.25035/pad.2016.003)).
- The same study calculated that at a 10% selection rate, 28 of 43 selected candidates (65%) would come from the instructed-cheating group `T2` ([Bloemers, Oud & van Dam, 2016](https://doi.org/10.25035/pad.2016.003)).
- The canonical unproctored-testing threat model named in the peer-reviewed literature since 2010 is that "a more capable conspirator can sit beside the real applicant and answer test items" `T2` ([Guo & Drasgow, 2010, *International Journal of Selection and Assessment*](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-2389.2010.00518.x)).
- Applying the Guo–Drasgow Z-test to 954 real employment candidates estimated 13.84% suspected cheaters, who spent 5.78 seconds per item longer than expected on the proctored verification test `T2` ([Aguado et al., 2018, *The Spanish Journal of Psychology*](https://doi.org/10.1017/sjp.2018.50)).
- A response-time person-fit method for detecting cheating during computerized adaptive testing was validated only in simulation, with the author noting field testing in real-world scenarios as future work and applicability limited to multiple-choice items `T2` ([Bungaro et al., 2026, *Computational Statistics*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00180-026-01739-1)).
- Seven widely used GPT detectors falsely flagged 61.3% of 91 human-written TOEFL essays as AI-generated while classifying US eighth-grade essays near-perfectly `T2` ([Liang et al., 2023, *Patterns*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100779)).
- In that study all seven detectors unanimously misclassified 19.8% of the human TOEFL essays, and at least one detector flagged 97.8% of them `T2` ([Liang et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100779)).
- The mechanism was text perplexity, which penalizes writers using a more limited range of linguistic expressions — a signal with no analogue in a figural item where the response is a selected option `T2` ([Liang et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100779)).
- Keystroke-dynamics detectors reached F1 above 97% at identifying AI-assisted writing in structured settings while text-only detectors and 44 human evaluators performed near chance — a method that requires typed production and does not transfer to option selection ([Mehta et al., 2025/2026, arXiv:2511.12468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.12468)).
- LSAC announced that starting with the August 2026 LSAT it is moving to in-center testing for almost all US and international test takers, citing security and integrity of the test ([LSAC, Evolving How We Deliver the LSAT](https://www.lsac.org/blog/evolving-how-we-deliver-lsat-increase-test-security-and-test-taker-success)).
- Remote LSAT exceptions now require a candidate to be more than 180 miles or three hours' driving from a center, and those testing remotely must use a smartphone as a second camera ([LSAC, LSAT Remote Testing and Distance Exceptions](https://www.lsac.org/lsat/about/lsat-remote-testing-distance-exceptions)).
- ACCA has restricted remotely invigilated exams to countries with no exam centre provision, with all session-based exams held in centres from March 2026, citing concerns about the ongoing security of remote exams ([ACCA Global, FAQs on the limited availability of remote exams](https://www.accaglobal.com/gb/en/help/faqs-on-the-limited-availability-of-remote-exams.html)).
- ACCA's chief executive was reported by the *Financial Times* as attributing the decision partly to students photographing questions to feed into chatbots, with cheating sophistication outpacing safeguards — a secondhand attribution, not an ACCA published statement ([Scottish Financial News](https://www.scottishfinancialnews.com/articles/acca-abandons-remote-exams-as-ai-cheating-outpaces-safeguards)).
- A vendor press release reports proctored-assessment cheating and fraud attempt rates rising from 16% in 2024 to 35% in 2025, and unproctored assessments showing score increases "more than 4X larger" than proctored ones — vendor-reported, no methodology published, commercial interest in the finding ([CodeSignal press release, Feb 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/codesignal-detection-systems-identify-and-stop-record-high-cheating-attempts-as-assessment-fraud-more-than-doubled-in-2025-302696534.html)).
- Multiple proctoring and exam-security vendors state that lockdown browsers operate only inside the browser and are structurally blind to a second device, a mobile hotspot, or a locally hosted model — vendor claims from firms selling the remedy, and to date not independently replicated ([Proctortrack](https://proctortrack.com/second-screen-cheating-online-exams-browser-lockdown-limitations/); [Forasoft](https://www.forasoft.com/blog/article/online-proctoring-anti-cheating-2026); [Aiseptor](https://aiseptor.com/blog/why-browser-proctoring-fails-ai-cheating)).
- Surveys across more than 40 US high schools found 60–70% of students reporting at least one cheating behavior in the previous month, a rate unchanged or slightly decreased after ChatGPT's release ([Stanford Graduate School of Education, Lee & Pope](https://ed.stanford.edu/news/what-do-ai-chatbots-really-mean-students-and-cheating)).

**DOK 2 — Summary:** Unproctored administration inflates ability scores by about a fifth of a standard deviation on average and reshuffles rank order substantially, and the item types most resistant to 2016-era cheating were precisely the figural and analogical ones — because the tools available then, including a human helper, could not solve them. Detection of model assistance from response data alone is method-dependent: text detectors are unreliable and demonstrably biased against non-native writers, keystroke methods work well but need typed production a figural item does not generate, and the response-time person-fit approaches that *would* apply to option selection remain simulation-validated rather than field-validated. Two major testing bodies withdrew remote delivery in 2026 rather than attempt to close the gap technically.

## 3. Coaching, the parent in the room, and whether remote administration is equivalent at all

**Source:** Peer-reviewed meta-analyses of coaching and retest effects on cognitive ability tests; the g-loading debate over whether score gains are real ability; third-party-observer studies in pediatric neuropsychology; teleassessment equivalence studies and systematic reviews; independent municipal analysis of test-based gifted admissions.

**DOK 1 — Facts:**

- A meta-analysis of 107 samples and 134,436 participants found an adjusted overall retest effect of d = .26 on cognitive ability tests, larger when practice was accompanied by coaching and when identical forms were reused `T2` ([Hausknecht et al., 2007, *Journal of Applied Psychology*](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.92.2.373)).
- A later meta-analysis of 174 samples from 122 studies (N = 153,185) confirmed significant retest effects and found no further score gains after the third administration `T2` ([Scharfen, Peters & Holling, 2018, *Intelligence*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intell.2018.01.003)).
- A 2025 latent-growth study (N = 203) reported linear improvement of about 10.45 IQ points per assessment across three sessions, roughly two-thirds of a standard deviation `T2` ([Investigating retest effects in cognitive ability tests: An operation-specific approach, *Intelligence*, 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160289625000911)).
- A meta-analysis of 64 test-retest studies using IQ batteries (N = 26,990) reported a correlation of −1.00 between subtest g-loadings and score gains, interpreted as score gains containing no g — an extreme value obtained via the method of correlated vectors and worth treating as contested `T2` ([te Nijenhuis, van Vianen & van der Flier, 2007, *Intelligence*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intell.2006.07.006)).
- The same lead author's earlier study found that practice data did *not* support the hypothesis that more g-loaded tests resist preparation, while coaching data did — the two findings sit in tension within one research programme `T2` ([te Nijenhuis, Voskuijl & Schijve, 2001, *International Journal of Selection and Assessment*](https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-2389.00182)).
- In a study of 552 children aged 10–11 taking seven parallel forms of an 11-plus selection test, familiarisation and practice produced no significant change in means, three hours of coaching produced a statistically significant shift in means while individuals maintained their rank order, and sustained coaching over nine months produced substantial gains `T2` ([Bunting & Mooney, 2001, *Educational Psychology*](https://doi.org/10.1080/01443410120065450)).
- New York City's Independent Budget Office found that under test-based gifted admissions fewer than one quarter of test-takers were eligible, and Black and Hispanic students were less than half as likely as White students to score high enough for both citywide and district programs ([NYC IBO, 2022](https://ibo.nyc.ny.us/iboreports/inequity-in-access-an-examination-of-the-test-based-admissions-system-to-the-citys-gifted-talented-program-schools-brief-2022.html)).
- A commercial preparation provider advertises that 93% of its students qualified for NYC gifted programs and over 45% scored at the 99th percentile — a vendor self-report with no control group, no independent audit, and obvious selection bias in who enrolls ([FasTracKids NYC](https://ftkny.com/test-preparation/nyc-gifted-talented-test-prep/)).
- In a study of 53 children aged 6–8 given the Test of Nonverbal Intelligence with and without a parent observing, parental observation status significantly interacted with observation order to affect performance, with the effect attributable mainly to a steeper improvement slope when the parent observed first `T2` ([Yantz et al., 2009, *The Clinical Neuropsychologist*](https://doi.org/10.1080/13854040801894722)).
- With a significant-other observer present, 30 test-takers showed performance deficits on attention, speed of information processing, and verbal fluency, and none on cognitive flexibility or motor speed `T2` ([Kehrer et al., 2000, *The Clinical Neuropsychologist*](https://doi.org/10.1076/1385-4046%28200002%2914:1;1-8;ft067)).
- A test publisher's remote-administration guidance instructs that a non-professional in-home facilitator must remain neutral, not provide additional encouragement, and not answer on behalf of the child, and warns that children may look to the facilitator for guidance in ways that will influence performance — publisher guidance, not an empirical finding ([Pearson, Telepractice and the NEPSY-II](https://www.pearsonassessments.com/content/dam/school/global/clinical/us/assets/telepractice/guidance-documents/telepractice-and-the-nepsy-ii.pdf)).
- In 893 clinically referred children and adolescents aged 4–17, only two of seven remotely versus in-person administered subtests differed significantly — WISC-V Visual Puzzles (d = .33) and KTEA-3 Math Concepts (d = .18) — with WISC-V Matrix Reasoning showing no significant difference `T2` ([Hamner et al., 2021, *Journal of the International Neuropsychological Society*](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1355617721001053)).
- In 476 youths aged 6–17 assessed at home by telehealth versus in person, nine of twelve neuropsychological measures showed no differences and three verbal tests were about one scaled score point lower in person `T2` ([Manning et al., 2024, *The Clinical Neuropsychologist*](https://doi.org/10.1080/13854046.2024.2403734)).
- A systematic review of 23 studies of cognitive tele-assessment in children reported similarity in performance scores against in-person assessment while stressing that methodological precautions are required for technical and procedural challenges `T2` ([Ruffini et al., 2021, *Child Neuropsychology*](https://doi.org/10.1080/09297049.2021.2005011)).
- A separate systematic review of pediatric tele-neuropsychology concluded the approach is feasible and acceptable but that performance validity was not tested in the included studies, samples were small and mostly white, and there is a critical need for tests specifically normed for remote administration `T2` ([Harder et al., 2023, PMC10231293](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10231293/)).
- In 19 youths with neurodevelopmental disorders given the WJ-IV in person versus in-home remotely, results were generally equivalent except that Fluid Reasoning and Auditory Processing scores differed significantly across modalities — a very small sample and a single study `T2` ([Moreau et al., 2023, *Canadian Journal of School Psychology*](https://doi.org/10.1177/08295735231199858)).

**DOK 2 — Summary:** Coaching and repeated testing reliably raise cognitive-test scores — roughly a quarter of a standard deviation on average, and substantially more with sustained preparation of the kind sold for children's selection tests — but the literature disputes whether those gains are ability or artefact, and one 11-plus study found short coaching moved the mean while leaving rank order intact. Adult presence during children's testing has a measurable but modality-specific and directionally inconsistent effect, and publishers already treat an in-home non-professional facilitator as a validity risk requiring explicit instruction not to help. Remote administration of children's cognitive tests looks broadly equivalent to in-person in clinical samples, with two caveats that bear directly on nonverbal reasoning: the one study reporting a fluid-reasoning discrepancy had N = 19, and every equivalence study reviewed was clinician-supervised and low-stakes rather than unsupervised and high-stakes.

## Candidate tensions

- ARC Prize's verified 92.5% on ARC-AGI-2 versus ~28% near-chance performance on image-delivered abstract puzzles: the same models look superhuman or subhuman at abstraction depending entirely on whether the item arrives as text or as a picture.
- Perception-bound failure (55.4% of errors, VRIQ) versus decomposition-bound failure (CARV's finding for closed-source models): the benchmarks disagree about *where* figural reasoning breaks, which changes whether transcription by a human helper would fix it.
- ARC Prize's 66% average human baseline for ARC-AGI-2 versus an independent reinterpretation putting it at 53% or lower: how much of the "models beat humans" claim survives depends on an unresolved measurement dispute.
- Steger et al.'s Δ = 0.20 inflation in unproctored ability testing versus Nye et al.'s two-step study finding no evidence of cheating and *higher* proctored scores: whether unproctored delivery is compromised is not settled empirically.
- Bloemers et al. found figural and analogical items immune to cheating with human help, while the perception-bottleneck literature implies a human who describes the item to a model supplies exactly the missing capability: the cheat-resistance of figural items may be a property of the era's tools rather than of the item type.
- ACCA and LSAC withdrawing remote delivery in 2026 on integrity grounds versus Stanford survey data showing high-school cheating rates unchanged after ChatGPT: institutional risk perception and measured student behaviour are moving in opposite directions.
- te Nijenhuis et al. (2007) reporting no g in score gains versus te Nijenhuis et al. (2001) finding practice data unsupportive of that hypothesis: whether coaching produces real ability change is contested within the same research programme.
- Bunting & Mooney's finding that three hours of coaching shifted means while preserving rank order versus the assumption that coaching corrupts selection: for a rank-based selection decision, mean shift and rank shift are different harms.
- Teleassessment equivalence findings in clinician-supervised clinical samples versus Moreau et al.'s significant fluid-reasoning discrepancy and the systematic-review finding that performance validity was never tested: the equivalence evidence may not transfer to unsupervised high-stakes nonverbal reasoning.
- Coaching as the dominant inflation channel (legal, d ≈ .26 to two-thirds SD, unequally distributed by income) versus AI assistance as the dominant channel (illegal, currently perception-limited on figural items): the two hypotheses imply opposite priorities for test design.
- Adult presence as a validity threat (Yantz, Kehrer, publisher facilitator warnings) versus adult presence as a practical necessity for young children testing at home: for a young child the coach and the required helper are the same person.
