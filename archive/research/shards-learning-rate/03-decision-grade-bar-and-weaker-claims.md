# Decision-Grade Bar, and the Ladder of Weaker Claims

**General idea.** The published testing standards and the psychometric literature treat "this
score correlates with something" and "this score may help decide a child's placement" as two
different claims carrying two different evidentiary burdens. The second requires classification
evidence — decision consistency and decision accuracy — that cannot be read off a reliability
coefficient, plus error reported *at the cut*, subgroup fairness evidence at the cut, and a
demonstrated increment over information already in hand. The standards impose these obligations on
a measure that merely *contributes to* a decision as well: growth and difference scores used for
individual students must carry their own validity, reliability, and fairness evidence, and nothing
in the standards exempts a non-decisive input. Where an absolute individual rate cannot meet that
bar, the literature supplies a descending ladder of weaker uses — rank, coarse band, tie-breaker,
follow-up flag, research-only signal — but each rung has its own preconditions, and two of them are
hostile to intuition: partial pooling can preserve a child's rank while collapsing the spread that
made ranking meaningful, and the tie-breaker rung concentrates a weak measure exactly where its
error rate is highest.

## 1. What a score must clear to carry a consequential decision — and what a contributing signal must clear as well

**Source:** AERA, APA & NCME, *Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing* (2014) `T2`;
Kane (2013), *Journal of Educational Measurement* `T2`; Livingston & Lewis (1995), *Journal of
Educational Measurement* `T2`; Hunsley & Meyer (2003), *Psychological Assessment* `T2`.

**DOK 1 — Facts:**

- It is the proposed score interpretations and uses that are validated, not the test or the test
  scores (Kane, 2013).
- Validating the score interpretation on which a score use is based does not validate the score use
  (Kane, 2013).
- More-ambitious claims require more support than less-ambitious claims (Kane, 2013).
- Evaluating a score use requires evaluating the consequences of that use, and negative
  consequences can render a score use unacceptable (Kane, 2013).
- Rejecting a score use does not necessarily invalidate the prior, underlying score interpretation
  (Kane, 2013).
- When a test or combination of measures is used to make classification decisions, estimates should
  be provided of the percentage of test takers who would be classified the same way on two
  replications of the procedure (AERA, APA & NCME, 2014, Standard 2.16).
- The standard error of measurement cannot be translated into an expected percentage of consistent
  or accurate decisions without strong assumptions about the distributions of measurement errors and
  true scores (AERA, APA & NCME, 2014, Standard 2.16 comment).
- Decision accuracy and decision consistency are separately estimated quantities, and the published
  method for estimating them applies to composite scores produced by any scoring method, including a
  weighted composite (Livingston & Lewis, 1995).
- Where cut scores are specified for selection or classification, standard errors of measurement
  should be reported in the vicinity of each cut score (AERA, APA & NCME, 2014, Standard 2.14).
- When there is credible evidence that conditional standard errors of measurement will differ
  substantially across subgroups, the extent and impact of those differences should be investigated
  and reported (AERA, APA & NCME, 2014, Standard 2.15).
- A predictor may show no significant differential item functioning yet still show group differences
  in predictive relations, so measurement bias and predictive bias must be investigated
  independently (AERA, APA & NCME, 2014, ch. 3).
- Correlation coefficients provide inadequate evidence for or against a differential-prediction
  hypothesis when groups differ in means and variances on the test and the criterion (AERA, APA &
  NCME, 2014, Standard 3.7).
- When test scores are used in conjunction with other variables to predict an outcome, analyses
  should include those additional variables, and it is often informative to estimate the increment
  in predictive accuracy when each variable is introduced in addition to all other available
  variables (AERA, APA & NCME, 2014, Standard 1.19).
- There has been insufficient effort in most areas of applied psychology to evaluate incremental
  validity at all (Hunsley & Meyer, 2003).
- Empirically derived weights for combining predictors can capitalize on chance factors in a given
  sample, so multi-predictor analyses should be verified by cross-validation or equivalent analysis
  (AERA, APA & NCME, 2014, Standard 1.19 comment).
- A decision or characterization that will have major impact on a student should take into
  consideration not just scores from a single test but other relevant information, and those
  additional data sources should themselves demonstrate information relevant to the intended
  construct (AERA, APA & NCME, 2014, Standard 12.10).
- When difference or growth scores are used for individual students, such scores should be clearly
  defined, and evidence of their validity, reliability/precision, **and fairness** should be
  reported (AERA, APA & NCME, 2014, Standard 12.11).
- Where complex statistical models are used to predict scores for individual students, the method
  for constructing the models should be made explicit and justified, with supporting technical and
  interpretive information provided to score users (AERA, APA & NCME, 2014, Standard 12.11).
- In testing individuals for diagnostic or special-program placement purposes, test scores should
  not be the sole indicators of an individual's functioning or competence (AERA, APA & NCME, 2014,
  Standard 3.18).
- Applying group-level (nomothetic) incremental-validity findings to an individual case is itself an
  identified methodological challenge, not a solved step (Hunsley & Meyer, 2003).

**DOK 2 — Summary:** The bar is not a reliability number; it is a use-specific argument that must
carry classification evidence the reliability coefficient cannot supply. A measure entering a
consequential decision therefore owes decision consistency and decision accuracy estimates, error
reported at the cut rather than on average, subgroup fairness evidence treated as separate from
measurement bias, and a demonstrated increment over what is already measured. Critically, the
standards attach validity, reliability, and fairness obligations to individual growth and difference
scores directly (Standard 12.11) and require that supplementary information used in a major-impact
decision itself be construct-relevant (Standard 12.10) — so a "contributing but not decisive" signal
inherits the requirements rather than escaping them.

## 2. Rank and band: the spread requirement, and what makes a reference distribution defensible

**Source:** Willett (1989), *Educational and Psychological Measurement* `T2`; AERA, APA & NCME
(2014) `T2`; Kanaya, Scullin & Ceci (2003), *American Psychologist* `T2`; Kane & Staiger (2002),
*Journal of Economic Perspectives* `T2`.

**DOK 1 — Facts:**

- The reliability of measured individual change depends on three things: the magnitude of
  inter-individual heterogeneity in true growth, the size of the measurement-error variance, and the
  number of waves of data collected (Willett, 1989).
- Because inter-individual heterogeneity in true growth is one of its inputs, growth-rate
  reliability confounds group heterogeneity in growth rate with measurement precision and is not an
  index of the instrument's reliability (Willett, 1989).
- Dramatic increases in the reliability of change measurement can be achieved by collecting
  relatively few additional waves beyond a two-wave pre-test/post-test design (Willett, 1989).
- Reports of norming studies should include precise specification of the population sampled,
  sampling procedures and participation rates, any weighting of the sample, the dates of testing,
  and descriptive statistics, and technical documentation should indicate the precision of the norms
  themselves (AERA, APA & NCME, 2014, Standard 5.9).
- The information provided about norms should be sufficient to let users judge whether the norms are
  appropriate for interpreting the scores of local examinees (AERA, APA & NCME, 2014, Standard 5.9
  comment).
- A test publisher is responsible for renorming with sufficient frequency to permit continued
  accurate and appropriate score interpretation, and users must either have up-to-date norms or
  evidence that older norms remain appropriate (AERA, APA & NCME, 2014, Standard 5.11).
- In longitudinal records from nine U.S. sites, students in the borderline and mild
  mental-retardation range lost an average of 5.6 IQ points when retested on a renormed test and
  were more likely to be classified as mentally retarded than peers retested on the same test
  (Kanaya, Scullin & Ceci, 2003).
- Children of the same cognitive ability could be classified differently depending on which test
  norms were in use in the year they were tested (Kanaya, Scullin & Ceci, 2003).
- Because the median U.S. elementary school contained only about 69 children per grade level,
  school-level mean test-score measures are quite imprecise, and rewards or sanctions keyed to
  either extreme of the distribution primarily affect small units (Kane & Staiger, 2002).
- When an individual student's scores from different tests are compared, the reliability and
  standard error of the difference score are affected by the relationship between the constructs
  measured as well as by each test's standard error, and overlapping constructs can render
  difference-score reliability low (AERA, APA & NCME, 2014, Standard 12.12).

**DOK 2 — Summary:** Ranking is not a free downgrade from point estimation: the same quantity that
makes an individual growth rate estimable — genuine between-child heterogeneity in true growth —
is what a rank needs in order to carry information, and Willett's decomposition makes that
dependence explicit alongside error variance and occasion count. A coarse band additionally needs a
reference distribution whose sampled population, participation, weighting, testing dates, and
precision are documented and current; the Flynn-effect evidence shows what happens when that
condition lapses, with a 5.6-point average shift changing who fell on which side of a
classification line. Small-unit noise compounds at the tails, which is where band and rank cutoffs
usually sit.

## 3. Shrinkage against rank, and the tie-breaker applied at the margin

**Source:** Shen & Louis (1998), *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society Series B* `T2`;
Lockwood, Louis & McCaffrey (2002), *Journal of Educational and Behavioral Statistics* `T2`;
Lathrop (1986), *The Journal of Educational Research* `T2`; Jølstad & Gustavsson (2023), *Journal
of Medical Ethics* `T2`.

**DOK 1 — Facts:**

- In the two-stage compound sampling model the posterior means are optimal under squared-error loss
  but can perform poorly in estimating the histogram of the parameters or in ranking them (Shen &
  Louis, 1998).
- No single set of estimates can simultaneously optimize unit-specific estimation, estimation of the
  parameter histogram, and estimation of the ranks (Shen & Louis, 1998).
- Ranks derived from posterior means are themselves shrunk toward the mid-rank of the set and are
  generally not integers (Shen & Louis, 1998).
- An estimator constructed to optimize the empirical distribution function and the ranks
  outperformed both posterior means and constrained Bayes estimates on those goals, at the cost of
  some squared-error optimality on individual estimates (Shen & Louis, 1998).
- The mean squared error of percentile (rank) estimates depends on the signal-to-noise ratio — the
  ratio of the unit-level variance component to the variance of the unit-specific estimator — and
  only moderately on the number of units being ranked (Lockwood, Louis & McCaffrey, 2002).
- Mean squared error of percentile estimates is large for commonly encountered variance ratios, and
  an unrealistically large ratio is required for ideal ranking performance (Lockwood, Louis &
  McCaffrey, 2002).
- Percentile-specific mean squared error interacts with the variance ratio especially at extreme
  percentiles, degrading decision rules that key on identifying the extremes (Lockwood, Louis &
  McCaffrey, 2002).
- Even optimal percentile estimators must be assessed for whether they perform well enough to be
  used in evaluative decisions at all (Lockwood, Louis & McCaffrey, 2002).
- For most examinees the unreliability of a well-designed assessment is not sufficient to cause
  misclassification, but for the significant portion of examinees with scores near the cutting score
  the risk of misclassification is substantial even for instruments with relatively high retest
  reliability (Lathrop, 1986).
- A tie-breaker treated as a secondary consideration has, by construction, either infinitesimal
  value or value lexically subordinate to the primary criteria (Jølstad & Gustavsson, 2023).
- The "second tie-breaker problem" asks whether a factor that is significant only when there are no
  relevant differences among options on primary criteria can plausibly be significant enough to
  overcome the reasons for preferring a lottery (Jølstad & Gustavsson, 2023).
- The instances where a tie-breaker seems necessary are precisely the situations where there are
  strong reasons for preferring a lottery, and factors judged genuinely valuable should be included
  among the primary considerations rather than held back as tie-breakers (Jølstad & Gustavsson,
  2023).
- Targeted searching located this argument in the medical-ethics priority-setting literature and did
  not locate an equivalent developed treatment in the educational-measurement literature on
  multiple-criteria identification, which reports combination-rule effects on classification without
  addressing whether marginal-only application of a weak measure is defensible (Jølstad &
  Gustavsson, 2023; Lathrop, 1986).

**DOK 2 — Summary:** Partial pooling does not protect a ranking use — it distorts it in a specific
direction, because posterior means shrink and the ranks built from them shrink toward the middle,
and no estimator optimizes individual estimates, the spread, and the ordering at once. Rank
accuracy is governed by a signal-to-noise ratio that is unfavourable at realistic values and worst
at the extremes, which are exactly the percentiles selection rules read. On the tie-breaker rung,
the psychometric literature establishes that misclassification risk concentrates near the cut even
for reliable instruments, and the normative argument that a marginal-only tie-breaker is
self-undermining and weaker than a lottery is developed in medical-ethics priority setting rather
than in educational measurement — an absence worth recording, since it means the tie-breaker rung
has no measurement-side defence to lean on.

## 4. Follow-up flag, research-only signal, and what a weak input does inside a multi-criteria rule

**Source:** Glover & Albers (2007), *Journal of School Psychology* `T2`; McBee, Peters & Waterman
(2014), *Gifted Child Quarterly* `T2`; AERA, APA & NCME (2014) `T2`.

**DOK 1 — Facts:**

- Universal screening is framed as a prerequisite to providing early prevention and intervention
  services rather than as a placement determination (Glover & Albers, 2007).
- Criteria for making informed decisions about which screening tools are appropriate were not
  readily available to those working in educational settings, and a screener must be evaluated for
  appropriateness to the specific context and purpose, technical adequacy, and usability (Glover &
  Albers, 2007).
- Choice of combination rule — conjunctive ("and"), disjunctive/complementary ("or"), or
  compensatory ("mean") — changes the size of the identified population, the ability heterogeneity
  of the identified students, and the psychometric performance of a multiple-criteria identification
  system (McBee, Peters & Waterman, 2014).
- That combination-rule analysis was conducted by statistical simulation of an operational,
  mandated, multiple-criteria state identification system (McBee, Peters & Waterman, 2014).
- It may be neither advisable nor lawful to admit students automatically on a single measured
  threshold without considering additional relevant information about their performance (AERA, APA &
  NCME, 2014, Standard 12.10 comment).
- Where tests are selected for evaluation or accountability settings, the intended uses and the
  consequences they are expected to promote should be clearly described, together with cautions
  against inappropriate uses (AERA, APA & NCME, 2014, Standard 13.7).
- Claims that a testing use will improve outcomes should be made explicit when the system is adopted
  and evidence supporting their validity should be provided when available (AERA, APA & NCME, 2014,
  Standard 13.7 comment).
- Multiple sources of information should be used for diagnostic and placement purposes, alternative
  explanations for test performance considered, and the professional judgement of someone familiar
  with the test brought to bear (AERA, APA & NCME, 2014, Standard 3.18).

**DOK 1 note on rung-by-rung requirements:** a rank use requires demonstrated between-child true
variance exceeding error plus an assessed rank signal-to-noise ratio (Willett, 1989; Lockwood,
Louis & McCaffrey, 2002); a band use additionally requires a documented, current, population-matched
reference distribution and a reported classification error rate at each boundary (AERA, APA & NCME,
2014, Standards 5.9, 5.11, 2.14, 2.16); a tie-breaker use requires error evidence specifically at
the margin, not on average (Lathrop, 1986); a follow-up flag requires context-specific technical
adequacy for the screening purpose and a route to further assessment (Glover & Albers, 2007); and a
research-only signal requires an explicitly stated use boundary with cautions against inappropriate
use (AERA, APA & NCME, 2014, Standard 13.7).

**DOK 2 — Summary:** The two lowest rungs are the only ones whose evidentiary preconditions are
already articulated as ordinary practice: a screening flag is judged on fit to purpose, technical
adequacy, and usability rather than on fitness to place a child, and a research-only signal is
governed by an explicit statement of intended use with cautions attached. The rung immediately above
them is where the ladder metaphor leaks, because a measure described as one input among several is
still combined by some rule, and rule choice alone changes who is identified and how psychometrically
sound the system is. Kane's separation of interpretation from use is what makes the bottom of the
ladder coherent: a growth-rate interpretation can remain defensible for study while the gating use
built on it is rejected.

## Candidate tensions

- Multiple-measure requirements are invoked to justify adding a weak signal, while the same
  standards require each contributing signal to carry its own validity, reliability, and fairness
  evidence.
- Ranking is treated as a modest retreat from absolute measurement, yet rank accuracy is governed by
  a signal-to-noise ratio described as unfavourable at realistic values.
- Partial pooling is adopted to stabilise individual estimates, but the ranks it yields are shrunk
  toward the middle, which is the opposite of what a ranking use needs.
- Adding waves raises change reliability, while every added occasion lengthens the observation
  window during which the child's decision is pending.
- Growth-rate reliability rises with between-child heterogeneity, so the same statistic looks better
  precisely in cohorts that are more unequal at intake.
- Band boundaries require a current, population-matched reference distribution, while any renorming
  event itself shifts who falls on which side.
- Small-unit noise is worst at the distribution's extremes, which is where selection cutoffs are
  normally placed.
- The tie-breaker rung is defended as a low-stakes hedge and simultaneously applies a weak measure
  to the examinees whose misclassification risk is highest.
- The normative case against marginal-only tie-breakers is developed in medical-ethics priority
  setting, leaving educational measurement without a corresponding treatment.
- A lottery is impartial at a genuine tie but forfeits any information a weak measure might still
  carry.
- Incremental validity is required before adding a predictor, while the literature reports that
  incremental validity is seldom evaluated at all.
- Differential item functioning and differential prediction must be investigated separately, so
  clearing one provides no assurance about the other.
- Research-only logging is defended as consequence-free, yet a logged signal becomes available to
  future decision rules.
- Consequences can invalidate a score use even where the underlying score interpretation stands.
