## Key findings (decision-relevant)

### A. Current admissions criteria Crystal applies (Part 2)
Derived from what on-campus GT School students follow; "pretty good success" so far. Currently
a **human decision** (see F). Multiple qualifying paths:

1. **MAP screeners > 95th percentile ("hustler kids").** Even with only ~90th on CogAT, two
   MAP screeners over the 95th percentile → admit; they already top their class and will keep
   up with (or exceed) Timeback rigor.
2. **Blended aggregate > 90th percentile.** E.g., MAP ~92/93 + CogAT ~89 → combined over the
   90th → admit.
3. **Exceptional-ability waiver.** 99th-percentile composite across the board → admit.

### B. The hard reading/literacy gate (Part 2) — most decision-relevant for the in-house test
- **Hard reading cutoff for EVERY student, regardless of CogAT: at least the 85th percentile
  fall MAP Reading score for the grade they are entering** (a basic RIT score per grade).
- **Exception (essentially ELL only):** a 99th-percentile (exceptional) cognitive student whose
  reading is only ~70th–80th percentile is *not* rejected — cognitive ability is expected to let
  them catch up, "usually only for English-as-a-second-language learners." (Timeback isn't
  offered in other languages yet — see D.)
- Rationale: if a child cannot read/keep up, Timeback (self-paced, reading-based, fast, little
  re-teaching) will not serve them.

### C. Timeback-fit is the real target, not generic "giftedness" (Part 1)
- The test should measure **whether a student would excel on the Timeback platform**, not just a
  generic gifted metric. Some non-"gifted" kids thrive on Timeback (they can be 4–5 grade levels
  ahead) and "mimic giftedness"; some genuinely gifted kids will *not* be served by it.
- "You have to be a certain kind of child." Timeback = self-paced, fast, no hand-holding, little
  content re-review. Poor fit examples she gave: nonverbal autistic students; students needing
  "an outrageous amount of extra time" on specific content; a domain-only prodigy (e.g., a
  musician) who doesn't enjoy academics; 2e students in the bottom ~10th percentile of learners
  (GT Anywhere marketed taking learning-disabled students and is now struggling with fit).
- "Giftedness can come with a lack of something academically."

### D. Language dependency (Part 1)
- "If you're incredibly gifted and you can't speak English, we don't offer it yet in other
  languages — Timeback won't serve you." (Ties to the ELL nuance in B.)

### E. Speed / learning-science signals worth testing (Part 1)
- In Timeback's optimal zone a student gets ~80–90% right **so long as they read the content**;
  "speed" ≈ **not needing concepts re-introduced** — absorbing/adapting to new info on the 1st–2nd
  exposure and jumping ahead. GT kids burn through multiple grade levels per year.
- Named signals: **pattern recognition** and **spatial recognition** — drawing connections from
  new info based on prior knowledge (not memorization; "recognizing relationships").
- "System-hacking": kids who succeed on Alpha Read/Alpha Write figure out the pattern the system
  wants quickly (she cited a near-nonreader reaching ~grade-4 language via pattern use + text-to-
  speech). → supports testing pattern/spatial reasoning and fast rule-induction.

### F. Automation requirement (Parts 1–2)
- Today Crystal is effectively the **sole reviewer** (+ onboarding help; lead guides weigh in
  after shadow days on behavioral fit). She *likes* human review now (can rescue near-miss kids,
  e.g., ~94th, or a kid who tested in a noisy room of 12).
- **But for scale it must be automated:** "thousands and thousands of children" on the virtual
  side; the in-house test's algorithm "needs to be pretty darn accurate" to auto-cut ("try again
  in six months / a year"). Manual per-application review is "impractical" at scale.
- Manual behavioral review at the **shadow-day** stage will always remain to some degree.

### G. CogAT accuracy & the Mindprint cautionary tale (Part 1)
- Few **false positives** on CogAT; some **false negatives**, "especially with the little ones."
- Kids at 97th–99th CogAT but behind in MAP math often "fly through four grade levels in six
  months" → she reads CogAT as accurate ("they'll catch up").
- CogAT is "the only truly fairly accurate measurement" she's seen so far, despite gripes about
  length/administration.
- **Mindprint caution:** in 2024/early 2025 some ~80th-percentile-CogAT kids were admitted on
  Mindprint "gifted" results *without* CogAT; one family is "still really struggling" a year in →
  why she is "heavy on both" giftedness *and* acceleration-fit.

### H. Validation data is available (Part 1)
- She can **dummy-test the ~46 on-campus GT School students in one afternoon** (they enjoy
  beta-testing), or a sample — everyone has CogAT + MAP data → a dataset to compare the new test
  vs CogAT vs MAP. Keep the test **≤ ~45 min**.
- She can pull historical **CogAT distributions from Riverside** (including pre-tenure), and
  "scrape" cohort data "by tomorrow." Median/range CogAT data offered.

### I. Volume, scale & expansion (Part 1)
- Current size: **physical campus ~40–46 students; GT Anywhere (virtual) ~300**, K–8.
- "MIT-ready by 8th grade" / Joe's ~**100,000-kid** goal → needs a scalable automated screener.
- Expansion: can open campuses fast given locations + interest, but **won't open below ~20–30
  starting students** (traction/vibe); a physical campus holds up to ~100. Next-year goal: 3–4

### J. Process pain & the "one GT Universe portal" (Part 1)
- GT Anywhere onboarding is "a tangled mess" (workflows routed to 3 people, some no longer at the

- She has an **admissions/reviewer dashboard** built on the back end (via her husband's company),
  aggregating candidates/scores/stats; live on a dummy page, not publicly deployed. **Goal:
  interns integrate the new test into that environment** ("100% that is the goal"); she'll share
  the tech stack.

### K. Two-track proposal discussed (interns → Crystal, Part 1)
- Interns described Track A (auto-admit on strong scores, ~current) + Track B for the
  regression-discontinuity group (~50% of gifted cohort missed by current testing) using
  artifacts/portfolio (compositions, chess tournaments, test scores, portfolio pieces, a
  self-intro video).
- Crystal: portfolio/artifacts are worth including, **but every path must still confirm the
  student will be served by Timeback** (see C).
